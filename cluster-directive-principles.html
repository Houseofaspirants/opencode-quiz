What Directive Principles of State Policy (DPSP) covers

Why the lane is split this way and the order to work through it.

The Directive Principles sit in Part IV of the Constitution and set out the aims the state should pursue while governing. They are the classic near-miss topic in Indian Polity: candidates confuse them with Fundamental Rights, which are enforceable in court, while the Directive Principles are not. That single distinction generates a large share of the questions in this lane, so it should be settled before anything else. The principles are conventionally read in three groups - socialist, Gandhian and liberal-intellectual - and examiners like to ask which category a given provision belongs to. The lanes below work through that grouping first and then through the relationship between the two sets of provisions, which is where the harder questions are drawn from.
