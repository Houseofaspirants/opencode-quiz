Basic Concepts of Economy - Part 2: all 18 questions

Read the question, pick an option, then open the answer.

  1. Which market type has only one seller controlling the entire market?

    1. Perfect competition
    2. Monopolistic competition
    3. Monopoly
    4. Oligopoly
    Show answer

    Answer: (C) Monopoly

    Explanation: In monopoly, one seller controls the entire market and sets the price. Perfect and monopolistic competition have many sellers, whereas oligopoly has a few dominant firms. This distinction is useful for matching concepts accurately in competitive examinations.

  2. Which market type—seller-number pair is correctly matched?

    1. Oligopoly — very many sellers
    2. Monopolistic competition — one seller
    3. Perfect competition — very many sellers
    4. Monopoly — a few sellers
    Show answer

    Answer: (C) Perfect competition — very many sellers

    Explanation: Perfect competition has very many sellers according to the notes. Monopolistic competition also has many sellers, oligopoly has a few, and monopoly has one seller. This distinction is useful for matching concepts accurately in competitive examinations.

  3. Which of the following is NOT correct?

    1. Perfect competition is hypothetical
    2. Monopolistic competition has similar but slightly differentiated products
    3. Monopoly has no seller able to influence price
    4. Oligopoly firms may compete or collaborate
    Show answer

    Answer: (C) Monopoly has no seller able to influence price

    Explanation: The incorrect statement is that monopoly has no price-influencing seller; its sole seller sets the price. The other three statements correctly describe perfect competition, monopolistic competition and oligopoly. This distinction is useful for matching concepts accurately in competitive examinations.

  4. Match GDP expenditure sectors with their expenditure component: 1. Household sector 2. Business/production sector 3. Government sector 4. Foreign/external sector (a) Net exports (b) Government purchases (c) Consumption expenditure (d) Investment expenditure Select the correct code.

    1. 1-b, 2-a, 3-d, 4-c
    2. 1-d, 2-c, 3-a, 4-b
    3. 1-c, 2-d, 3-b, 4-a
    4. 1-a, 2-b, 3-c, 4-d
    Show answer

    Answer: (C) 1-c, 2-d, 3-b, 4-a

    Explanation: The correct mapping is household-consumption, business-investment, government purchases and external-net exports. The other codes interchange the four distinct expenditure roles stated in the notes. This distinction is useful for matching concepts accurately in competitive examinations.

  5. The household sector creates market demand mainly by:

    1. Setting the price of every product
    2. Buying final goods and services from the market
    3. Issuing currency to banks
    4. Managing all imports
    Show answer

    Answer: (B) Buying final goods and services from the market

    Explanation: Households create market demand by purchasing final goods and services. Price setting is not a household role, currency issuance is not stated as one, and imports are not managed by households. This distinction is useful for matching concepts accurately in competitive examinations.

  6. Which factor services are supplied by households to businesses according to the notes?

    1. Land, labour and capital
    2. Taxes, imports and exports
    3. Goods, prices and profits
    4. Loans, insurance and transport
    Show answer

    Answer: (A) Land, labour and capital

    Explanation: Households provide businesses with factor services of land, labour and capital. Taxes and imports are payments or transactions, while the remaining sets are not the factor services listed in the notes. This distinction is useful for matching concepts accurately in competitive examinations.

  7. Assertion (A): Household savings are a main source of capital formation in India. Reason (R): Money left after consumption may be saved in banks or financial organisations.

    1. Both A and R are true and R is the correct explanation of A
    2. Both A and R are true but R is not the correct explanation of A
    3. A is true but R is false
    4. A is false but R is true
    Show answer

    Answer: (A) Both A and R are true and R is the correct explanation of A

    Explanation: Both statements are true, and saving the income left after consumption explains why households contribute to capital formation. The other alternatives incorrectly reject the truth or the explanatory connection between the two statements. This distinction is useful for matching concepts accurately in competitive examinations.

  8. The production sector makes payments for: 1. Household factor services 2. Government taxes 3. Imports of materials Which of the statements given above are correct?

    1. 1 only
    2. 1 and 2 only
    3. 2 and 3 only
    4. 1, 2 and 3
    Show answer

    Answer: (D) 1, 2 and 3

    Explanation: The production sector pays for household factor services, government taxes and imported materials. Each of the three listed payments is expressly mentioned in the notes, so no narrower combination is correct. This distinction is useful for matching concepts accurately in competitive examinations.

  9. The stages through which raw materials pass to become a finished product are called the:

    1. Consumption process
    2. Manufacturing process
    3. Exchange process
    4. Saving process
    Show answer

    Answer: (B) Manufacturing process

    Explanation: The notes call the stages from raw material to finished product the manufacturing process. Consumption, exchange and saving refer to different economic actions, not this production transformation. This distinction is useful for matching concepts accurately in competitive examinations.

  10. The government acts in the economy as a:

    1. Consumer only
    2. Producer only
    3. Regulator only
    4. Consumer, producer and regulator
    Show answer

    Answer: (D) Consumer, producer and regulator

    Explanation: The government sector acts as consumer, producer and regulator. Each of the first three choices is incomplete because the notes assign all three roles to government. This distinction is useful for matching concepts accurately in competitive examinations.

  11. The external sector covers international economic interactions between:

    1. Government departments within one state
    2. Residents of the country and the rest of the world
    3. Only domestic producers and consumers
    4. Only banks and insurance companies
    Show answer

    Answer: (B) Residents of the country and the rest of the world

    Explanation: The external sector covers international interactions between the country’s residents, public or private, and the rest of the world. The other choices are confined to domestic actors and do not express the international scope of the external sector. This distinction is useful for matching concepts accurately in competitive examinations.

  12. Which of the following is NOT listed as an example of external-sector activity in the notes?

    1. Exports
    2. International investment
    3. Capital account
    4. Retail outlet
    Show answer

    Answer: (D) Retail outlet

    Explanation: Retail outlet is not listed as an external-sector activity; it is an example of a physical market. Exports, international investment and capital account are all listed among external-sector examples. This distinction is useful for matching concepts accurately in competitive examinations.

  13. Assertion (A): A household can be a producer in the economy. Reason (R): Household members may work as employees in manufacturing.

    1. Both A and R are true and R is the correct explanation of A
    2. Both A and R are true but R is not the correct explanation of A
    3. A is true but R is false
    4. A is false but R is true
    Show answer

    Answer: (A) Both A and R are true and R is the correct explanation of A

    Explanation: Both statements are true, and working as employees in manufacturing explains the household’s producer role. The other alternatives wrongly deny the stated truth or the direct explanatory link. This distinction is useful for matching concepts accurately in competitive examinations.

  14. Consider the following statements about the government sector: 1. It includes government services. 2. It includes government-owned businesses. 3. Its GDP expenditure role is net exports. Which of the statements given above are correct?

    1. 1 and 2 only
    2. 2 and 3 only
    3. 1 and 3 only
    4. 1, 2 and 3
    Show answer

    Answer: (A) 1 and 2 only

    Explanation: Statements 1 and 2 are correct because the government sector includes services and government-owned businesses. Statement 3 is incorrect because net exports belong to the foreign/external sector, while government’s component is government purchases. This distinction is useful for matching concepts accurately in competitive examinations.

  15. The closest example of perfect competition given in the notes is:

    1. Indian Railways
    2. Agricultural produce markets
    3. Telecom industry
    4. Restaurants
    Show answer

    Answer: (B) Agricultural produce markets

    Explanation: Agricultural produce markets are given as the closest example of perfect competition. Indian Railways is the monopoly example, telecom is oligopoly, and restaurants are linked with monopolistic competition. This distinction is useful for matching concepts accurately in competitive examinations.

  16. Which example is associated with monopolistic competition in the notes?

    1. Mining
    2. Toothpaste
    3. Steel manufacturing
    4. Indian Railways
    Show answer

    Answer: (B) Toothpaste

    Explanation: Toothpaste is listed as an example of monopolistic competition. Mining is primary activity, steel manufacturing is secondary activity, and Indian Railways is the monopoly example. This distinction is useful for matching concepts accurately in competitive examinations.

  17. Which feature distinguishes monopolistic competition from perfect competition in the notes?

    1. Products are similar but slightly differentiated
    2. There is only one seller
    3. Products must be homogeneous
    4. No seller can charge a slightly higher price
    Show answer

    Answer: (A) Products are similar but slightly differentiated

    Explanation: Monopolistic competition has similar but slightly differentiated products, allowing sellers to charge slightly higher prices. One seller describes monopoly, homogeneous products describe perfect competition, and the final choice contradicts the notes. This distinction is useful for matching concepts accurately in competitive examinations.

  18. Consider the following statements: 1. The household sector pays taxes to the government. 2. The business/production sector is responsible for creating goods and services. 3. The external sector includes balance of payments. Which of the statements given above are correct?

    1. 1 and 2 only
    2. 2 and 3 only
    3. 1 and 3 only
    4. 1, 2 and 3
    Show answer

    Answer: (D) 1, 2 and 3

    Explanation: All three statements are correct as stated in the notes. Households pay taxes, the production sector creates goods and services, and balance of payments is included among external-sector interactions. This distinction is useful for matching concepts accurately in competitive examinations.
