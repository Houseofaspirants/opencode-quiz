Question bank

Union and its Territory - Part 1: all 25 questions

Read the question, pick an option, then open the answer.

  1. Article 1 of the Constitution describes India, that is Bharat, as a:

    1. Quasi-federal Union
    2. Federation of States
    3. Union of States
    4. Confederation of States
    Show answer

    Answer: (C) Union of States

    Explanation: Article 1 describes India, that is Bharat, as a 'Union of States' rather than a federation of states.

  2. According to Article 1, the territory of India is classified into how many categories?

    1. Two
    2. Four
    3. Three
    4. Five
    Show answer

    Answer: (C) Three

    Explanation: Territory of India: (1) territories of the states, (2) union territories, (3) territories that may be acquired by the Government of India.

  3. Which of the following is NOT a category of the territory of India under Article 1?

    1. Union territories
    2. Territories leased to foreign countries
    3. Territories that may be acquired by the Government of India
    4. Territories of the states
    Show answer

    Answer: (B) Territories leased to foreign countries

    Explanation: The three categories are territories of the states, union territories and territories that may be acquired.

  4. The names of states and union territories and their territorial extent are given in which Schedule?

    1. Fourth Schedule
    2. Second Schedule
    3. Seventh Schedule
    4. First Schedule
    Show answer

    Answer: (D) First Schedule

    Explanation: The names of states and UTs and their territorial extent are given in the First Schedule.

  5. Union territories and acquired territories are administered directly by:

    1. The nearest state government
    2. The Central government
    3. The Supreme Court
    4. The Election Commission
    Show answer

    Answer: (B) The Central government

    Explanation: Union territories and acquired territories are directly administered by the Central government.

  6. Why is India called a 'Union of States' and not a federation?

    1. States have their own constitutions
    2. States have the right to secede
    3. The country is an integral whole, divided into states only for administrative convenience
    4. States signed an agreement to form India
    Show answer

    Answer: (C) The country is an integral whole, divided into states only for administrative convenience

    Explanation: India is an integral whole, divided into states only for the convenience of administration.

  7. Which Article empowers Parliament to 'admit into the Union of India, or establish, new states'?

    1. Article 3
    2. Article 2
    3. Article 4
    4. Article 1
    Show answer

    Answer: (B) Article 2

    Explanation: Article 2 empowers Parliament to admit into the Union, or establish, new states on terms it thinks fit.

  8. Article 2 grants which two powers to Parliament?

    1. To admit new states into the Union and to establish new states
    2. To cede territory and to acquire territory
    3. To create union territories and abolish states
    4. To alter boundaries and names of states
    Show answer

    Answer: (A) To admit new states into the Union and to establish new states

    Explanation: Article 2: (a) power to admit new states into the Union; (b) power to establish new states.

  9. Which Article relates to the formation of new states and alteration of areas, boundaries or names of existing states?

    1. Article 2
    2. Article 1
    3. Article 368
    4. Article 3
    Show answer

    Answer: (D) Article 3

    Explanation: Article 3 relates to the formation of new states or changes in existing states.

  10. Under Article 3, Parliament can do all of the following EXCEPT:

    1. Cede Indian territory to a foreign state
    2. Alter the name of any state
    3. Increase the area of any state
    4. Alter the boundaries of any state
    Show answer

    Answer: (A) Cede Indian territory to a foreign state

    Explanation: Ceding Indian territory to a foreign state requires a constitutional amendment under Article 368, not a law under Article 3.

  11. A bill under Article 3 can be introduced in Parliament only with the prior recommendation of:

    1. The President
    2. The Prime Minister
    3. The Speaker of Lok Sabha
    4. The concerned Governor
    Show answer

    Answer: (A) The President

    Explanation: Such a bill can be introduced in Parliament only with the prior recommendation of the President.

  12. Before recommending a bill under Article 3, the President must refer it to:

    1. The Finance Commission
    2. The Supreme Court for its opinion
    3. The Election Commission
    4. The concerned state legislature for its views
    Show answer

    Answer: (D) The concerned state legislature for its views

    Explanation: The President must refer the bill to the concerned state legislature for its views within a specified period.

  13. Consider the following statements regarding Article 3: 1. The bill requires the prior recommendation of the President. 2. Parliament is bound by the views of the concerned state legislature. Which of the statements given above is/are correct?

    1. 1 only
    2. 2 only
    3. Both 1 and 2
    4. Neither 1 nor 2
    Show answer

    Answer: (A) 1 only

    Explanation: Statement 1 is correct. The President (or Parliament) is NOT bound by the views of the state legislature.

  14. India is rightly described as:

    1. A confederation of sovereign states
    2. An indestructible union of destructible states
    3. A destructible union of indestructible states
    4. An indestructible union of indestructible states
    Show answer

    Answer: (B) An indestructible union of destructible states

    Explanation: India is 'an indestructible union of destructible states': the Centre can reorganise states, but states cannot destroy the Union.

  15. Laws made under Articles 2 and 3 are NOT considered amendments of the Constitution under Article 368. This is declared by:

    1. Article 4
    2. Article 352
    3. Article 5
    4. Article 1
    Show answer

    Answer: (A) Article 4

    Explanation: Article 4 declares that laws under Articles 2 and 3 are not amendments under Article 368.

  16. Laws for the reorganisation of states under Article 3 can be passed by:

    1. A two-thirds majority in a joint sitting
    2. Special majority plus ratification by half the states
    3. A simple majority through the ordinary legislative process
    4. A special majority under Article 368
    Show answer

    Answer: (C) A simple majority through the ordinary legislative process

    Explanation: Under Article 4, such laws are passed by a simple majority through the ordinary legislative process.

  17. Indian territory can be ceded to a foreign state only by:

    1. A law under Article 3
    2. Amending the Constitution under Article 368
    3. A resolution of the Lok Sabha
    4. An executive order of the President
    Show answer

    Answer: (B) Amending the Constitution under Article 368

    Explanation: Indian territory can be ceded to a foreign state only by amending the Constitution under Article 368.

  18. Match the Articles with their subject: A. Article 1 – 1. Admission or establishment of new states B. Article 2 – 2. Laws under Arts 2 & 3 not amendments C. Article 3 – 3. Name and territory of the Union D. Article 4 – 4. Formation of new states and alteration of existing states

    1. A-3, B-1, C-2, D-4
    2. A-3, B-4, C-1, D-2
    3. A-1, B-3, C-4, D-2
    4. A-3, B-1, C-4, D-2
    Show answer

    Answer: (D) A-3, B-1, C-4, D-2

    Explanation: Art 1 – name & territory; Art 2 – admission/establishment; Art 3 – formation/alteration of states; Art 4 – such laws not amendments.

  19. At the time of independence, India had which two kinds of political units?

    1. States and union territories
    2. Part A and Part B states
    3. British provinces and princely states
    4. Provinces and districts
    Show answer

    Answer: (C) British provinces and princely states

    Explanation: At independence, India had British provinces (direct British rule) and princely states (ruled by native princes under British paramountcy).

  20. How many princely states were there within India's geographical boundaries at the time of independence?

    1. 500
    2. 552
    3. 565
    4. 549
    Show answer

    Answer: (B) 552

    Explanation: Of the 552 princely states within India's boundaries, 549 joined India after independence.

  21. Which three princely states initially refused to join India?

    1. Hyderabad, Junagarh and Kashmir
    2. Patiala, Hyderabad and Kashmir
    3. Junagarh, Bhopal and Kashmir
    4. Hyderabad, Mysore and Travancore
    Show answer

    Answer: (A) Hyderabad, Junagarh and Kashmir

    Explanation: Hyderabad, Junagarh and Kashmir refused to join initially but were later integrated.

  22. Hyderabad was integrated with India through:

    1. Referendum
    2. Instrument of Accession
    3. Treaty with Pakistan
    4. Police action
    Show answer

    Answer: (D) Police action

    Explanation: Hyderabad – police action; Junagarh – referendum; Kashmir – Instrument of Accession.

  23. Junagarh was integrated with India through:

    1. Instrument of Accession
    2. Police action
    3. Military war
    4. Referendum
    Show answer

    Answer: (D) Referendum

    Explanation: Junagarh was integrated through a referendum.

  24. On which date did Kashmir sign the Instrument of Accession with India?

    1. 13 September 1948
    2. 26 January 1950
    3. 15 August 1947
    4. 26 October 1947
    Show answer

    Answer: (D) 26 October 1947

    Explanation: Kashmir joined India through the Instrument of Accession on 26 October 1947.

  25. Who played an important role in the integration of the princely states along with Sardar Vallabhbhai Patel?

    1. K.M. Munshi
    2. V.K. Krishna Menon
    3. V.P. Menon
    4. B.N. Rau
    Show answer

    Answer: (C) V.P. Menon

    Explanation: Sardar Vallabhbhai Patel and V.P. Menon played an important role in integrating the princely states.
