Question bank

System of Government in India - Part 1: all 25 questions

Read the question, pick an option, then open the answer.

  1. At the central level, who is the head of the nation (head of state)?

    1. The Prime Minister
    2. The Chief Justice
    3. The President
    4. The Speaker
    Show answer

    Answer: (C) The President

    Explanation: At the central level, the President is the head of state; the Prime Minister is the head of government.

  2. Who is the head of government at the central level in India?

    1. The President
    2. The Prime Minister
    3. The Vice-President
    4. The Cabinet Secretary
    Show answer

    Answer: (B) The Prime Minister

    Explanation: The Prime Minister is the head of government at the Centre.

  3. At the state level, who is the head of state?

    1. The Governor
    2. The Chief Minister
    3. The Chief Secretary
    4. The Speaker of the Assembly
    Show answer

    Answer: (A) The Governor

    Explanation: At the state level, the Governor is the head of state and the Chief Minister is the head of government.

  4. Who is the head of a district in India?

    1. District Magistrate / Deputy Commissioner
    2. Superintendent of Police
    3. Zila Parishad Chairman
    4. Member of Legislative Assembly
    Show answer

    Answer: (A) District Magistrate / Deputy Commissioner

    Explanation: At the district level, the District Magistrate / Deputy Commissioner (D.C.) is the head of the district.

  5. Rural areas in a district are administered by:

    1. State Legislature
    2. Village panchayats
    3. Municipal corporations
    4. Cantonment boards
    Show answer

    Answer: (B) Village panchayats

    Explanation: Rural areas are administered by village panchayats; urban areas by municipal bodies.

  6. Laws made by a state legislature are applicable:

    1. In neighbouring states also
    2. In the whole of India
    3. Only in that state
    4. Only in union territories
    Show answer

    Answer: (C) Only in that state

    Explanation: Each state has its own legislature that makes laws applicable only in that state.

  7. Arrange the levels of Indian administration from top to bottom:

    1. Central Government → State Government → District Administration → Local Government
    2. State Government → Central Government → Local Government → District Administration
    3. Central Government → District Administration → State Government → Local Government
    4. Local Government → District Administration → State Government → Central Government
    Show answer

    Answer: (A) Central Government → State Government → District Administration → Local Government

    Explanation: The hierarchy is Centre → State → District → Local (Panchayats/Municipalities).

  8. A democratic government is a group of people:

    1. Selected by religious leaders
    2. Appointed by the army
    3. Nominated by the monarch
    4. Elected directly or indirectly by the people to run the society
    Show answer

    Answer: (D) Elected directly or indirectly by the people to run the society

    Explanation: A democratic government is a group of people elected directly or indirectly by the people of a nation to run its society effectively.

  9. Which of the following countries follows the presidential system of government?

    1. India
    2. UK
    3. USA
    4. Canada
    Show answer

    Answer: (C) USA

    Explanation: Presidential system — USA, South Korea; Parliamentary — UK, India, Canada.

  10. Which of the following groups of countries follow the parliamentary system?

    1. USA and South Korea
    2. USA, India and UK
    3. UK, India and Canada
    4. India, USA and Canada
    Show answer

    Answer: (C) UK, India and Canada

    Explanation: UK, India and Canada follow the parliamentary system.

  11. South Korea follows which system of government?

    1. Monarchy
    2. Parliamentary
    3. Presidential
    4. Quasi-federal parliamentary
    Show answer

    Answer: (C) Presidential

    Explanation: South Korea, like the USA, follows the presidential system.

  12. In the parliamentary system, the chief executive is:

    1. The President
    2. The Prime Minister
    3. The Chief Justice
    4. The Speaker
    Show answer

    Answer: (B) The Prime Minister

    Explanation: Chief executive: Prime Minister in the parliamentary system; President in the presidential system.

  13. In the presidential system, the chief executive is:

    1. The Vice-President
    2. The President
    3. The Senate leader
    4. The Prime Minister
    Show answer

    Answer: (B) The President

    Explanation: In the presidential system, the President is the chief executive.

  14. In India's parliamentary system, the government is formed by:

    1. The President's nominees
    2. The party that wins the most seats in the Rajya Sabha
    3. The party with most state governments
    4. The party that wins the most seats in the Lok Sabha election
    Show answer

    Answer: (D) The party that wins the most seats in the Lok Sabha election

    Explanation: The government is formed by the party winning the most seats in the Lok Sabha; it selects a leader as Prime Minister.

  15. In India, the President is called the:

    1. Real executive
    2. Chief legislator
    3. Nominal executive
    4. Head of government
    Show answer

    Answer: (C) Nominal executive

    Explanation: The President is the nominal executive; the Prime Minister is the real executive.

  16. In India, the real executive is:

    1. The President
    2. The Governor
    3. The Prime Minister
    4. The Vice-President
    Show answer

    Answer: (C) The Prime Minister

    Explanation: The Prime Minister (head of government) is the real executive.

  17. How is the Prime Minister elected in India?

    1. By the Rajya Sabha only
    2. Directly by the people
    3. By the President's discretion alone
    4. Indirectly, by the members of the legislature
    Show answer

    Answer: (D) Indirectly, by the members of the legislature

    Explanation: The Prime Minister is elected indirectly by the members of the legislature (Parliament).

  18. Which of the following is a feature of the parliamentary system?

    1. Separation of powers
    2. Fixed tenure
    3. Single executive
    4. Dual executive
    Show answer

    Answer: (D) Dual executive

    Explanation: Parliamentary system has a dual executive (President and Prime Minister).

  19. Which of the following is a feature of the presidential system?

    1. Collective responsibility
    2. Separation of powers
    3. Dissolution of the lower house
    4. Double membership
    Show answer

    Answer: (B) Separation of powers

    Explanation: Presidential system: separation of powers, single executive, fixed tenure, no dissolution.

  20. 'Fusion of powers' is a characteristic of:

    1. Unitary system only
    2. Military rule
    3. Presidential system
    4. Parliamentary system
    Show answer

    Answer: (D) Parliamentary system

    Explanation: Parliamentary system: fusion of powers; presidential system: separation of powers.

  21. 'Double membership' in the parliamentary system means:

    1. Members of the legislature also become members of the executive
    2. Members can belong to two parties
    3. A person can be a member of both Houses
    4. A person can hold two citizenships
    Show answer

    Answer: (A) Members of the legislature also become members of the executive

    Explanation: Double membership: members of the legislature also become members of the executive (ministers).

  22. Consider the following statements:1.In the parliamentary system, the tenure of the government is fixed.2.In the presidential system, the lower house cannot be dissolved.Which of the statements given above is/are correct?

    1. 1 only
    2. 2 only
    3. Both 1 and 2
    4. Neither 1 nor 2
    Show answer

    Answer: (B) 2 only

    Explanation: Parliamentary system: tenure not fixed. Presidential system: fixed tenure and no dissolution of the lower house.

  23. 'Collective responsibility' is a feature of:

    1. Parliamentary system
    2. Both systems equally
    3. Presidential system
    4. Neither system
    Show answer

    Answer: (A) Parliamentary system

    Explanation: Collective responsibility is a feature of the parliamentary system; the presidential system has non-responsibility.

  24. In which system is the President the head of both state and government?

    1. Unitary parliamentary system
    2. Parliamentary system
    3. Quasi-federal system
    4. Presidential system
    Show answer

    Answer: (D) Presidential system

    Explanation: In the presidential system, there is one head — the President is head of both state and government.

  25. Match the features with the system:A.Majority party rule – 1. PresidentialB.Single membership – 2. ParliamentaryC.Leadership of the Prime Minister – 3. ParliamentaryD.Fixed tenure – 4. Presidential

    1. A-2, B-1, C-3, D-4
    2. A-1, B-2, C-3, D-4
    3. A-2, B-3, C-1, D-4
    4. A-2, B-1, C-4, D-3
    Show answer

    Answer: (A) A-2, B-1, C-3, D-4

    Explanation: Majority party rule and PM leadership – parliamentary; single membership and fixed tenure – presidential.
