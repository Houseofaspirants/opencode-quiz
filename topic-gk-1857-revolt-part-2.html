  2. The British captured Delhi on:

    1. 10 May 1857
    2. 20 September 1857
    3. 6 December 1857
    4. 2 August 1858
    Show answer

    Answer: (B) 20 September 1857

    Explanation: The correct answer is 20 September 1857. The British captured Delhi on 20 September 1857. Bahadur Shah was then exiled to Rangoon, ending the House of the Mughals. The other dates relate to Meerut, Kanpur and the Government of India Act respectively. This distinction is useful for matching and chronology questions on the Revolt of 1857.

  3. In which month did Rani Laxmibai die fighting the British near Gwalior?

    1. July 1857
    2. December 1857
    3. June 1858
    4. November 1858
    Show answer

    Answer: (C) June 1858

    Explanation: Rani Laxmibai died fighting near Gwalior in June 1858. Sir Hugh Rose had already recaptured Jhansi earlier, in April 1858. July 1857 is associated with Shah Mal’s death, December 1857 with Kanpur, and November 1858 with the Queen’s Proclamation.

  4. Consider the following statements about the later phase of the revolt:1.By the end of 1859, Kunwar Singh, Tantia Tope and Bakht Khan were all dead.2.Rao Sahib was the nephew of Nana Saheb.3.The Begum of Awadh was forced to hide in Nepal.Which of the statements given above are correct?

    1. 1 and 2 only
    2. 1 and 3 only
    3. 2 and 3 only
    4. 1, 2 and 3
    Show answer

    Answer: (D) 1, 2 and 3

    Explanation: All three statements are correct. By the end of 1859 the leaders of the revolt, including Kunwar Singh, Tantia Tope, Bakht Khan, Khan Bahadur Khan of Bareilly and Maulvi Ahmadullah, were dead. Rao Sahib was Nana Saheb’s nephew. The Begum of Awadh was forced to hide in Nepal, and British authority was fully re-established by the end of 1859.

  5. Which of the following were reasons for the failure of the Revolt of 1857?1.Eastern, southern and western parts remained largely unaffected.2.Educated Indians viewed it as backward-looking.3.Most Indian rulers actively supported the revolt.4.Some classes and groups worked against it.

    1. 1, 2 and 4 only
    2. 1 and 3 only
    3. 2 and 3 only
    4. 1, 2, 3 and 4
    Show answer

    Answer: (A) 1, 2 and 4 only

    Explanation: The correct answer is 1, 2 and 4 only. Lack of all-India participation, the attitude of educated Indians, and opposition from some groups contributed to failure. Most Indian rulers refused to join and often helped the British, so statement 3 is false. The revolt nevertheless saw complete cooperation between Hindus and Muslims. This distinction is useful for matching and chronology questions on the Revolt of 1857.

  6. Which of the following rulers did NOT help the British during the Revolt of 1857?

    1. Maharaja of Kashmir
    2. Nana Saheb of Kanpur
    3. Sindhia of Gwalior
    4. Holkar of Indore
    Show answer

    Answer: (B) Nana Saheb of Kanpur

    Explanation: The correct answer is Nana Saheb of Kanpur. Sindhia of Gwalior, Holkar of Indore and the Maharaja of Kashmir are listed among rulers who helped the British. Nana Saheb fought against the British at Kanpur. The rulers of Patiala and Sindh and other Sikh chieftains are also listed as supporters of the British. This distinction is useful for matching and chronology questions on the Revolt of 1857.

  7. Assertion (A): The Revolt of 1857 failed despite complete cooperation between Hindus and Muslims during the revolt.Reason (R): The revolt lacked all-India participation and support from several important groups.

    1. A is true but R is false
    2. A is false but R is true
    3. Both A and R are true and R is the correct explanation of A
    4. Both A and R are true but R is not the correct explanation of A
    Show answer

    Answer: (C) Both A and R are true and R is the correct explanation of A

    Explanation: The correct answer is both A and R are true and R is the correct explanation of A. The revolt failed, although Hindu-Muslim cooperation remained complete throughout it. Lack of all-India participation and absence of support from important groups were reasons for that failure. The reason directly explains why cooperation alone did not ensure success. This distinction is useful for matching and chronology questions on the Revolt of 1857.

  8. The Act for the Better Government of India was passed by the British Parliament on:

    1. 1 November 1858
    2. 1 January 1859
    3. 1 January 1861
    4. 2 August 1858
    Show answer

    Answer: (D) 2 August 1858

    Explanation: The correct answer is 2 August 1858. The British Parliament passed the Act for the Better Government of India on 2 August 1858. It is also known as the Government of India Act, 1858. The Queen’s Proclamation followed on 1 November 1858. This distinction is useful for matching and chronology questions on the Revolt of 1857.

With reference to the Government of India Act, 1858, consider the following statements:1.Queen Victoria was declared the sovereign of British India.2.A Secretary of State for India, a member of the British cabinet, was appointed.3.The East India Company continued direct responsibility for administration.Which of the statements given above are correct?

    1. 1 and 2 only

    4. 1, 2 and 3

    Answer: (A) 1 and 2 only

    Explanation: The correct answer is 1 and 2 only. The Act declared Queen Victoria sovereign of British India and appointed a Secretary of State for India. The British Crown took direct responsibility, while Company rule was abolished, so statement 3 is false. This was a central constitutional consequence of the revolt. This distinction is useful for matching and chronology questions on the Revolt of 1857.

  13. After the revolt, Indian states were required to recognise the paramountcy of the:

    1. British Crown
    2. British Parliament
    3. Secretary of State for India
    4. East India Company
    Show answer

    Answer: (A) British Crown

    Explanation: The correct answer is British Crown. Indian states had to recognise the paramountcy of the British Crown and were treated as parts of a single charge. The East India Company’s rule had been abolished. Parliament passed the Act, and the Secretary of State was an appointed office. This distinction is useful for matching and chronology questions on the Revolt of 1857.

  14. In line with Queen's Proclamation, which Act was passed to open government services irrespective of race or creed?

    3. Indian Councils Act, 1861

    Answer: (B) Indian Civil Service Act, 1861

    Explanation: The correct answer is Indian Civil Service Act, 1861. Government services were opened to all irrespective of race or creed, and the Indian Civil Service Act of 1861 was passed in line with the proclamation. The Government of India Act ended Company rule. The other Acts are not identified here with this provision. This distinction is useful for matching and chronology questions on the Revolt of 1857.

  17. From which of the following regions were 'martial' races recruited after the revolt?

    1. Punjab, Nepal and the north-western frontier
    2. Madras, Bombay and Bengal
    3. Awadh, Bundelkhand and Bihar
    4. Bengal, Bihar and Orissa
    Show answer

    Answer: (A) Punjab, Nepal and the north-western frontier

    Explanation: The correct answer is Punjab, Nepal and the north-western frontier. Recruits were drawn from martial races of Punjab, Nepal and the north-western frontier because they had stayed loyal to the British during the revolt. The other regional sets are not specified for this recruitment policy. This was part of army reorganisation. This distinction is useful for matching and chronology questions on the Revolt of 1857.

  18. Which of the following is NOT correct regarding the post-1857 army?

    1. European soldiers were increased.
    2. All higher army and artillery posts were reserved for Indians.
    3. Separate units were created by caste, community and region.
    4. Indian soldiers were drastically reduced.
    Show answer

    Answer: (B) All higher army and artillery posts were reserved for Indians.

    Explanation: The correct answer is all higher army and artillery posts were reserved for Indians. All higher posts in the army and artillery departments were reserved for Europeans, not Indians. Indian troop numbers were reduced, European troop numbers increased, and separate units were created. These measures formed part of British military policy after 1857. This distinction is useful for matching and chronology questions on the Revolt of 1857.

The sequence connects the constitutional changes with the final Mughal ruler’s death. This distinction is useful for matching and chronology questions on the Revolt of 1857.

  20. Who described the Revolt of 1857 as a 'War of Independence'?

    1. Lawrence and Seeley
    2. T.R. Holmes
    3. R.C. Majumdar
    4. V.D. Savarkar
    Show answer

    Answer: (D) V.D. Savarkar

    Explanation: The correct answer is V.D. Savarkar. V.D. Savarkar described it as a War of Independence. R.C. Majumdar called it neither First, nor National, nor a War of Independence; Lawrence and Seeley called it a mere sepoy mutiny; and T.R. Holmes described a conflict between civilisation and barbarism. These are distinct interpretations. This distinction is useful for matching and chronology questions on the Revolt of 1857.
