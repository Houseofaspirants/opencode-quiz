I cleared the Punjab Police Sub Inspector exam with **Rank 2 (713/800)**. These three posts share one paper, and its subject part is an aircraft maintenance engineer's syllabus - AME and aeronautical diploma holders are the natural candidates.

> **PSSSB Advertisement No. 02 of 2024** was published on **14 February 2024**; applications ran from **16 February to 11 March 2024**, fee by 14 March 2024. Age is counted as on 1 January 2024. This page uses that advertisement and the board's official plan of written examination for the three posts. Our copy of the advertisement is a scan in which the post counts are not legible, so they are not given here.

## Latest notification

The three posts were advertised in **PSSSB Advt 02/2024**, together with [Labour Inspector Grade-I](exams/psssb-labour-inspector.html). No newer advertisement for them is in our archive yet.

## Eligibility (Advt 02/2024)

- **Technician Grade-I:** 10+2, a **3-year DGCA-approved Aircraft Maintenance Engineering (AME) course**, **3 years of helicopter maintenance experience** and DOEACC O-level.
- **Technical Officer and Technician Grade-III:** 10+2, a **3-year AME course** and DOEACC O-level.
- All three: **matric with Punjabi.**

## Salary and pay

| Post | Basic pay (7th CPC) |

## Exam pattern (Advt 02/2024 official plan)

| Part | What | Questions / marks | Role |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| A | Matric-level Punjabi | 50 | **Qualifying - 25 marks needed**, no negative marking |
| B | GK, Punjab history and culture, reasoning, Punjabi, English, ICT | 40 | Counts for merit |
| C | Subject - aircraft engineering and maintenance | 60 | Counts for merit |

- **Time:** 2 hours 30 minutes. **1/4 mark (0.25) cut** for each wrong answer in Parts B and C.

## Syllabus

**Part B (40):** GK and current affairs 10, Punjab history and culture 5, reasoning and numeracy 10, Punjabi 5, English 5, ICT 5.

**Part C (60) - nine units:**

1. **Electrical fundamentals** - DC circuits and network analysis, AC values and power factor, transformers, DC motors and generators, AC generators and motors.
2. **Aerodynamics** - airflow, boundary layer, lift and drag, aerofoil terms, stalls, stability.
3. **Electronics and digital techniques** - diodes, SCR, transistors, logic and op-amp circuits, synchros, ECAM, EFIS, GPS, TCAS, ESD-sensitive devices.
4. **Materials and hardware** - aircraft alloy steels and heat treatment, composites and non-metallics, sealants, fabrics, bolts, locking devices and rivets.
5. **Gas turbine and piston engines** - compressors, stall and surge, turbine blades, start and ignition systems, 2- and 4-stroke engines, construction, supercharging.
6. **Propellers** - blade angle and pitch, construction, fixed, controllable and constant-speed types, balancing, ice protection.
7. **Instrument systems (ATA 31) and lights (ATA 33)** - pitot-static and gyroscopic instruments, compasses, external and internal lights.
8. **Maintenance regulations** - ICAO, Aircraft Act and Rules, DGCA, CAR-21, CAR-M, CAR-145, CAR-66, CAR-147, airworthiness and registration documents.
9. **Human factors** - human performance, social psychology, fatigue and stress, human error, workplace hazards.

## How to prepare

1. **Use the DGCA CAR-66 module books** - modules 3, 4, 5, 6, 8, 9, 10, 15, 16 and 17 cover most of these units.
2. **Regulations (unit 8)** are pure memory - make a one-page table of each CAR.
3. **Part B is 40 marks**; a week of Punjab history and grammar is enough to make it safe.
