I own Atomic Habits in three languages. I first read it in English, then in Hindi, then in Punjabi. On the podcast the host said what I felt: we connect most with Punjabi. That small experiment changed how I study.

## Why your own language works better

When you read in English that you know but do not think in, part of your brain is busy translating. In your mother tongue that effort disappears and all your attention goes to the idea. You understand faster, and what you understand, you remember.

At first I read everything in English because I thought that was the "serious" way. Then someone suggested that the same authors' books are available in Punjabi. The understanding was simply better.

## How to use this for exam preparation

**1. Learn the concept in Punjabi first.**
For history, polity or economy, read the chapter in Punjabi notes until you can explain it out loud.

**2. Add the English terms.**
Write the key words in English beside the Punjabi: Fundamental Rights, Preamble, Five Year Plan. Exams and questions often use them.

**3. Revise in the language of your exam.**
If you are writing a Punjabi-medium paper, do your MCQs in Punjabi. If English, switch to English MCQs for revision.

**4. Read one book in two languages.**
Try it once with a book you love. You will notice ideas you missed the first time and pick up vocabulary for free.

## A double benefit in Punjab

Punjabi is itself a section in most Punjab exams, and in many PSSSB papers it is a qualifying part. Every page you read in good Punjabi improves your vocabulary, spelling and sentence sense for that section too.

## Do not feel small about it

Many students feel embarrassed about studying in Punjabi. Do not. Understanding is the goal, not looking impressive. Topper or not, everyone learns best in the language they dream in.

That is why every chapter on House of Aspirants has notes in both Punjabi and English, with MCQs in both.
