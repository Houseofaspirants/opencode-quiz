In a family where nobody has worn a uniform, saying "I want to join the police" can feel like dropping a bomb at the dinner table. That was my house too.

## My family's fear

I never imagined I would join the police. I decided in the last semester of my graduation. My mother was especially scared. She has seen how hard police work is, and how many allegations people make against the police. Even today she sometimes says: prepare for other civil jobs, clear those, and leave this.

My father was different. He said: "We trust you. Do what you have decided."

## Why I still chose it

I felt this was a field where I could do a good job till retirement and serve people. I also believe something I said on the podcast: if every person in uniform thought this way, it would be easier for them and for society. Trust in the uniform is growing, and I want to be part of that.

## How to talk to your family

**1. Listen to the fear before you answer it.**
Most parents fear two things: danger, and what people say about the police. Do not dismiss it. Say it out loud for them: "I know you are worried about safety and about the image."

**2. Be specific about the role.**
"Police" means many different jobs: district, armed, investigation, intelligence, technical cadres and more. Explain which post you want and what the work is.

**3. Show the plan, not the dream.**
Which exam, which year, how many hours a day, and what you will do if it takes longer. A plan calms parents more than passion does.

**4. Let one parent be your bridge.**
For me it was my father. Win one person first; they will help you with the other.

**5. Let your preparation speak.**
When your family sees you studying with discipline every day, the fear slowly becomes pride.

## Respect their fallback, but stay honest

If your family wants you to keep one civil exam as a backup, that can be fair. But do not fill forms for jobs you will never join; it can take a seat from someone who needs it. I explain this in [Should You Apply for Every Government Exam?](blog-should-you-apply-for-every-government-exam)

## Today

My mother still worries sometimes. But the day I called her at 1 a.m. to say I had Rank 2, she did not ask about danger. She asked if it was true.
