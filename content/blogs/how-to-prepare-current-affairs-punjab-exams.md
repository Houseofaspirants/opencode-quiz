In the papers I sat, **current affairs** gave a lot of questions, along with Indian Polity and Modern History. It is also the part of GK that changes every day, which makes students panic and read everything. Don't. The same rule that got me to Rank 2 works here too: **minimum resources, maximum revision.**

## Why current affairs needs its own system

GK is vast, and you can't cover all of it. But current affairs has a clear boundary, the months before your exam, so it is the most scoring part of GK if you handle it with a system. Without one, you read the news for months and remember nothing on exam day.

## My system

### 1. One daily source, 30 minutes

Pick **one** daily source and stay with it. Thirty focused minutes a day is enough. Reading three newspapers and five channels is the same mistake as reading five books for one subject: confusion, and no time left to revise.

### 2. Notes in your own words, by category

Don't copy news. Write one line per fact, under fixed headings:

- **Punjab:** schemes, appointments, events, rankings
- **National:** schemes, appointments, reports and indices
- **Awards and honours**
- **Sports**
- **Important days and themes**
- **International:** summits, organisations, agreements

Keeping **Punjab separate** matters. Punjab papers ask Punjab current affairs that national compilations often skip.

### 3. A monthly compilation

At the end of every month, read one monthly compilation and fill the gaps in your notes. This is why I write the **monthly current affairs magazine** on this site: one place for each month, Punjab included, so you don't have to collect it from ten sources.

### 4. Revise with 1-7-30

Each month's notes get revised the next day, after 7 days and after 30 days, exactly like any other subject. Without this, you will remember January's news only as a blur by June.

### 5. How far back to go

Cover **the last 6 to 12 months** before the exam. Spend most time on the **last 6 months**, and keep Punjab-specific facts for the whole year.

## What to skip

- **Political statements and opinion pieces.** Papers ask facts, not views.
- **Very local crime and accident news.**
- **Stock market and daily price movements.**
- **Long analysis.** If a fact can't be written in one line, it is rarely asked.

## In the last month

- Stop adding new sources. Revise your own notes only.
- Give **mock tests at your exam's time of day**, at home, in the same conditions (heat, no AC if your centre may not have it). Current affairs questions in the mocks show you which months you are weak in.
- On the day of the paper, don't open any notes. Last-minute reading only causes panic.

## Common mistakes

- Reading the news every day but **never revising**.
- Following too many sources and channels.
- Ignoring **Punjab** news because national compilations don't cover it.
- Starting current affairs only in the last month.

## Start today

Make the six headings in a notebook today. Read your one source for 30 minutes and write five one-line facts. At the end of the month, read the monthly magazine on this site and fill the gaps. Then revise with 1-7-30, and attempt the current affairs quizzes here every week.
