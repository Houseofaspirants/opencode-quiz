The question I get asked most is: "How many hours did you study every day?" The short answer is **8 to 10 hours on average.**

But the number alone does not help anyone. What matters is how those hours were spread across the day, and how I kept the routine going for months without burning out. Here is my full day, from waking up to going to sleep.

## My daily timetable

| Time | What I did |
|---|---|
| 9:00 am | Wake up, get fresh |
| 10:00 – 11:30 | Shift 1 (study) |
| 11:30 – 12:00 | 30-minute break |
| 12:00 – 1:30 | Shift 2 (study) |
| 1:30 – 2:30 | 1-hour break (lunch, rest) |
| 2:30 – 4:00 | Shift 3 (study) |
| 4:00 – 4:30 | 30-minute break |
| 4:30 – 6:00 | Shift 4 (study) |
| 6:00 – 8:00 | Gym / ground, bath, dinner |
| 8:00 – 9:30 | Shift 5 (study) |
| 9:30 – 10:00 | 30-minute break |
| 10:00 – 11:30 | Shift 6 (study) |
| 12:00 – 1:00 am | Plan the next day: what to study and how |
| 1:00 am – 9:00 am | Sleep (a full 8 hours) |

That is six shifts of 1.5 hours, about 9 hours a day. On a slow day it was 8 hours; on a good day, 10.

## Why this routine worked

- **A fixed place to study.** I had a study table and chair in my room. Sitting there at 10 am meant study had started.
- **Short shifts, fixed breaks.** Nobody can sit for 10 hours straight. A 1.5-hour shift is long enough to focus and short enough to finish. My blog on the [1.5-hour shift method](blog-study-in-90-minute-shifts) explains this in detail.
- **Gym in the evening.** The day's tiredness and frustration went out at the gym, so the two night shifts started with a fresh mind.
- **No compromise on sleep.** Whether I slept at 12 or 1, I got 8 hours. Without full sleep, the next day's 9 hours do not happen.

- **Planning the next day at night.** 12 to 1 am was my planning hour. I decided what to study the next day and how to study it. So when I sat down at 10 am, I already knew where to start and wasted no time deciding.

## Do you need to study 10 hours too?

Not on day one. If you are just starting, begin with 2 or 3 shifts and add more slowly. Doing 10 hours in week one and quitting in week three is worse than 5 hours a day for months. Hours matter, but studying every single day matters more.
