Many students write to me thinking that a topper was always a topper. I was not. For most of my school life I was at the bottom of the class, and at 14 my name was in an FIR. This is the full story I shared on the Daily Post Punjabi podcast.

## A village boy who did not study

I studied in my village school. My two elder sisters were good at studies. I was not. Until class 9, I do not think I studied properly even once. I played cricket with boys three or four years older than me, and my whole day went in roaming around.

## The FIR at 14

In class 9 there was a fight at school. It grew so big that on 4 January 2010 an FIR was registered with seven sections, including attempt to murder. I was 13–14 years old. I was not involved in the fight, but people fighting on my side were, so my name was added.

My family went to the High Court. On 27 July 2012 the FIR was quashed. Clearing it cost my middle-class family somewhere between 5 and 10 lakh rupees in lawyers' fees and settlements. That is when it hit me: my family had paid for my mistakes in money and in respect.

## Two kinds of answers

People in my village had started calling me a goonda. Relatives would not let their children play with me. I had two choices: answer them with anger, or answer them with results. I chose results. The first step was to leave the circle I was in. I asked my parents to send me to a hostel outside the village.

## Failed before I even started

At the first academy, the entrance test teacher was told to help me. He read out the questions and still I could not write anything. He came out and said, "Your son is so weak he cannot even write." I failed. The same day my family got me admitted to another hostel school in Jalandhar.

## Studying in a cupboard with a torch

That hostel was full of boys whose parents had sent them there out of frustration. If anyone saw you studying, they would tease you until you stopped. So I studied inside the store-room cupboard with a torch. For the first time in my life I fought with someone because I wanted to study. The warden finally gave me a separate room. I learned my ABC properly in that hostel, from children's books.

My class 9 score was 40%. In class 10 I got 60%, without copying.

## "You can't do non-medical"

After class 10 my teachers said: with your marks, take arts. I took that as being called weak again, so I chose non-medical. I got 76% in class 12, the first in my family to pass non-medical.

## LPU, placement, and saying no

During graduation at LPU I decided I would go into civil services. In the last semester I took my name out of campus placement, even though the packages start at several lakh. My teachers called my home to convince my parents. My father said, "We trust you. Do what you feel is right."

## Two near misses

In 2021 I scored very well in the SI exam, but the paper leaked and was cancelled. When it was held again, I scored 613 and the general cut-off was 623. I missed by 10 marks. How I went from 613 to 713 is in my blog [From 613 to 713](blog-previous-year-papers-613-to-713).

## The result night

On 10 February 2024 the result came out while I was in Dalhousie. Late at night I opened the PDF and started looking for my name from the bottom, because I was scared I had missed again. My name was near the top: Rank 2. I called my mother at 1 a.m. She said, "You are lying."

## What changed

The same relatives who told their children to stay away from me now ask me what their children should study. I do not blame them; society works like that. But I have proved one thing: whatever your past is, changing your life needs one decision and a different mindset.
