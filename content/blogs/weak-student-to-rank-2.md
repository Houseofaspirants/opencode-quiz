"I am weak in studies, government exams are not for me." I get this message almost every week. So let me tell you where I started.

## Where I started

Until class 9 I could say the ABC, but not in flow. My friends in the village used to tease me by asking me the spelling of "aeroplane" or "elephant". I could not write a simple answer in an entrance test even when the teacher read the questions out to me. In class 9 I scored 40%.

## Step 1: Accept your real level

The first thing I did in the hostel was something most students feel ashamed of. I picked up children's books, the ones with ABC and simple words, and learned from there. I fixed my pronunciation. Nobody becomes strong by pretending to be at a level they are not at.

**For you:** If a chapter in a standard book is not making sense, go one level down. School NCERT-level books are not an insult; they are the foundation.

## Step 2: Small wins, honestly earned

From 40% in class 9 I went to 60% in class 10, in a school where there was no copying. That 20% jump mattered more to me than any rank. It told me that effort works.

**For you:** Do not compare yourself to toppers in week one. Compare yourself to who you were last month.

## Step 3: Do not let others decide your limit

After class 10 my teachers said I should take arts because of my marks. Arts is a great stream (I recommend it for government jobs today), but the way they said it meant "you are weak". I chose non-medical out of stubbornness and got 76% in class 12, the first in my family to pass non-medical.

**For you:** Choose based on your goal, not on the label someone gives you.

## Step 4: Self-study or a teacher?

On the podcast I said this honestly: sharp students can self-study; students like me need someone to explain from the book. If you are starting weak, a good teacher, online class or a structured set of notes saves you months. Read my take in [Self Study vs Coaching](blog-self-study-vs-coaching).

## Step 5: Years, not weeks

From the bottom of my class to Rank 2 took years: school, graduation, two near misses, and six years after graduation to get the job. A weak start only means your plan has to be longer and steadier, not that it will fail.

## A simple start plan

- Pick one subject you fear the least and start there.
- Read one chapter a day from a basic book, then do that chapter's MCQs.
- Keep a notebook of words and concepts you did not understand; revise it weekly.
- After two months, move to standard books and previous year papers.
