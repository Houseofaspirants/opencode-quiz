This blog is for parents. If you are an aspirant, share it with your mother and father.

## What I saw in my own home

When I was preparing, relatives would tell my parents: "He sits behind a closed door all day. Check if he is even studying, or watching movies." Sometimes my parents also started to worry: is he really studying or just passing time? At times they said: leave it, if it is not happening, do something else.

I understand it. When a child walks a long road, the destination is far and there are many hurdles. But that is exactly the time the child needs support the most.

## What helped me

My father never came under anyone's influence. From the start he told me: "Son, we trust you. Do what you feel is right." When I refused campus placement, he backed me. That one sentence carried me through years.

## Five things parents can do

**1. Do not compare.**
In villages we compare marks with the neighbour's son or a cousin. Comparison did not make me study; it made me angry. It only worked because I chose to take it positively, and many children cannot.

**2. Support instead of criticising.**
When I was going wrong, criticism did not fix me. What a child needs is: "You can improve. You can become a better person." I once read a line in a book that stayed with me: you may feel satisfied after criticising someone, but the relationship gets damaged, and the person remembers those words even after they succeed.

**3. Trust the closed door.**
Long hours alone are normal in preparation. If you want to know how it is going, ask about the plan: which subject this week, which mock test, what score.

**4. Be the wall against relatives.**
You do not need to argue with anyone. Just do not pass their pressure on to your child.

**5. Agree on a time-bound plan together.**
Ask which exam, by when, and what happens if it does not work out. Read [Why Every Aspirant Needs a Plan B](blog-plan-b-for-government-job-aspirants) together.

## A note for city and village parents

Parents in cities are often more aware of exams and careers. In villages, most parents have never seen this process. That is not your fault. Ask, read, and let your child explain it to you.

## The result

The same relatives who doubted me now call to ask what their own children should do. My parents' trust paid off. Yours can too.
