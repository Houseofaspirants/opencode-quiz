The first thing students cut during preparation is sleep. In my routine, sleep had the highest priority. I studied 8-10 hours a day and **slept 8 hours every single day.**

## My sleep timing

After my last night shift, I planned the next day from 12 to 1 am, so I usually slept around 1. But whenever I slept, I woke up at 9, so I always got a full 8 hours. That is why my day started at 10 am, not at 5 or 6. I took my study hours from the day's shifts, not from my sleep.

## Why sleep matters

- **The next day's study.** Only with good sleep can you give your best the next day. With half sleep, the book is in front of you but the mind is somewhere else.
- **What you study stays longer.** What I studied in the day came back much better when my sleep was full. That is what helps in the exam.
- **9 hours of study needs sleep.** If you sleep 5 hours, you feel sleepy by the afternoon and your shifts break.

## A common myth

Do not cut your sleep after hearing things like "toppers sleep only 4-5 hours". In my experience, hours squeezed out of sleep are mostly wasted, because a tired brain does not remember what it studies.

## Small tips for better sleep

- Keep your sleeping and waking time about the same every day.
- Exercise in the evening. In my experience, I slept better on gym days.
- Do not scroll reels before sleeping, or another half hour is gone.
- If you sleep late, set your wake-up time to match, so you still get 8 hours.
