On the podcast the host noticed that my Instagram is full of lines about motivation and self-love, and asked where that comes from. Honestly, from books. I keep 15–20 of them in English, Punjabi and Hindi. Once you get the habit of reading them, you do not need anyone's motivation; you can motivate yourself even when you are alone.

Here are the five I recommend most, in the order I would read them.

## 1. Atomic Habits (James Clear)

**What it gave me:** a method. The book shows that small habits repeated daily change you more than big plans. It is the reason the gym became part of my day, and why missing it now feels like something is incomplete. I liked it so much that I read it in all three languages. How I use it for studying is in [The 10-Minute Study Rule](blog-atomic-habits-10-minute-study-rule).

## 2. The Psychology of Money (Morgan Housel)

**What it gave me:** respect for money. It explains that handling money is more about behaviour than intelligence. Most people around us earn but never learn how to manage, invest and spend; this book starts that thinking.

## 3. The Richest Man in Babylon (George S. Clason)

**What it gave me:** the simplest money rules I know, through short stories. It is only around a hundred pages, and of all the money books I have read, it is my favourite.

## 4. Think and Grow Rich (Napoleon Hill)

**What it gave me:** the power of a fixed goal and a burning desire for it. Useful when your preparation is long and you need to keep the target in front of you.

## 5. Rich Dad Poor Dad (Robert Kiyosaki)

**What it gave me:** a change in point of view. After reading it I felt I had been thinking about money in the wrong way all along, and that nobody had taught us these things.

## How to read without hurting your preparation

- **15–20 pages a day, at a fixed time.** Before sleeping or during a break works well.
- **Read in your language.** Most of these are available in Punjabi and Hindi; you understand and remember better in your own language ([why I do this](blog-read-in-your-mother-tongue)).
- **Write one line per chapter.** The lesson in your own words, not a quote.
- **Never during the last 3 months.** Then it is only previous papers and revision ([my last 3 months plan](blog-last-3-months-strategy)).

These are my personal picks, not exam books. For syllabus books, see the Books section on House of Aspirants.
