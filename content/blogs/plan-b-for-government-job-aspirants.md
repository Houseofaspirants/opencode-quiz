On the podcast I said something that many aspirants do not like to hear. In the exam I appeared for, roughly one to one and a half lakh candidates sat for a few hundred posts. Even if only one lakh appeared, around 99,700 of them did not get that job. What do they do next?

## The numbers behind the dream

Government jobs are respected, secure and worth fighting for; I fought for one for years. But no government can create a government job for every young person. Look at the official vacancy numbers in any big notification and compare them with the number of applicants. The gap is huge in almost every exam.

That does not mean you should not try. It means you should try with a plan for both outcomes.

## Why we end up without a Plan B

- **Herd study.** Many of us study what everyone studies, without asking what it leads to. Then there are more graduates in one subject than jobs.
- **No guidance.** In school, our guidance is limited to teachers and family, who mostly know medical, non-medical, B.Tech and M.Tech. Many departments and careers never come up.
- **Fear of looking unserious.** Students think a backup means they do not believe in themselves.

## What a Plan B can look like

1. **A skill that pays:** computer skills, accounts, typing and stenography, teaching, a trade. Many of these also help in exams.
2. **A related exam with a different timeline:** if your target is SI, a clerk or state exam you would genuinely join (see [Should You Apply for Every Government Exam?](blog-should-you-apply-for-every-government-exam)).
3. **A private job in your field,** with preparation alongside ([Study With a Job](blog-study-with-a-job)).
4. **Business.** If you have the skill to sell, as I say, even a broken nail, you can do business. Not everyone has that skill, but if you do, parents should take it seriously.

## How to build it without losing focus

- **Set a deadline.** Decide now: "I will give my target exam this many attempts or years."
- **Give the Plan B a small, fixed time.** For example, one hour a day or weekends.
- **Choose a Plan B that overlaps.** Computer, Punjabi typing or accounts can help your exam too.
- **Revisit it once a year,** not every week.

## Guidance should start early

I believe schools should have a guidance class in the year before class 10 ends and before class 12 ends, showing students all the options. Until that happens, families and students have to do this thinking themselves.

## A Plan B is not a lack of belief

I believed in myself and still knew what I would do if it did not work out. That calmness helped me prepare better, not worse.
