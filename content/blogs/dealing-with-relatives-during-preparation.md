The biggest problem during my preparation was not books or the syllabus. It was the people around me: relatives, people from my village, and sometimes even family members.

## What every aspirant hears

- "What are you doing? You've been studying for so long, and still nothing?"
- "There's nothing here. Go abroad."
- "Government jobs are for the lucky. You can't get one without money or contacts."
- "You're weak at studies. It will never happen for you."
- "Only those whose parents are officers get selected."

Hearing things like this again and again demotivates you, and your mind stops staying on your studies.

## My strategy: meet less, talk less

- I met relatives and negative people around me very rarely.
- Family functions, religious events or get-togethers: I went to very few of them.
- The reason was simple: I would only bring negativity back from there, and the next day's study would suffer.

## Why people do this

In my experience, many people around you do not want you to move ahead. They are not happy about your progress, so they hand you negativity. This is not only about government exams. Wherever in life you are working hard, you will meet these people.

About money and contacts, I will only say this: my first attempt was 613 and my next was 713. Those 100 marks came from hard work, not from anyone's contacts. The full story is in my blog [From 613 to 713](blog-previous-year-papers-613-to-713).

## What you can do

- Cut down on meeting and talking with negative people.
- You do not need to attend every function during your preparation.
- Keep a short answer ready when someone asks, like "Preparation is going on, I'll tell you when the result comes." Do not get into arguments.
- Stay close to the one or two people who support you.
- Answer them with your result, not with words.

If you want to stay motivated for a long time and keep control of yourself, staying away from negative people matters most.
