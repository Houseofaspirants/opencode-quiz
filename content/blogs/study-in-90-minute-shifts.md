Many students sit down in the morning planning to study 10 hours, and pick up their phone after 2. The same happened to me until I split my day into small parts. My method was simple: **1.5-hour shifts.**

## How a shift worked

1. Study for 1.5 hours, then take a 30-minute break.
2. Study another 1.5 hours. After two shifts, take a longer break (1 hour, or the 2-3 hour gym break in the evening).
3. Repeat this pair three times a day: morning, afternoon and night.

That makes six shifts, or about 9 hours of study. The full timetable is in my blog [How Many Hours I Studied Daily](blog-how-many-hours-i-studied-daily).

## Why it works

- **The goal feels small.** "10 hours" sounds scary. "Just 1.5 hours" sounds easy. I only had to think about one shift at a time.
- **The mind gets rest.** Focus starts to drop after about 1.5 hours. A 30-minute break means the next shift starts fresh.
- **Counting is easy.** At the end of the day, I only checked how many shifts I had done. Six meant a good day.

## What to do in a break

A break is rest for the mind. Get up and walk, drink water, eat something. Keep the break time fixed too, so 30 minutes does not turn into 2 hours.

## How to start

- In week one, do 2-3 shifts a day, then add one shift each week.
- If you are preparing alongside a job or college, one shift in the morning and 1-2 at night are enough.
- Before sleeping, plan the next day's shifts: what you will study in each one. I did this every night from 12 to 1 am, so I never wasted morning time deciding.
- Keep your phone away during a shift.
