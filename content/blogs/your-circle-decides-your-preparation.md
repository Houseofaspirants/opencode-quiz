My blog on [dealing with relatives](blog-dealing-with-relatives-during-preparation) is about the taunts you hear. This one is about something quieter and more dangerous: the friends you spend your day with.

## What my circle looked like

At 13–14 my friends were three or four years older than me. We played cricket, roamed the village and called it friendship. My family kept telling me not to stay with those boys. I did not listen, and that circle is what put my name in an FIR at 14 (the full story is in [My Story](blog-my-story-false-fir-to-si-rank-2)).

## The line I still believe

I said this on the podcast: when you live with people who have a sick mindset, you start feeling low like them. When you live with people who study, who dream of buying a BMW or growing their father's business, you start thinking like them. Your thought process becomes theirs.

## What I did

I asked my parents to send me to a hostel outside the village. Changing place alone did not fix everything. My first hostel was full of spoiled boys too, and studying there meant hiding in a cupboard with a torch. So I made the second change: I found the one other boy who wanted to study and we asked the warden for a separate room.

The lesson: a new place does not change you; new people do.

## Signs your circle is hurting your preparation

- Your evenings go in chatting, roaming or reels, and you call it "relaxing".
- Friends make fun of you for studying or for going to bed early.
- Nobody around you talks about exams, books or goals.
- You come back from them with less energy than you went with.

## How to change your circle without leaving home

- **Reduce, do not announce.** You do not need to fight anyone. Just give them fewer hours.
- **Find one serious partner.** One friend preparing for the same exam is worth ten casual friends.
- **Study where people study.** A library or a study room changes your mood the moment you walk in.
- **Choose online circles carefully.** Join groups where people share daily targets, not memes.
- **Keep one supportive elder close.** In my case it was my father, who trusted me when others did not.

## You can change, too

The boy who was once called a bad influence is now asked for guidance by the same families. That happened because I changed the people I sat with. Change your circle, and your preparation changes with it.
