On the podcast I said something that surprised a few people: most of us are financially illiterate. Look around your village or city and I would guess 90–95% of people do not know how to manage, invest and spend the money they earn. A government job gives you a steady salary. It does not teach you what to do with it.

*This is general learning from books and life, not investment advice. For decisions about your own money, speak to a qualified advisor.*

## The trap I see most often

Everyone knows what their salary is. Then they spend more than that: a new bike on EMI, a phone on EMI, functions, gifts. A few months later, a part of every salary is already gone before it arrives. That is the debt trap, and it catches people with good jobs too.

## Five habits to build from the first salary

**1. Pay yourself first.**
Decide a fixed share to save the day the salary comes, before you spend anything. The amount matters less than doing it every month.

**2. Build an emergency fund.**
Keep a few months of expenses aside for illness, family needs or surprises, so that one bad month does not push you into a loan.

**3. Separate needs from wants.**
Before any big purchase, wait a week. Most wants disappear; needs stay.

**4. Be very careful with loans and EMIs.**
A loan for a home or education can make sense. A loan for something you want to show off rarely does.

**5. Learn before you invest.**
Do not put money anywhere because a colleague or a reel said so. Understand what it is, the risk, and how you will get the money back.

## Books that taught me this

- **The Richest Man in Babylon**: the simplest rules on saving and growing money, through short stories. My favourite money book.
- **The Psychology of Money**: why behaviour matters more than intelligence with money.
- **Rich Dad Poor Dad**: changes how you look at income, assets and spending.
- **Think and Grow Rich**: about goals and the mindset behind building wealth.

More on these in [5 Non-Syllabus Books Every Aspirant Should Read](blog-non-syllabus-books-for-aspirants).

## For aspirants still preparing

Start learning this now. When the job comes, you will already have a plan for the first salary instead of learning from mistakes.
