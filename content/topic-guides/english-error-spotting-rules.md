For English, I didn't use any big grammar book. The Punjab Board books of classes 10, 11 and 12 were enough, because the paper tests the same rules again and again. Error spotting is where those rules show up most. Here are the ones I saw most, each with a sentence you can remember.

## What this topic covers

A method first: **find the subject and the verb of every sentence before anything else.** Most errors are about how they agree, or about the small words around them.

### Subject-verb agreement

1. **Each / every / either / neither + singular verb.**
   ✗ Each of the boys *have* a book. ✓ Each of the boys **has** a book.
2. **"One of the" + plural noun + singular verb.**
   ✗ One of my friends *are* a doctor. ✓ One of my friends **is** a doctor.
3. **Neither... nor / either... or: the verb agrees with the nearer subject.**
   ✓ Neither the teacher nor the students **were** present.
4. **"Along with", "as well as", "with" don't change the subject.**
   ✓ The captain, along with his players, **was** honoured.

### Nouns and articles

5. **Uncountable nouns have no plural:** information, advice, furniture, luggage, equipment.
   ✗ He gave me many *advices*. ✓ He gave me **much advice / a piece of advice**.
6. **"A" or "an" goes by sound, not spelling.**
   ✓ **an** hour, **an** MLA, **an** honest man; **a** university, **a** European, **a** one-day match.

### Prepositions

7. **No preposition after:** discuss, comprise, reach, enter, order, resemble.
   ✗ We discussed *about* the plan. ✓ We **discussed** the plan.
8. **Fixed pairs:** married **to**, senior **to**, prefer **to**, superior **to**, different **from**, accused **of**, angry **with** a person, angry **at** a thing.
   ✗ He is senior *than* me. ✓ He is senior **to** me.

### Tenses

9. **Since + point of time, for + period of time,** with present perfect (continuous).
   ✓ I have lived here **since 2020** / **for six years**.
10. **No future tense after "when / if / before / until" (time and condition clauses).**
    ✗ I will call you when he *will come*. ✓ I will call you when he **comes**.
11. **"Hardly / scarcely ... when"; "no sooner ... than".**
    ✓ No sooner had he left **than** it started raining. ✓ Hardly had he left **when** it started raining.
12. **Imaginary wish or condition: "were" for every subject.**
    ✓ If I **were** you, I would apologise.

### Pronouns and conjunctions

13. **After a preposition, use the object form.**
    ✗ Between you and *I*. ✓ Between you and **me**.
14. **"Lest" is followed by "should", never by "not".**
    ✓ Work hard lest you **should** fail.
15. **"Though ... yet", "not only ... but also", "both ... and" (never "both ... as well as").**
    ✓ He is **not only** intelligent **but also** hardworking.

## How it is asked in the exam

The sentence is split into three or four parts (A, B, C, D), with one option for "No error". Read the whole sentence once, find the subject and verb, then check the small words: articles, prepositions and the second half of conjunction pairs.

## Points to remember

- **Don't hunt for big words.** Errors are almost always in small words.
- "**No error**" is a real answer. If you've checked subject, verb, article, preposition and tense and found nothing, choose it.
- **Collective nouns** (team, committee, jury) take a singular verb when acting as one unit.

## Common mistakes

- Choosing the part with the hard word because it "sounds wrong".
- Matching the verb with the noun nearest to it instead of the real subject.
- Using "than" after senior, junior, superior, inferior, prefer.

## Practise straight after reading

Write these fifteen rules in your own notebook, one example each. Solve error-spotting questions from previous Punjab papers and from the English quizzes on this site. Every time you miss one, write which rule it was. That rule list is what you revise after 1, 7 and 30 days.
