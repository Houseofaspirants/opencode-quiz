Polity gave a lot of questions in my papers, and Parliament is the chapter where students lose marks by mixing up two sets of numbers. The fix is simple: never learn Parliament and the State Legislature separately. Learn them **side by side** in one table, the way they are laid out below.

## What this topic covers

### Composition

- **Parliament (Article 79):** the **President + Lok Sabha + Rajya Sabha**.
- **State Legislature (Article 168):** the **Governor + Legislative Assembly** (+ **Legislative Council** where it exists).

### Side by side

| Point | Parliament | State Legislature |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Lower house | Lok Sabha | Vidhan Sabha (Legislative Assembly) |
| Upper house | Rajya Sabha | Vidhan Parishad (Legislative Council), only in some states |
| Max. strength, lower house | **550** (543 at present) | **500**, min. **60** (Article 170) |
| Max. strength, upper house | **250** (238 elected + 12 nominated) | Not more than **1/3** of the Assembly, min. **40** |
| Minimum age, lower house | **25** | **25** |
| Minimum age, upper house | **30** | **30** |
| Term, lower house | **5 years** (Article 83) | **5 years** (Article 172) |
| Upper house | Permanent; 1/3 retire every 2 years; member's term 6 years | Same pattern |
| Presiding officer, lower house | **Speaker** | Speaker |
| Presiding officer, upper house | **Vice-President** (ex-officio Chairman) | Chairman, elected by the Council |
| Money bill | Article **110**; only in Lok Sabha; Rajya Sabha can hold it **14 days** | Article **198**; only in Assembly; Council can hold it **14 days** |
| Joint sitting | Yes, Article **108**, presided by the **Speaker** | **No joint sitting** |
| Ordinance | **President, Article 123** | **Governor, Article 213** |
| Quorum | **1/10** of total members | 1/10 or **10 members**, whichever is more |

### Upper houses in the states

- **Article 169:** Parliament creates or abolishes a Legislative Council, on a resolution passed by the state Assembly by a **special majority**.
- Six states have a Council today: **Andhra Pradesh, Bihar, Karnataka, Maharashtra, Telangana and Uttar Pradesh**.
- **Punjab abolished its Legislative Council** with effect from **1 January 1970**. Punjab is **unicameral**.

### Punjab's numbers

- **Vidhan Sabha:** **117** seats.
- **Lok Sabha:** **13** seats.
- **Rajya Sabha:** **7** seats.

### Other facts the paper likes

- The **104th Amendment (2019)** ended the nomination of two Anglo-Indian members to the Lok Sabha.
- The **Rajya Sabha** cannot be dissolved.
- The **Speaker** decides whether a bill is a money bill, and that decision is final.
- **Zero Hour** is an Indian innovation; it isn't mentioned in the Constitution.

## How it is asked in the exam

- "Who presides over a joint sitting?" (Speaker.)
- "Minimum age to be a Rajya Sabha member?" (30.)
- "Money bills are defined in which article?" (110.)
- "Which state has a Legislative Council?" (Options with Punjab are a trap.)
- "Number of Lok Sabha seats from Punjab?" (13.)

## Points to remember

- **Two 14-day rules:** Rajya Sabha on money bills, and Legislative Council on money bills.
- **Joint sitting exists only for Parliament,** and never for money bills or constitutional amendment bills.
- **Article 123 and Article 213** are the same power, one for the President and one for the Governor.

## Common mistakes

- Writing **552** as the Lok Sabha maximum. After the 104th Amendment it is **550**.
- Making the Vice-President a **member** of the Rajya Sabha. He is its Chairman, not a member.
- Assuming Punjab has a Legislative Council because it once did.
