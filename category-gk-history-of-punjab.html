Quick answer

History of Punjab is the state-specific half of the history paper: the Indus valley, the Sikh period from the Gurus to the Maharaja, the Anglo-Sikh wars and Partition. Learn it as one continuous sequence, because this lane repeats heavily in Punjab Police and PSSSB papers.

About this category

All about History of Punjab

Where this lane sits inside General Knowledge.

History of Punjab is what makes a state exam a state exam, and it carries more weight here than anywhere else in the history block. The Indus civilization, the rise of the Sikh Gurus, the Misl period, Maharaja Ranjit Singh's reign, the two Anglo-Sikh wars and the partition of 1947 form one continuous line, and examiners work almost entirely inside it.

Because the same thread runs from Ancient through Modern History, revise it once as a Punjab timeline and attach the national events to it. Attempt the sets untimed to find the gaps, write down every date that costs you twice, and read that one file the night before the paper instead of rereading the lane.

This lane sits inside General Knowledge (15 lanes in total), so it stays narrow on purpose: 12 topic sets and 218 questions currently publish under History of Punjab. Every set is untimed; each question carries four options and explains why the right answer is right.

Run a set once to find the gaps, then retake it a few days later. The questions you miss twice are your revision list - keep them to one line each and revise the file the night before the paper.
