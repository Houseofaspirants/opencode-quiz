Question bank

Fundamental Rights and Writs - Part 3: all 25 questions

Read the question, pick an option, then open the answer.

  1. Which writ is issued to enforce the right of individual liberty against unlawful detention?

    1. Mandamus
    2. Quo-Warranto
    3. Habeas Corpus
    4. Certiorari
    Show answer

    Answer: (C) Habeas Corpus

    Explanation: Habeas Corpus enforces individual liberty against unlawful detention; the detained person must be produced before the court.

  2. The literal meaning of 'Mandamus' is:

    1. To be certified
    2. We command
    3. To have the body of
    4. To forbid
    Show answer

    Answer: (B) We command

    Explanation: Mandamus means 'we command'.

  3. Which writ is issued to a public official who has failed or refused to perform his duty?

    1. Habeas Corpus
    2. Mandamus
    3. Quo-Warranto
    4. Prohibition
    Show answer

    Answer: (B) Mandamus

    Explanation: Mandamus is issued to a public official who has failed or refused to perform his duty, ordering him to resume his work.

  4. The literal meaning of the writ 'Prohibition' is:

    1. We command
    2. By what warrant
    3. To forbid
    4. To be informed
    Show answer

    Answer: (C) To forbid

    Explanation: Prohibition means 'to forbid'.

  5. Which writ is issued by a higher court to a lower court to prevent it from exceeding its jurisdiction?

    1. Habeas Corpus
    2. Mandamus
    3. Quo-Warranto
    4. Prohibition
    Show answer

    Answer: (D) Prohibition

    Explanation: Prohibition is issued by a higher court to a lower court to prevent it from exceeding its jurisdiction or usurping one it does not have.

  6. The literal meaning of 'Certiorari' is:

    1. To be certified or to be informed
    2. By what authority
    3. We command
    4. To forbid
    Show answer

    Answer: (A) To be certified or to be informed

    Explanation: Certiorari means 'to be certified' or 'to be informed'.

  7. Which writ is issued by a higher court to transfer a pending case from a lower court to itself or to quash its order?

    1. Prohibition
    2. Quo-Warranto
    3. Mandamus
    4. Certiorari
    Show answer

    Answer: (D) Certiorari

    Explanation: Certiorari is issued to transfer a pending case to the higher court or to quash the lower court's order.

  8. The literal meaning of 'Quo-Warranto' is:

    1. By what authority or warrant
    2. We command
    3. To forbid
    4. To have the body of
    Show answer

    Answer: (A) By what authority or warrant

    Explanation: Quo-Warranto means 'by what authority or warrant'.

  9. Which writ is issued to prevent illegal usurpation of a public office by a person?

    1. Prohibition
    2. Mandamus
    3. Certiorari
    4. Quo-Warranto
    Show answer

    Answer: (D) Quo-Warranto

    Explanation: Quo-Warranto prevents illegal usurpation of a public office; the court enquires into the legality of the person's claim.

  10. Writs in the Indian Constitution have been borrowed from:

    1. The US Constitution
    2. The Government of India Act, 1935
    3. English law, where they are called 'prerogative writs'
    4. The Irish Constitution
    Show answer

    Answer: (C) English law, where they are called 'prerogative writs'

    Explanation: Writs are borrowed from English law, where they are known as 'prerogative writs'.

  11. Whose writ jurisdiction is wider — and why?

    1. High Court's, because it can issue writs for Fundamental Rights and for any other purpose
    2. Both are exactly equal
    3. Supreme Court's, because it can issue writs for any purpose
    4. Supreme Court's, because it covers all of India
    Show answer

    Answer: (A) High Court's, because it can issue writs for Fundamental Rights and for any other purpose

    Explanation: The High Court (Art. 226) can issue writs for Fundamental Rights and 'for any other purpose'; the SC only for Fundamental Rights.

  12. Consider the following statements:1.The Supreme Court may refuse to exercise its writ jurisdiction under Article 32.2.The remedy under Article 226 is discretionary.Which of the statements given above is/are correct?

    1. 1 only
    2. 2 only
    3. Both 1 and 2
    4. Neither 1 nor 2
    Show answer

    Answer: (B) 2 only

    Explanation: Remedy under Article 32 is itself a Fundamental Right, so the SC may not refuse. The High Court's remedy under Article 226 is discretionary.

  13. Regarding territorial reach of writs, the Supreme Court can issue writs:

    1. Against a person or government throughout India
    2. Only against central government
    3. Only within Delhi
    4. Only within the state where the petitioner lives
    Show answer

    Answer: (A) Against a person or government throughout India

    Explanation: The SC can issue writs throughout India; a High Court only within its territorial jurisdiction.

  14. Which writ can be issued ONLY against judicial or quasi-judicial bodies (courts/tribunals)?

    1. Mandamus
    2. Quo-Warranto
    3. Prohibition
    4. Habeas Corpus
    Show answer

    Answer: (C) Prohibition

    Explanation: Prohibition is issued only against courts/tribunals.

  15. Match the writs with their purpose:A.Habeas Corpus – 1. Public officeB.Mandamus – 2. Unlawful detentionC.Certiorari – 3. Public official not performing dutyD.Quo-Warranto – 4. Quash order of lower court

    1. A-2, B-3, C-4, D-1
    2. A-2, B-3, C-1, D-4
    3. A-2, B-4, C-3, D-1
    4. A-3, B-2, C-4, D-1
    Show answer

    Answer: (A) A-2, B-3, C-4, D-1

    Explanation: Habeas Corpus – detention; Mandamus – duty; Certiorari – quash/transfer; Quo-Warranto – public office.

  16. Mandamus can be issued against all of the following EXCEPT:

    1. An inferior court or tribunal
    2. A public official
    3. A public corporation
    4. The President of India acting in personal capacity
    Show answer

    Answer: (D) The President of India acting in personal capacity

    Explanation: Mandamus can be issued against public officials, public bodies, corporations, inferior courts, tribunals or government. It does not lie against the President in personal capacity.

  17. The Supreme Court is regarded as the ________ of the Fundamental Rights of citizens.

    1. Amending authority
    2. Author
    3. Suspending authority
    4. Defender (guarantor)
    Show answer

    Answer: (D) Defender (guarantor)

    Explanation: The Supreme Court is the defender of the Fundamental Rights of citizens and has original and wide powers for this purpose.

  18. Which of the following statements about Fundamental Rights is INCORRECT?

    1. They are justiciable
    2. They apply only to citizens and never to foreigners
    3. They can be restricted under martial law
    4. They are enshrined in Part III
    Show answer

    Answer: (B) They apply only to citizens and never to foreigners

    Explanation: Some Fundamental Rights (e.g., Articles 14, 20, 21) are available to foreigners too; only Articles 15, 16, 19, 29, 30 are restricted to citizens.

  19. Fundamental Rights apply without discrimination on the basis of:

    1. Income only
    2. Race, religion, gender, etc.
    3. Citizenship only
    4. Educational qualification
    Show answer

    Answer: (B) Race, religion, gender, etc.

    Explanation: Fundamental Rights apply without discrimination on the basis of race, religion, gender, etc.

  20. Which Article contains the right to 'freedom of residence'?

    1. Article 19
    2. Article 21
    3. Article 15
    4. Article 22
    Show answer

    Answer: (A) Article 19

    Explanation: Freedom of residence is one of the six freedoms under Article 19.

  21. Which Fundamental Right includes the writs of habeas corpus, mandamus, prohibition, certiorari and quo-warranto?

    1. Right against Exploitation
    2. Right to Constitutional Remedies
    3. Right to Equality
    4. Right to Freedom
    Show answer

    Answer: (B) Right to Constitutional Remedies

    Explanation: Article 32 (Right to Constitutional Remedies) includes the five writs.

  22. Originally, how many Fundamental Rights were there in the Constitution?

    1. Six
    2. Five
    3. Seven
    4. Eight
    Show answer

    Answer: (C) Seven

    Explanation: Originally there were seven Fundamental Rights; the Right to Property was later removed, leaving six.

  23. The Right to Property, before its deletion, was contained in which Article?

    1. Article 19(1)(g)
    2. Article 30
    3. Article 31
    4. Article 300-A
    Show answer

    Answer: (C) Article 31

    Explanation: The Right to Property was under Article 31 before the 44th Amendment (1978).

  24. When is the writ of Habeas Corpus typically issued?

    1. When a lower court exceeds jurisdiction
    2. When a public servant refuses to do his duty
    3. When a person holds a public office illegally
    4. When a person is arrested or detained without any lawful reason
    Show answer

    Answer: (D) When a person is arrested or detained without any lawful reason

    Explanation: Habeas Corpus is issued when a person is arrested without any reason; the detaining authority must produce the person before the court.

  25. Which of the following is NOT covered under the term 'State' in Article 12?

    1. ONGC
    2. Parliament of India
    3. A municipal corporation
    4. A purely private company not acting as an instrument of the State
    Show answer

    Answer: (D) A purely private company not acting as an instrument of the State

    Explanation: A private body is 'State' only if it works as an instrument of the State; Parliament, local bodies and authorities like ONGC are included.
