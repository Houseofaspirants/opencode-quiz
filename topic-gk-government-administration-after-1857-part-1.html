Government Administration After 1857 - Part 1: all 25 questions

Read the question, pick an option, then open the answer.

  1. After the Revolt of 1857, which authority took over the government of India from the East India Company?

    1. The British cabinet
    2. The British Crown
    3. The Board of Control
    4. The Company’s Directors
    Show answer

    Answer: (B) The British Crown

    Explanation: The British Crown took over the government of India after the Revolt of 1857. The cabinet was a ministerial body, whereas the Board of Control and the Company’s Directors belonged to the earlier governing arrangement. The transfer provides the background to the standard central, provincial and local administrative changes.

  2. Match the place with the office through which India was governed after 1858.1.England2.Indiaa.Viceroyb.Secretary of State for India

    1. 1-a, 2-b
    2. 1-b, 2-a
    3. 1-a, 2-a
    4. 1-b, 2-b
    Show answer

    Answer: (B) 1-b, 2-a

    Explanation: England is associated with the Secretary of State for India, so 1-b is correct. India is associated with the Viceroy, so 2-a is correct. The other codes either reverse these locations or assign the same office to both settings. This distinction separates the administration in England from the government operating in India.

  3. Before the post-1857 arrangement, which pair held the power to govern India?

    1. The British cabinet and provincial councils
    2. The Crown and local bodies
    3. The Company’s Directors and the Board of Control
    4. Presidency Governors and Chief Commissioners
    Show answer

    Answer: (C) The Company’s Directors and the Board of Control

    Explanation: The earlier governing power was held by the Company’s Directors and the Board of Control. The cabinet and provincial councils are not identified as that earlier pair. Nor do standard accounts give this role to the Crown with local bodies or to provincial administrators acting together. The standard account uses this earlier arrangement to explain the subsequent administrative reorganisation.

  4. The Secretary of State for India was a member of which body?

    1. The Viceroy’s Executive Council
    2. A presidency’s Executive Council
    3. A provincial legislative council
    4. The British cabinet
    Show answer

    Answer: (D) The British cabinet

    Explanation: The Secretary of State for India was a member of the British cabinet. The Viceroy’s Executive Council belonged to the administration in India. A presidency’s Executive Council and a provincial legislative council operated at the provincial level. The cabinet connection placed this office within the British ministerial structure.

  5. How many members were in the council assisting the Secretary of State for India?

    1. 15
    2. 16
    3. 18
    4. 20
    Show answer

    Answer: (A) 15

    Explanation: The council assisting the Secretary of State had 15 members. The figures 16, 18 and 20 do not match the strength. This was the council attached to the Secretary of State, rather than a provincial council. Its size should therefore be associated with the correct administrative office.

  6. Consider the following statements about parliamentary control after 1857.1.The Secretary of State for India was answerable to the British Parliament.2.Ultimate power over India remained with the British Parliament.Which of the above is/are correct?

    1. 1 only
    2. 2 only
    3. Both 1 and 2
    4. Neither 1 nor 2
    Show answer

    Answer: (C) Both 1 and 2

    Explanation: Statement 1 is correct because the Secretary of State was answerable to the British Parliament. Statement 2 is also correct because Parliament retained ultimate power over India. The options accepting only one statement omit a supported fact, while the option rejecting both contradicts standard accounts. Together, the statements distinguish ministerial accountability from ultimate parliamentary authority.

  7. Assertion (A): The council assisting the Secretary of State did not have the final decision-making role.Reason (R): All initiatives and final decisions rested with the Secretary of State, and the council was only advisory.

    1. Both A and R are true and R is the correct explanation of A
    2. Both A and R are true but R is not the correct explanation of A
    3. A is true but R is false
    4. A is false but R is true
    Show answer

    Answer: (A) Both A and R are true and R is the correct explanation of A

    Explanation: Assertion A is true: the council did not hold the final decision-making role. Reason R is also true: initiatives and final decisions belonged to the Secretary of State, while the council only advised. R directly explains A, so treating the two statements as unrelated is incorrect. This distinction is essential when comparing consultation with decision-making authority.

  8. Which statement about the Governor-General’s new title of Viceroy is NOT correct?

    1. The title increased the office-holder’s prestige
    2. The title necessarily increased the office-holder’s authority
    3. The Governor-General continued to run the government in India
    4. The new title was Viceroy
    Show answer

    Answer: (B) The title necessarily increased the office-holder’s authority

    Explanation: The new title increased prestige, if not authority; they do not establish a necessary increase in authority. They explicitly state that the government in India continued to be run by the Governor-General. They also identify Viceroy as the new title. The exam-relevant distinction here is between enhanced prestige and enhanced power.

  9. Which body assisted the Viceroy in the executive administration?

    1. The Board of Control
    2. The Company’s Directors
    3. The Executive Council
    4. The British cabinet
    Show answer

    Answer: (C) The Executive Council

    Explanation: The Viceroy was assisted by an Executive Council. The Board of Control and the Company’s Directors are associated with the earlier governing arrangement. The British cabinet belonged to the administration in Britain. The word executive helps distinguish this council’s administrative role from legislative work.

  10. Consider the following roles of members of the Viceroy’s Executive Council.1.Heads of various departments2.Official advisers to the ViceroyWhich of these roles did they perform?

    1. 1 only
    2. 2 only
    3. Neither 1 nor 2
    4. Both 1 and 2
    Show answer

    Answer: (D) Both 1 and 2

    Explanation: Standard accounts identify Executive Council members as heads of various departments, so role 1 is correct. They also acted as the Viceroy’s official advisers, so role 2 is correct. Neither role excludes the other, and rejecting both is inconsistent with standard accounts. Their position therefore combined departmental responsibility with official advice.

  11. For making laws, how many additional members could the Viceroy add?

    1. 2 to 4
    2. 6 to 12
    3. 4 to 6
    4. 12 to 15
    Show answer

    Answer: (B) 6 to 12

    Explanation: The Viceroy could add six to twelve additional members for law-making. The ranges two to four and four to six are below the stated range. Twelve to fifteen is also incorrect because it changes the upper limit. The figure refers specifically to additional members for legislative work.

  12. Assertion (A): In a group of ten additional law-making members, at least five had to be non-officials.Reason (R): At least half of the additional law-making members had to be non-officials.

    1. Both A and R are true and R is the correct explanation of A
    2. Both A and R are true but R is not the correct explanation of A
    3. A is true but R is false
    4. A is false but R is true
    Show answer

    Answer: (A) Both A and R are true and R is the correct explanation of A

    Explanation: Assertion A correctly applies the minimum share to a group of ten. Reason R correctly states that at least half of the additional members had to be non-officials. Half of ten is five, so R directly explains A. The rule states a minimum share rather than requiring exactly half in every case.

  13. Which description correctly identifies the non-official additional members’ possible backgrounds?

    1. They had to be Indian only
    2. They had to be English only
    3. They could be Indian or English
    4. They had to be Company Directors only
    Show answer

    Answer: (C) They could be Indian or English

    Explanation: Standard accounts explicitly allow the non-official additional members to be Indian or English. Restricting them to Indians alone or English people alone excludes a category allowed by standard accounts. Requiring them to be Company Directors introduces a condition that standard accounts do not give. Non-official status should therefore not be confused with an exclusively Indian background.

  14. Arrange the following developments from earliest to latest.1.Legislative powers were taken away from Madras and Bombay.2.Pitt’s India Act introduced the dual system.3.The Indian Councils Act restored legislative powers to Madras and Bombay.

    1. 1-2-3
    2. 2-1-3
    3. 2-3-1
    4. 3-1-2
    Show answer

    Answer: (B) 2-1-3

    Explanation: Pitt’s India Act introduced the dual system in 1784, placing event 2 first. The legislative powers of Madras and Bombay were taken away in 1833, placing event 1 next. The Indian Councils Act restored those powers in 1861, placing event 3 last. Thus 2-1-3 is the only correct sequence; standard accounts also state that the post-1857 arrangement ended the dual system.

  15. Consider the following statements about the subsequent spread of legislative councils.1.Legislative councils were later established in other provinces too.2.Legislative councils remained permanently confined to the original provinces.Which of the above is/are correct?

    1. 2 only
    2. Both 1 and 2
    3. Neither 1 nor 2
    4. 1 only
    Show answer

    Answer: (D) 1 only

    Explanation: Statement 1 is correct: after the Indian Councils Act, 1861, legislative councils were later set up in other provinces as well (for example Bengal in 1862). Statement 2 is therefore incorrect, so only statement 1 is correct.

  16. Which statement about the three presidencies is NOT correct?

    1. Bombay was one of the three presidencies
    2. Madras was one of the three presidencies
    3. The three presidencies had fewer rights and powers than other provinces
    4. Calcutta (Bengal) was one of the three presidencies
    Show answer

    Answer: (C) The three presidencies had fewer rights and powers than other provinces

    Explanation: The three presidencies had more rights and powers than the other provinces, so the claim of fewer powers is incorrect. Bombay is explicitly included in the group. Madras and Calcutta (Bengal) are also explicitly included. The comparison shows that provincial units did not all enjoy an identical administrative position.

  17. Match the type of province with its administrative head or heads.1.Presidency2.Other provincea.Lieutenant Governor or Chief Commissionerb.Governor

    1. 1-a, 2-b
    2. 1-a, 2-a
    3. 1-b, 2-b
    4. 1-b, 2-a
    Show answer

    Answer: (D) 1-b, 2-a

    Explanation: A presidency had a Governor as its head, giving the match 1-b. Other provinces were administered by Lieutenant Governors and Chief Commissioners, giving 2-a. The remaining codes swap these arrangements or wrongly use the same category of head for both. Standard accounts organise provincial administration by the type of province.

  18. How many members were in a presidency Governor’s Executive Council?

    1. 2
    2. 3
    3. 4
    4. 5
    Show answer

    Answer: (B) 3

    Explanation: The provincial administration list specifies an Executive Council of three for a presidency Governor. Two, four and five do not match the stated strength. The figure belongs to the Governor’s provincial Executive Council. It should be read in its provincial context rather than transferred to a different council.

  19. Match the provincial office with its appointing authority.1.Presidency Governor2.Lieutenant Governor or Chief Commissioner of another provincea.Governor-Generalb.Crown

    1. 1-a, 2-b
    2. 1-a, 2-a
    3. 1-b, 2-a
    4. 1-b, 2-b
    Show answer

    Answer: (C) 1-b, 2-a

    Explanation: The Crown appointed presidency Governors, so 1-b is correct. The Governor-General appointed Lieutenant Governors and Chief Commissioners of other provinces, so 2-a is correct. The other options reverse the authorities or incorrectly apply one appointing authority to both categories. The appointing authority varied with the category of provincial administration.

  20. Match the local self-government resolution with its year.1.Mayo’s Resolution2.Ripon’s Resolutiona.1882b.1870

    1. 1-a, 2-b
    2. 1-a, 2-a
    3. 1-b, 2-b
    4. 1-b, 2-a
    Show answer

    Answer: (D) 1-b, 2-a

    Explanation: Mayo’s Resolution belongs to 1870, so 1-b is correct. Ripon’s Resolution belongs to 1882, so 2-a is correct. The other codes reverse the years or assign the same year to both resolutions. The two resolutions mark successive stages in the standard account of local self-government.

  21. Which of the following groups contains only the fields for which Mayo’s Resolution (1870) transferred funds to local management?

    1. Education, sanitation, medical relief and local public works
    2. Education, sanitation, medical relief and provincial legislation
    3. Education, sanitation, departmental advice and local public works
    4. Education, legislative councils, medical relief and local public works
    Show answer

    Answer: (A) Education, sanitation, medical relief and local public works

    Explanation: The resolution expressly lists education, sanitation, medical relief and local public works. Provincial legislation is not one of the funding fields in that list. Departmental advice and legislative councils belong to other administrative discussions, rather than this list of local services. The resolution thus links local involvement to the management of funds devoted to specified public services.

  22. Assertion (A): Mayo’s Resolution treated local involvement as necessary for successful management of the specified service funds.Reason (R): It stressed local interest, supervision and care as necessary for such success.

    1. Both A and R are true and R is the correct explanation of A
    2. Both A and R are true but R is not the correct explanation of A
    3. A is true but R is false
    4. A is false but R is true
    Show answer

    Answer: (A) Both A and R are true and R is the correct explanation of A

    Explanation: Assertion A is true because the resolution regards local involvement as necessary for successful fund management. Reason R is true because the specified requirements are local interest, supervision and care. These requirements directly explain the importance assigned to local involvement, so the unrelated-explanation option is wrong. The emphasis concerns how funds are managed, rather than merely naming the services receiving funds.

  23. Consider the following statements about financial decentralisation.1.Mayo’s government had begun applying the principle to the provinces.2.Ripon’s government wanted provincial governments to apply the same principle to local bodies.Which of the above is/are correct?

    1. 1 only
    2. 2 only
    3. Neither 1 nor 2
    4. Both 1 and 2
    Show answer

    Answer: (D) Both 1 and 2

    Explanation: Statement 1 is correct: Mayo’s government had begun applying financial decentralisation to the provinces. Statement 2 is correct: Ripon’s government wanted provincial governments to extend that principle to local bodies. Accepting only one statement misses the continuity, while rejecting both contradicts standard accounts. The important relationship is the extension of a provincial financial principle to the local level.

  24. Who is called the “Father of Local Self-Government in India”?

    1. Lord Ripon
    2. Lord Dalhousie
    3. Lord Wellesley
    4. Lord Mayo
    Show answer

    Answer: (A) Lord Ripon

    Explanation: Standard accounts call Lord Ripon the Father of Local Self-Government in India for his contributions. Lord Mayo is discussed as another contributor to the development of local self-government, but is not assigned this title. Neither Lord Dalhousie nor Lord Wellesley is given this title. The title is an attribution of significance, rather than the name of a formal administrative office.

  25. Which measure is regarded as the first step towards local self-government in India?

    1. Mayo’s Resolution
    2. Ripon’s Resolution
    3. Pitt’s India Act
    4. The Indian Councils Act
    Show answer

    Answer: (A) Mayo’s Resolution

    Explanation: Lord Mayo’s Resolution of 1870 on financial decentralisation is regarded as the first step towards local self-government. Ripon’s Resolution of 1882 came later and earned Ripon the title “Father of Local Self-Government”. Pitt’s India Act (1784) created the dual system, and the Indian Councils Act (1861) dealt with legislative councils.
