Baba Banda Singh Bahadur and the Dal Khalsa - Part 2: all 24 questions

Read the question, pick an option, then open the answer.

  1. At which place was Banda Singh Bahadur born?

    1. Amritsar
    2. Sirhind
    3. Rajouri
    4. Kahnuwan
    Show answer

    Answer: (C) Rajouri

    Explanation: The notes identify Rajouri as the birthplace of Banda Singh Bahadur. Amritsar hosted the Sarbat Khalsa meetings, Sirhind was captured after Chappar Chiri, and Kahnuwan was the site of the Chhota Ghallughara. Rajouri is presently identified in the notes with Jammu and Kashmir.

  2. Which of the following place-region associations is NOT correct according to the notes?

    1. Rajouri—Punjab
    2. Kup-Rahira—near Malerkotla
    3. Kahnuwan—near Gurdaspur
    4. Rajouri—Jammu and Kashmir
    Show answer

    Answer: (A) Rajouri—Punjab

    Explanation: Rajouri is associated with Jammu and Kashmir in the notes, so its pairing with Punjab is incorrect. Kup-Rahira is described as near Malerkotla, while Kahnuwan is described as near Gurdaspur. Rajouri was the birthplace of Banda Singh Bahadur.

  3. Assertion (A): Banda Singh's meeting with Guru Gobind Singh Ji proved to be a turning point in Sikh history. Reason (R): Banda Singh was deeply impressed by the Guru's teachings and philosophy. Choose the correct answer.

    1. Both A and R are true and R is the correct explanation of A
    2. Both A and R are true but R is not the correct explanation of A
    3. A is true but R is false
    4. A is false but R is true
    Show answer

    Answer: (A) Both A and R are true and R is the correct explanation of A

    Explanation: Both the assertion and reason are true. The notes describe the meeting as a turning point and directly connect it with Banda Singh being deeply impressed by the Guru's teachings and philosophy. Therefore, the reason correctly explains the assertion.

  4. Whose martyrdom did Banda Singh solemnly vow to avenge?

    1. Bhai Mani Singh
    2. The sons of Guru Gobind Singh Ji
    3. Ajai Singh
    4. Jassa Singh Ahluwalia
    Show answer

    Answer: (B) The sons of Guru Gobind Singh Ji

    Explanation: Banda Singh vowed to avenge the martyrdom of the sons of Guru Gobind Singh Ji. Bhai Mani Singh's execution belongs to the later background of the Chhota Ghallughara, and Ajai Singh was Banda Singh's son. Jassa Singh Ahluwalia led the later Sikh revival.

  5. Who was killed in the Battle of Chappar Chiri?

    1. Wazir Khan
    2. Zakariya Khan
    3. Yahiya Khan
    4. Farrukhsiyar
    Show answer

    Answer: (A) Wazir Khan

    Explanation: Wazir Khan was killed in the Battle of Chappar Chiri. Zakariya Khan was the Governor of Lahore who granted the title of Nawab to Kapur Singh, while Yahiya Khan led Mughal forces during the Chhota Ghallughara. Farrukhsiyar was the Mughal emperor whose forces later captured Banda Singh.

  6. Consider the following statements about Banda Singh's victory at Chappar Chiri: 1. It led to the capture of Sirhind. 2. It fulfilled his oath to Guru Gobind Singh Ji. Which of the statements given above is/are correct?

    1. 1 only
    2. 2 only
    3. Both 1 and 2
    4. Neither 1 nor 2
    Show answer

    Answer: (C) Both 1 and 2

    Explanation: Both statements are correct. The notes say that the Chappar Chiri victory led to the capture of Sirhind and fulfilled Banda Singh's oath to Guru Gobind Singh Ji. Therefore, the option containing both statements is correct.

  7. How is Baba Banda Singh Bahadur characterised in the chapter subtitle?

    1. Sultan-ul-Qaum
    2. The first Sikh ruler
    3. Head Granthi of Harmandir Sahib
    4. Governor of Sirhind
    Show answer

    Answer: (B) The first Sikh ruler

    Explanation: The chapter presents Baba Banda Singh Bahadur as the first Sikh ruler. Sultan-ul-Qaum refers to Jassa Singh Ahluwalia, Head Granthi of Harmandir Sahib refers to Bhai Mani Singh, and Governor of Sirhind refers to Wazir Khan. Each alternative title or office belongs to another person in the notes.

  8. Consider the following statements about the Sarbat Khalsa meetings: 1. One annual meeting was held on Vaisakhi. 2. The other annual meeting was held on Diwali. Which of the statements given above is/are correct?

    1. 1 only
    2. 2 only
    3. Neither 1 nor 2
    4. Both 1 and 2
    Show answer

    Answer: (D) Both 1 and 2

    Explanation: Both statements are correct. The notes state that the Sarbat Khalsa met twice a year, on Vaisakhi and Diwali. Therefore, the option containing both statements is correct.

  9. Nadir Shah was the ruler of which country, as identified in the notes?

    1. Jammu and Kashmir
    2. Persia (Iran)
    3. India
    4. Punjab
    Show answer

    Answer: (B) Persia (Iran)

    Explanation: The notes identify Nadir Shah as the ruler of Persia, or Iran. Jammu and Kashmir is associated with Rajouri, India was the country Nadir Shah invaded, and Punjab lay on his return route. Thus Persia (Iran) is the correct answer.

  10. Match List-I with List-II and select the correct code: List-I 1. Chhota Ghallughara 2. Wadda Ghallughara List-II A. Great Holocaust B. Lesser Holocaust

    1. 1-A, 2-B
    2. 1-A, 2-A
    3. 1-B, 2-B
    4. 1-B, 2-A
    Show answer

    Answer: (D) 1-B, 2-A

    Explanation: Chhota Ghallughara is translated in the notes as the Lesser Holocaust, whereas Wadda Ghallughara is the Great Holocaust. Therefore, 1-B and 2-A is the correct matching. The other codes interchange or duplicate these descriptions.

  11. Where did the Wadda Ghallughara take place?

    1. Kup-Rahira near Malerkotla
    2. Kahnuwan near Gurdaspur
    3. Chappar Chiri
    4. Amritsar
    Show answer

    Answer: (A) Kup-Rahira near Malerkotla

    Explanation: The Wadda Ghallughara occurred at Kup-Rahira near Malerkotla. Kahnuwan near Gurdaspur was the location of the Chhota Ghallughara, while Chappar Chiri is connected with Banda Singh's major victory. Amritsar was the meeting place of the Sarbat Khalsa.

  12. Which title was also used for Sardar Jassa Singh Ahluwalia?

    1. Nawab
    2. Governor of Lahore
    3. Sultan-ul-Qaum
    4. Head Granthi
    Show answer

    Answer: (C) Sultan-ul-Qaum

    Explanation: Sardar Jassa Singh Ahluwalia was also called Sultan-ul-Qaum. Nawab was the title granted to Kapur Singh, while Governor of Lahore refers to Zakariya Khan in the notes. Bhai Mani Singh was the Head Granthi of Harmandir Sahib.

  13. Who was the leader of the Dallewalia Misl?

    1. Dasaundha Singh
    2. Karora Singh
    3. Nand Singh
    4. Gulab Singh
    Show answer

    Answer: (D) Gulab Singh

    Explanation: The Misl table names Gulab Singh as the leader of the Dallewalia Misl. Dasaundha Singh led the Nishanwalia Misl, Karora Singh led the Karorsinghia Misl, and Nand Singh is listed with the Ramgarhia Misl. Each name comes from the same leadership table in the notes.

  14. Consider the following statements about the Sukerchakia Misl: 1. Charat Singh is listed as one of its leaders. 2. Ranjit Singh is also listed as one of its leaders. Which of the statements given above is/are correct?

    1. 1 only
    2. 2 only
    3. Neither 1 nor 2
    4. Both 1 and 2
    Show answer

    Answer: (D) Both 1 and 2

    Explanation: Both statements are correct. The table lists Charat Singh and Ranjit Singh with the Sukerchakia Misl. Therefore, the fourth option correctly includes both leaders.

  15. Baba Deep Singh led which Misl?

    1. Nakai Misl
    2. Ramgarhia Misl
    3. Shaheedan Misl
    4. Bhangi Misl
    Show answer

    Answer: (C) Shaheedan Misl

    Explanation: The notes pair Baba Deep Singh with the Shaheedan Misl. Hira Singh Nakai is paired with the Nakai Misl, while Nand Singh and Jassa Singh are listed with the Ramgarhia Misl. Chhajja Singh and Hari Singh are associated with the Bhangi Misl.

  16. Consider the following statements about the Ramgarhia Misl: 1. Nand Singh is listed as one of its leaders. 2. Jassa Singh is also listed as one of its leaders. Which of the statements given above is/are correct?

    1. 1 only
    2. Both 1 and 2
    3. 2 only
    4. Neither 1 nor 2
    Show answer

    Answer: (B) Both 1 and 2

    Explanation: Both statements are correct according to the Misl table. The Ramgarhia Misl is paired with Nand Singh and Jassa Singh. Consequently, the option including both statements is correct.

  17. Who led the Nishanwalia Misl?

    1. Dasaundha Singh
    2. Chaudhari Phul
    3. Karora Singh
    4. Gulab Singh
    Show answer

    Answer: (A) Dasaundha Singh

    Explanation: Dasaundha Singh is listed as the leader of the Nishanwalia Misl. Chaudhari Phul led the Phulkian Misl, Karora Singh led the Karorsinghia Misl, and Gulab Singh led the Dallewalia Misl. These associations are stated in the Misl table.

  18. Who was associated with the leadership of the Phulkian Misl?

    1. Hira Singh Nakai
    2. Jai Singh Kanhaiya
    3. Chaudhari Phul
    4. Baba Deep Singh
    Show answer

    Answer: (C) Chaudhari Phul

    Explanation: Chaudhari Phul is named as the leader of the Phulkian Misl. Hira Singh Nakai, Jai Singh Kanhaiya, and Baba Deep Singh are associated with the Nakai, Kanhaiya, and Shaheedan Misls respectively. The notes further distinguish the Phulkian Misl as independent.

  19. Consider the following statements: 1. Faizullapuria Misl was also called Singhpuria Misl. 2. Nawab Kapur Singh was its leader. Which of the statements given above is/are correct?

    1. 1 only
    2. 2 only
    3. Neither 1 nor 2
    4. Both 1 and 2
    Show answer

    Answer: (D) Both 1 and 2

    Explanation: Both statements are correct. The table gives Faizullapuria with Singhpuria in parentheses and names Nawab Kapur Singh as its leader. Therefore, the fourth option correctly includes both statements.

  20. Karora Singh was the leader of which Misl?

    1. Dallewalia Misl
    2. Karorsinghia Misl
    3. Nishanwalia Misl
    4. Faizullapuria Misl
    Show answer

    Answer: (B) Karorsinghia Misl

    Explanation: Karora Singh is paired with the Karorsinghia Misl in the notes. Gulab Singh led the Dallewalia Misl, Dasaundha Singh led the Nishanwalia Misl, and Nawab Kapur Singh led the Faizullapuria or Singhpuria Misl. The similarity between the leader's name and the Misl name is useful for recall.

  21. Arrange the following events in chronological order: 1. Execution of Banda Singh Bahadur. 2. Wadda Ghallughara. 3. Death of Jassa Singh Ahluwalia.

    1. 2-1-3
    2. 1-3-2
    3. 1-2-3
    4. 3-2-1
    Show answer

    Answer: (C) 1-2-3

    Explanation: Banda Singh Bahadur was executed in 1716, the Wadda Ghallughara occurred in 1762, and Jassa Singh Ahluwalia died in 1783. Thus the correct chronological order is 1-2-3. These events span the transition from Banda Singh's martyrdom to the later weakening of the Dal Khalsa.

  22. Consider the following statements about the period after Jassa Singh Ahluwalia's death: 1. The Dal Khalsa grew weaker. 2. This weakening paved the way for the rise of Maharaja Ranjit Singh. Which of the statements given above is/are correct?

    1. 1 only
    2. Both 1 and 2
    3. 2 only
    4. Neither 1 nor 2
    Show answer

    Answer: (B) Both 1 and 2

    Explanation: Both statements are correct. The notes say that the Dal Khalsa grew weaker after Jassa Singh Ahluwalia's death and that this decline paved the way for Maharaja Ranjit Singh's rise. Thus the option containing both statements is correct.

  23. On which date was Banda Singh Bahadur executed?

    1. 27 October 1670
    2. 12 May 1710
    3. December 1715
    4. 9 June 1716
    Show answer

    Answer: (D) 9 June 1716

    Explanation: Banda Singh Bahadur was executed on 9 June 1716. The year 1670 relates to his birth, 12 May 1710 to the Battle of Chappar Chiri, and December 1715 to his capture. His execution made him a martyr for the Sikh cause.

  24. How is the Dal Khalsa described in the chapter subtitle?

    1. The Sikh Confederacy
    2. The first Sikh ruler
    3. The Lesser Holocaust
    4. The Great Holocaust
    Show answer

    Answer: (A) The Sikh Confederacy

    Explanation: The chapter subtitle describes the Dal Khalsa as the Sikh Confederacy. The first Sikh ruler describes Banda Singh Bahadur, while the Lesser Holocaust and Great Holocaust refer to the Chhota and Wadda Ghallugharas respectively. Therefore, the first option is correct.
