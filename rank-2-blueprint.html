05 · Mistakes analysis

Where I lost marks

27 of 200 questions did not add to the score — 17 attempted and marked wrong, 10 left blank. Subject by subject, here is what the sheet shows and what the method says should have been done instead.

See the attempt sheet →
General Awareness 15 of 50 lost

Nine blank, six wrong — the largest leak on the paper

What the sheet shows
35 correct, 6 wrong and 9 left blank out of 50. At 70% this was the lowest accuracy on the paper and also the biggest pool of untouched questions.
What should have been done
The method is accuracy over attempts: attempt only once two options can be eliminated, and finish the static half first because it is finite. A blank costs nothing and a wrong answer costs marks, so the nine blanks were the cheap fix.

Source: row 1 of the attempt sheet.

Punjabi 5 of 20 lost

Everything attempted, one attempt in four wrong

What the sheet shows
All 20 attempted, 5 of them wrong and none blank — a 25% error rate on attempts, the highest of any subject, with nothing held back.
What should have been done
Eliminate two options before marking, the same rule as everywhere else and the one this subject did not get. Negative marking was in play, so a 20-question section needed restraint rather than reach.

Source: row 3 of the attempt sheet.

English 4 of 20 lost

One attempt in five came back wrong

What the sheet shows
16 correct, 4 wrong, nothing skipped out of 20. Every question was attempted and 20% of those attempts were wrong.
What should have been done
Grammar is a finite syllabus, so the fix is drilling the rules chapter by chapter until two options can always be eliminated — not re-reading the same chapter.

Source: row 6 of the attempt sheet.

Computer 2 of 30 lost

Small leak, no blanks

What the sheet shows
28 correct, 2 wrong and nothing skipped out of 30 — 93.3%, the second-highest accuracy on the paper.
What should have been done
Computer is finite like the rest of the static half: read once, drilled as facts, tested after every reading. Two misses out of thirty is a revision gap, not a subject problem.

Source: row 5 of the attempt sheet.

Quantitative Aptitude 1 of 30 lost

One question left on the table

What the sheet shows
29 correct and zero wrong out of 30 — the only subject with a perfect attempt record — with a single question left blank.
What should have been done
This is the rule working as intended. The blank was correct behaviour: a question that has not yielded in forty seconds gets marked and left, not wrestled.

Source: row 2 of the attempt sheet.

Reasoning 0 of 50 lost

Nothing lost — the row to copy

What the sheet shows
50 correct, 0 wrong, 0 blank out of 50. The only perfect row on the paper, and the single biggest contributor to the total.
What should have been done
Nothing. This is what the method looks like when it is applied: patterns taught before problems, speed built from repetition, and never hurrying.

Source: row 4 of the attempt sheet.

06 · Preparation strategy

Preparation strategy

Thirteen cards instead of one long read. Open the ones that apply to you — every card is drawn from the method that produced the sheet above, never from a book summary.

Study notes →

Topics

One subject at a time. Read it once properly, then immediately test it chapter by chapter on Practice, then write a one-page revision sheet from memory, then re-read only what the sheet failed to hold. A subject was not considered finished until a revision test on it came back above the target twice in a row. Nothing moved to the next subject on optimism.

Books

The shelf was deliberately small: one standard reference for each subject, plus previous year papers. The second book on a subject you have not finished the first one for is a way of feeling busy without learning anything. Every title, with who should skip it, sits on the Books shelf.

Revision

Revision beats reading. A chapter read once and revised three times outperforms four chapters read once, so the revision sheet existed for exactly this: write it from memory, then re-read only the gaps it failed to hold.

Current Affairs

Capped deliberately. Read monthly, revised weekly, never allowed to expand into all-day news browsing — it is the subject that most reliably disguises itself as work. Static subjects are finite and score every time; current affairs is infinite and scores variably, so the bulk of the marks came from the finite half.

Current Affairs is kept current on this site — use it as the capped, scheduled read rather than as an open tab.

Mock Tests

Every chapter was tested on the Practice door straight after reading it, and every wrong answer was reviewed before the next set — the same mistake recurring in three sets is worth more than five new sets. A subject only closed when a revision test cleared the target twice in a row. Full-length papers live on Practice; the originals live on Previous Year Papers.

Reasoning

Taught as patterns, not as problems. Once the pattern for a series or a puzzle is recognised, the time per question collapses. Speed came from repetition, never from hurrying — and the attempt sheet shows the result: 50 of 50.

Computer

Computer is finite — a syllabus that ends — so it got the treatment every finite subject got: read once, drilled as facts, tested after every reading. The sheet shows 28 correct out of 30 with nothing left blank.

Notes and sets are on Study and Practice under Computer.

English

Grammar rules were practised as rules rather than re-read as prose: a rule, a set of questions on it, then the review. The four wrong answers on the sheet are the rules worth drilling again — a finite list, not a general weakness.

Punjab GK

Read once, then drilled as facts — dates, articles, boundaries. Tested daily in small sets because these marks do not require thinking, only recall.

Time Management

Three rules held throughout: no session longer than ninety minutes, no subject touched twice in the same day in two different sessions, and a hard stop at the planned hour even if the chapter was unfinished. Finishing early was allowed; overrunning was not, because tomorrow's plan was built on today's stopping point.

In the exam hall the same rule applied — a question that has not yielded in forty seconds gets marked and left, not wrestled.

Physical Preparation

The physical test is not an afterthought to the written paper — it is a separate exam with its own syllabus, and it fails candidates who scored well. Running four to five kilometres most mornings, with the standard events practised properly twice a week rather than daily half-effort, kept it from becoming a panic in the last month. Physical work was scheduled like a study block, not squeezed in when energy happened to be left.

Daily Routine

Six hours on a working day, nine to ten on a free day, always in three blocks of ninety minutes with real gaps between them. Ninety minutes was the ceiling — past that, attention goes and the next hour is theatre.

Block 1 · morning

New chapter

Reading only, while the mind is cheapest to run. Never a test.

Block 2 · afternoon

Test it

Chapter quiz on the same material, then the review of every wrong answer.

Block 3 · evening

Revise

One-page sheet from memory, then the gaps written down for tomorrow.

Revision Cycle

Read → test → revise → repeat, in that order and never skipped. Block 3 wrote the one-page sheet from memory; block 1 the next morning started from the gaps it wrote down. A subject only closed when a revision test on it cleared the target twice in a row — optimism was never allowed to close anything.

10 · Questions

Frequently asked questions

Asked before starting, answered plainly.

All questions →

Is Rank 2 possible?

The rank is a result, not a requirement. What is repeatable is the method: a fixed timetable, one source per subject, chapter-level testing after every reading and an honest review of every wrong answer. Start with the timetable below and the score takes care of itself.

How many hours did you study?

Six focused hours on a working day and nine to ten on a free day, split into three blocks of ninety minutes each. The hours were never the variable — sitting down at the same time every day was.

Which books were actually used?

One standard book per subject and nothing more. A second book on a subject you have not finished the first one for is a way of feeling busy without learning anything. The full shelf sits in the Books section with a note on who should skip each one.

Should I join coaching?

No. Everything used to build this score — the notes, the practice sets, the mock tests, the previous year papers and this blueprint — is on this site for free. What coaching sells is a schedule, and a schedule is the one thing on this page you can copy for nothing.

How should beginners start?

Copy the timetable first and study to it for a week before changing anything. Then take one finite subject: read it once, test it chapter by chapter and write the one-page revision sheet from memory. Do not open a second subject until a revision test on the first comes back above target twice in a row.
