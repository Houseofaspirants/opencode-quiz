01 · My Journey

Where this actually started

It did not start with a strategy. It started with failing a practice set badly enough to stop guessing what the syllabus was and go and read it. The first month was unfocused - three subjects at once, no schedule, no way to tell whether any of it was working. The change was not studying more hours. It was writing down, at the end of every day, exactly what got done, and being honest when the answer was nothing.

Once there was a written plan, there was something to fail against, and failing against a plan is fixable. Failing against a mood is not.

02 · Rank

What Rank 2 does and does not mean

It means the marking was consistent across every paper, not that any single paper was brilliant. Rank is a by-product of variance: most candidates lose marks to inconsistency, not to a lack of knowledge. Two questions wrong per paper, every paper, is the difference between a rank and a list position.

Do not chase the rank. Chase the habit that makes a bad day impossible, and the rank is what comes out of it.

03 · Marks

713 out of 800 — where the marks came from

Three decisions did most of the work:

  • Accuracy over attempts. Attempting everything and hoping is a losing strategy once negative marking is in play. Every question was attempted only when two options could be eliminated first.
  • Static subjects first. General knowledge, polity, history and reasoning are finite and score every time. Current affairs is infinite and scores variably. The bulk of the marks came from the finite half.
  • Revision beats reading. A chapter read once and revised three times outperforms four chapters read once. The revision sheet existed for exactly this.

05 · Preparation Strategy

The method, in one paragraph

One subject at a time. Read it once properly, then immediately test it chapter by chapter on the Practice door, then write a one-page revision sheet from memory, then re-read only what the sheet failed to hold. A subject was not considered finished until a revision test on it came back above the target twice in a row. Nothing moved to the next subject on optimism.

06 · Daily Timetable

The day, as it ran

Six hours on a working day, nine to ten on a free day, always in three blocks of ninety minutes with real gaps between them. Ninety minutes was the ceiling — past that, attention goes and the next hour is theatre.

Block 1 · morning

New chapter

Reading only, while the mind is cheapest to run. Never a test.

Block 2 · afternoon

Test it

Chapter quiz on the same material, then the review of every wrong answer.

Block 3 · evening

Revise

One-page sheet from memory, then the gaps written down for tomorrow.

07 · Books I Studied

One book per subject, read properly

The shelf was deliberately small: one standard reference for each subject, plus previous year papers. The second book on a subject you have not finished the first one for is a way of feeling busy without learning anything. Every title that earned its place, with who should skip it, is on the Books shelf.

08 · Subject-wise Strategy

How each subject was handled

Punjab GK & Polity

Read once, then drilled as facts — dates, articles, boundaries. Tested daily in small sets because these marks do not require thinking, only recall.

Reasoning

Taught as patterns, not as problems. Once the pattern for a series or a puzzle is recognised, the time per question collapses. Speed came from repetition, never from hurrying.

Quantitative Aptitude

Formulas written by hand once, then only ever practised. The weakest area initially, and the one that moved most, because it is the most mechanical.

Current Affairs

Capped deliberately. Read monthly, revised weekly, never allowed to expand into all-day news browsing — it is the subject that most reliably disguises itself as work.

09 · Time Management

Time was the actual resource

Three rules held throughout: no session longer than ninety minutes, no subject touched twice in the same day in two different sessions, and a hard stop at the planned hour even if the chapter was unfinished. Finishing early was allowed; overrunning was not, because tomorrow's plan was built on today's stopping point.

In the exam hall the same rule applied — a question that has not yielded in forty seconds gets marked and left, not wrestled.

10 · Physical Preparation

The half that gets skipped

The physical test is not an afterthought to the written paper — it is a separate exam with its own syllabus, and it fails candidates who scored well. Running four to five kilometres most mornings, with the standard events practised properly twice a week rather than daily half-effort, kept it from becoming a panic in the last month. Physical work was scheduled like a study block, not squeezed in when energy happened to be left.

11 · Motivation

What actually kept it going

Not inspiration. The plan was kept deliberately small so it could be completed on a bad day — a bad day that still gets the block done keeps the chain intact, and the chain is the whole thing. Miss a day and the plan argues with you; miss a week and it stops talking entirely.

The other half of it: this site exists because the material was already written for the exam and giving it away costs nothing. Free, for everyone after you, is a larger reason to keep going than a rank is.

12 · Mistakes I Made

What cost marks, in order

  1. Reading three subjects at once in month one. Nothing finished, nothing revised, nothing tested. One at a time from then on.
  2. Treating current affairs as the main subject. Hours in, marks out — the worst ratio on the whole schedule until it was capped.
  3. Buying a second book instead of finishing the first. Shelf looked serious. Syllabus barely moved.
  4. Skipping the review of wrong answers. The same mistake recurring in three sets is worth more than five new sets.
  5. Studying past ninety minutes. The last half-hour of every long session was wasted and made the next morning worse.
13 · How I Scored 713

The whole thing, compressed

A fixed timetable that could be completed even on a bad day. One book per subject. Read, test, revise, repeat — in that order, never skipped. Chapter-level testing after every single reading, so nothing was assumed to have landed. Physical preparation scheduled like study. And a hard cap on the one subject that could swallow the whole schedule.

Every part of it — the notes, the practice sets, the revision tests, the previous year papers and the books list — is on this site and free.

Is 713 out of 800 realistic for me?

The number is a result, not a requirement. What is repeatable is the method: a fixed timetable, one source per subject, chapter-level testing after every reading and an honest review of every wrong answer. Start with the timetable and the score follows.

How many hours a day did this actually take?

Six focused hours on a working day and nine to ten on a free day, split into three blocks of ninety minutes. The hours were never the variable — sitting down at the same time every day was.

Which books were actually read, cover to cover?

One standard book per subject, and nothing more. The full list sits on the Books shelf with a note on who should skip each one.

Do I need paid coaching for this?

No. Everything used to build this score is on this site for free. What coaching sells is a schedule — and the schedule is the one thing on this page you can copy for nothing.
