Question bank

01 Subject And Predicate Part 1: all 25 questions

Read the question, pick an option, then open the answer.

  1. Identify the complete subject in: 'Swimming improves stamina.'

    1. Swimming
    2. stamina
    3. improves
    4. improves stamina
    Show answer

    Answer: (A) Swimming

    Explanation: The gerund 'Swimming' functions as a noun and is the subject. 'Stamina' is the object of the verb 'improves'.

  2. Identify the complete subject in: 'To finish the syllabus on time requires steady effort.'

    1. the syllabus
    2. steady effort
    3. To finish the syllabus on time
    4. To finish
    Show answer

    Answer: (C) To finish the syllabus on time

    Explanation: The infinitive phrase 'To finish the syllabus on time' functions as the subject. 'Steady effort' is the object of 'requires'.

  3. Identify the subject in: 'Here comes the inspection team.'

    1. Here
    2. comes
    3. Here comes
    4. the inspection team
    Show answer

    Answer: (D) the inspection team

    Explanation: In this inverted construction, the subject follows the verb. 'Here' indicates place and does not name what comes.

  4. Identify the complete subject in: 'The revised list of candidates from Bathinda reached the recruitment cell.'

    1. candidates from Bathinda
    2. The revised list of candidates from Bathinda
    3. The revised list
    4. the recruitment cell
    Show answer

    Answer: (B) The revised list of candidates from Bathinda

    Explanation: The complete subject includes the head noun 'list' and all its modifiers. 'The revised list' leaves out the modifying phrase 'of candidates from Bathinda'.

  5. Identify the complete subject in: 'Who did the selection panel recommend for the scholarship?'

    1. Who
    2. the scholarship
    3. the selection panel recommend
    4. the selection panel
    Show answer

    Answer: (D) the selection panel

    Explanation: The selection panel performs the act of recommending and is the subject. 'Who' is the fronted object asking about the person recommended.

  6. Identify the complete subject of the main clause in: 'That the server failed during registration surprised everyone.'

    1. the server
    2. That the server failed during registration
    3. everyone
    4. registration
    Show answer

    Answer: (B) That the server failed during registration

    Explanation: The entire that-clause functions as the subject of 'surprised'. 'The server' is the subject of the subordinate clause, not of the main clause.

  7. Identify the complete subject in: 'Simran and Kabir submitted separate applications.'

    1. Simran
    2. Simran and Kabir
    3. Kabir
    4. separate applications
    Show answer

    Answer: (B) Simran and Kabir

    Explanation: Two names joined by 'and' form the compound subject. Selecting either name alone leaves out one person performing the action.

  8. Identify the complete subject in: 'Why did the district officer postpone the hearing?'

    1. the district officer
    2. the hearing
    3. Why
    4. did the district officer
    Show answer

    Answer: (A) the district officer

    Explanation: The subject is 'the district officer', placed between the auxiliary 'did' and the main verb. The auxiliary remains part of the predicate.

  9. Identify the complete predicate in: 'Our neighbours installed a rainwater tank yesterday.'

    1. a rainwater tank yesterday
    2. Our neighbours installed
    3. installed a rainwater tank yesterday
    4. neighbours installed a rainwater tank
    Show answer

    Answer: (C) installed a rainwater tank yesterday

    Explanation: The complete predicate includes the verb and everything stated about the subject. 'A rainwater tank yesterday' omits the verb 'installed'.

  10. Identify the complete subject in: 'The village librarian issued fresh membership cards.'

    1. The village librarian
    2. The village librarian issued
    3. fresh membership cards
    4. issued fresh membership cards
    Show answer

    Answer: (A) The village librarian

    Explanation: The complete subject names who performs the action and includes its modifiers: 'The village librarian'. The membership cards receive the action and form the object.

  11. In the existential sentence 'There remain several unresolved objections', which expression is the grammatical dummy subject, as distinct from the postverbal noun phrase?

    1. several unresolved objections
    2. unresolved objections
    3. remain
    4. There
    Show answer

    Answer: (D) There

    Explanation: Existential 'there' occupies the grammatical subject position as a dummy subject. 'Several unresolved objections' is the postverbal noun phrase, often called the notional subject.

  12. Identify the complete subject in: 'The quality of the answers, rather than their length, determines the marks.'

    1. the answers
    2. The quality of the answers
    3. their length
    4. the marks
    Show answer

    Answer: (B) The quality of the answers

    Explanation: The subject is headed by 'quality', with 'of the answers' modifying it. The parenthetical contrast 'rather than their length' does not introduce another subject.

  13. Identify the complete subject in: 'Which applicant secured the highest score?'

    1. the highest score
    2. applicant
    3. Which
    4. Which applicant
    Show answer

    Answer: (D) Which applicant

    Explanation: The entire interrogative noun phrase 'Which applicant' is the subject. 'Applicant' alone omits its determiner.

  14. Select the correct division into complete subject and complete predicate: 'The clerk with the blue folder is waiting outside.'

    1. The clerk / with the blue folder is waiting outside
    2. The clerk with the blue / folder is waiting outside
    3. The clerk with the blue folder is / waiting outside
    4. The clerk with the blue folder / is waiting outside
    Show answer

    Answer: (D) The clerk with the blue folder / is waiting outside

    Explanation: The phrase 'with the blue folder' modifies 'clerk' and belongs to the complete subject. The predicate begins with the auxiliary 'is'.

  15. Identify the complete subject in: 'What the witness remembered changed the course of the inquiry.'

    1. What the witness remembered
    2. the witness
    3. the course of the inquiry
    4. What
    Show answer

    Answer: (A) What the witness remembered

    Explanation: The entire clause 'What the witness remembered' is the subject of 'changed'. 'The witness' is only the subject inside that clause.

  16. Identify the complete predicate in: 'The new timetable has reduced waiting time considerably.'

    1. reduced waiting time considerably
    2. has reduced waiting time considerably
    3. waiting time considerably
    4. The new timetable has reduced
    Show answer

    Answer: (B) has reduced waiting time considerably

    Explanation: The complete predicate includes both the auxiliary 'has' and the main verb with its dependants. Starting at 'reduced' incorrectly excludes part of the verb phrase.

  17. Identify the complete subject in: 'Neither the cashier nor the branch manager noticed the discrepancy.'

    1. the cashier
    2. the branch manager
    3. Neither the cashier nor the branch manager
    4. the discrepancy
    Show answer

    Answer: (C) Neither the cashier nor the branch manager

    Explanation: The paired conjunctions 'neither ... nor' connect both noun phrases into the compound subject. Selecting only the nearer noun phrase gives an incomplete subject.

  18. Identify the complete subject in: 'Only after the final bell did the invigilator collect the answer sheets.'

    1. the final bell
    2. the answer sheets
    3. the invigilator
    4. Only after the final bell
    Show answer

    Answer: (C) the invigilator

    Explanation: An initial restrictive expression beginning with 'only' triggers auxiliary-subject inversion. 'The final bell' is part of the time expression, while 'the invigilator' performs the action.

  19. Identify the complete subject in: 'Near the main gate stood two security guards.'

    1. Near the main gate
    2. the main gate
    3. two security guards
    4. stood two security guards
    Show answer

    Answer: (C) two security guards

    Explanation: The sentence places the location first and the subject after 'stood'. 'The main gate' belongs to the introductory prepositional phrase.

  20. Identify the complete subject in: 'The principal, along with two senior teachers, has reviewed the arrangements.'

    1. The principal
    2. two senior teachers
    3. The principal and two senior teachers
    4. the arrangements
    Show answer

    Answer: (A) The principal

    Explanation: The supplementary phrase 'along with two senior teachers' does not form a compound subject with 'The principal'. Replacing 'along with' by 'and' would change the sentence's grammatical structure.

  21. In 'Harpreet, submit the original certificate today', what is the understood grammatical subject of the imperative clause?

    1. you
    2. Harpreet
    3. the original certificate
    4. today
    Show answer

    Answer: (A) you

    Explanation: The imperative has the understood subject 'you'. 'Harpreet', separated by a comma, is a vocative identifying the person addressed.

  22. Identify the complete subject in: 'Attached to the notice were copies of the amended guidelines.'

    1. the notice
    2. the amended guidelines
    3. copies of the amended guidelines
    4. Attached to the notice
    Show answer

    Answer: (C) copies of the amended guidelines

    Explanation: The fronted complement causes inversion, placing the complete subject after 'were'. 'The amended guidelines' is only the complement of 'of' within that subject.

  23. Identify the complete subject in: 'The proposal that the committee approved yesterday needs further funding.'

    1. The proposal that the committee approved yesterday
    2. the committee
    3. The proposal
    4. further funding
    Show answer

    Answer: (A) The proposal that the committee approved yesterday

    Explanation: The relative clause modifies 'proposal' and forms part of the complete subject. 'The committee' is the subject of the embedded relative clause only.

  24. Identify the complete subject in: 'Checking every entry before submission prevents avoidable errors.'

    1. every entry
    2. Checking every entry before submission
    3. Checking every entry
    4. avoidable errors
    Show answer

    Answer: (B) Checking every entry before submission

    Explanation: The whole gerund phrase functions as the subject, including the time modifier 'before submission'. 'Every entry' is the object within that phrase.

  25. What is the understood subject of the imperative sentence 'Keep your admit card ready'?

    1. your admit card
    2. card
    3. ready
    4. you
    Show answer

    Answer: (D) you

    Explanation: An imperative normally has the understood subject 'you'. 'Your admit card' is the object of 'keep', not the subject.
