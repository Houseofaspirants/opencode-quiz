Question bank

02 Clauses And Phrases Part 1: all 25 questions

Read the question, pick an option, then open the answer.

  1. Identify the bracketed group in: The files [on the upper shelf] are confidential.

    1. Adverb phrase
    2. Noun clause
    3. Adjective phrase
    4. Adverb clause
    Show answer

    Answer: (C) Adjective phrase

    Explanation: The prepositional phrase modifies files and thus functions adjectivally. It does not modify the verb, so it is not an adverb phrase.

  2. Choose the connector that preserves this meaning: Immediately after the bell rang, the candidates stopped writing. No sooner had the bell rung ___ the candidates stopped writing.

    1. when
    2. then
    3. than
    4. that
    Show answer

    Answer: (C) than

    Explanation: No sooner pairs with than and requires inversion when placed initially. When pairs with hardly or scarcely in this construction.

  3. Identify the bracketed group in: The clerk worked [with great care].

    1. Noun clause
    2. Adverb phrase
    3. Adjective clause
    4. Noun phrase
    Show answer

    Answer: (B) Adverb phrase

    Explanation: The group modifies worked and has no subject–finite verb structure, so it is an adverb phrase. It does not name an entity as a noun phrase would.

  4. Choose the correctly formed sentence.

    1. The village where my aunt lives there has a dispensary.
    2. The village which my aunt lives has a dispensary.
    3. The village in where my aunt lives has a dispensary.
    4. The village where my aunt lives has a dispensary.
    Show answer

    Answer: (D) The village where my aunt lives has a dispensary.

    Explanation: Where supplies the location within the relative clause, so there must not repeat it. Which would require a preposition such as in.

  5. Choose the connector that makes the transfer independent of the employee’s consent: The order will take effect ___ the employee agrees or not.

    1. unless
    2. provided
    3. although
    4. whether
    Show answer

    Answer: (D) whether

    Explanation: Whether ... or not expresses that either alternative leaves the outcome unchanged. Provided makes agreement a condition and reverses the intended meaning.

  6. Identify the bracketed clause in: The revised procedure took less time [than the auditors had expected].

    1. Adverb clause of comparison
    2. Adverb clause of concession
    3. Adverb clause of result
    4. Adverb clause of time
    Show answer

    Answer: (A) Adverb clause of comparison

    Explanation: Than introduces the standard against which the time taken is compared. Mentioning time does not make the clause a time clause.

  7. Identify the function of the bracketed clause: The rumour [that the merit list had been withdrawn] proved false.

    1. Appositional content clause
    2. Relative clause modifying rumour
    3. Adverb clause expressing reason
    4. Noun clause functioning as object
    Show answer

    Answer: (A) Appositional content clause

    Explanation: The clause states the content of the rumour and is appositional. Unlike a relative clause, it has no missing subject or object referring back to rumour.

  8. Identify the bracketed clause in: Submit the application [where the notice directs you to submit it].

    1. Adverb clause of time
    2. Adverb clause of purpose
    3. Adverb clause of condition
    4. Adverb clause of place
    Show answer

    Answer: (D) Adverb clause of place

    Explanation: Where specifies the location of submission and introduces a place clause. It does not state when submission should occur.

  9. Choose the connector that makes possession of an admit card a necessary condition for entry: You cannot enter the hall ___ you carry your admit card.

    1. unless
    2. although
    3. because
    4. whereas
    Show answer

    Answer: (A) unless

    Explanation: Unless means if not: without an admit card, entry is barred. Although expresses concession and does not impose the required condition.

  10. Identify the bracketed group in: The guard waited [until the officer returned].

    1. Adverb clause of time
    2. Adverb clause of reason
    3. Adverb clause of purpose
    4. Adverb clause of condition
    Show answer

    Answer: (A) Adverb clause of time

    Explanation: Until sets the time limit of the waiting. It does not state a condition for the action.

  11. Choose the correct completion: By the time the interview begins tomorrow, the staff ___.

    1. had arranged the seats
    2. have arranged the seats
    3. will have arranged the seats
    4. would have arranged the seats
    Show answer

    Answer: (C) will have arranged the seats

    Explanation: The future perfect marks completion before a specified future event. The past perfect would require a past reference point.

  12. Choose the sentence that correctly attaches the opening non-finite clause.

    1. Having checked the vouchers, the discrepancy was noticed by the accountant.
    2. Having checked the vouchers, the accountant noticed a discrepancy.
    3. Having checked the vouchers, a discrepancy came to the accountant’s attention.
    4. Having checked the vouchers, the vouchers revealed a discrepancy.
    Show answer

    Answer: (B) Having checked the vouchers, the accountant noticed a discrepancy.

    Explanation: The understood subject of having checked must be the subject of the main clause: the accountant. Making discrepancy the subject leaves the opening clause dangling.

  13. Identify the bracketed group in: [What she reported] shocked the committee.

    1. Adjective clause
    2. Adverb clause
    3. Noun phrase
    4. Noun clause
    Show answer

    Answer: (D) Noun clause

    Explanation: The clause acts as the subject of shocked and therefore functions as a noun clause. It does not modify a noun as an adjective clause would.

  14. Identify the function of the bracketed clause: The panel confirmed [that the certificates were genuine].

    1. Subject
    2. Subject complement
    3. Direct object
    4. Apposition
    Show answer

    Answer: (C) Direct object

    Explanation: The clause gives the content confirmed and is the direct object of confirmed. It is not a complement after a linking verb.

  15. Which sentence contains a relative clause rather than an appositional content clause?

    1. The belief that the paper was leaked caused unrest.
    2. The report that the bridge was unsafe reached the office.
    3. The claim that the agent made was misleading.
    4. The news that the minister had resigned spread quickly.
    Show answer

    Answer: (C) The claim that the agent made was misleading.

    Explanation: That the agent made has an object gap referring to claim and is relative. The other that-clauses state the content of the preceding nouns.

  16. Which option correctly combines these statements using an adjective clause? The candidate won. He lives in Moga.

    1. The candidate who lives in Moga won.
    2. The candidate won because he lives in Moga.
    3. The candidate won when he lived in Moga.
    4. The candidate won although he lives in Moga.
    Show answer

    Answer: (A) The candidate who lives in Moga won.

    Explanation: Who lives in Moga modifies candidate and is an adjective clause. Because introduces a reason clause instead.

  17. Identify the bracketed group in: [To secure a regular appointment] remains his main ambition.

    1. Noun phrase functioning as object
    2. Infinitive clause functioning as subject
    3. Finite clause functioning as subject
    4. Adverb phrase functioning as adjunct
    Show answer

    Answer: (B) Infinitive clause functioning as subject

    Explanation: The infinitive clause functions as the subject of remains. To secure is non-finite, so the clause is not finite.

  18. Identify the function of the bracketed clause: The selection board will appoint [whoever scores the highest marks].

    1. Relative clause modifying board
    2. Noun clause functioning as direct object
    3. Adverb clause expressing condition
    4. Noun clause functioning as subject complement
    Show answer

    Answer: (B) Noun clause functioning as direct object

    Explanation: The fused relative clause functions nominally as the object of appoint. It has no separate antecedent noun that it modifies as an adjective clause.

  19. Choose the grammatically correct completion: The official to ___ the petition was addressed has retired.

    1. who
    2. whose
    3. that
    4. whom
    Show answer

    Answer: (D) whom

    Explanation: A relative pronoun immediately after a preposition takes the objective form whom. Who cannot serve as the object of the preceding to in formal examination English.

  20. Identify the bracketed clause in: The invigilator spoke softly [so that the other candidates would not be disturbed].

    1. Adverb clause of result
    2. Adverb clause of comparison
    3. Adverb clause of purpose
    4. Adverb clause of concession
    Show answer

    Answer: (C) Adverb clause of purpose

    Explanation: The clause expresses the intended aim of speaking softly and is a purpose clause. Would not be disturbed describes an intended outcome rather than an asserted actual result.

  21. Choose the appropriate connector: The recruitment test was postponed ___ the examination centre had been flooded.

    1. although
    2. because
    3. unless
    4. so that
    Show answer

    Answer: (B) because

    Explanation: Because introduces the reason for postponement. So that normally introduces an intended purpose, which flooding cannot be here.

  22. Identify the bracketed clause in: The queue was so long [that it extended beyond the gate].

    1. Adverb clause of purpose
    2. Adverb clause of result
    3. Adverb clause of condition
    4. Adverb clause of reason
    Show answer

    Answer: (B) Adverb clause of result

    Explanation: The so ... that construction presents the consequence of the queue’s length. It expresses an actual result, not an intended purpose.

  23. Identify the function of the bracketed clause: The difficulty is [that no witness remembers the date].

    1. Direct object
    2. Subject complement
    3. Subject
    4. Apposition
    Show answer

    Answer: (B) Subject complement

    Explanation: The clause follows linking is and specifies the difficulty, so it is a subject complement. Is does not take a direct object here.

  24. Identify the bracketed clause in: [Although the road was blocked], the ambulance reached the hospital.

    1. Adverb clause of result
    2. Adverb clause of reason
    3. Adverb clause of time
    4. Adverb clause of concession
    Show answer

    Answer: (D) Adverb clause of concession

    Explanation: Although introduces an obstacle despite which the main action occurred. A reason clause would explain the arrival rather than present an obstacle.

  25. Classify the bracketed clause by finiteness: [With the last bus having departed], the trainees hired a taxi.

    1. Non-finite clause with an explicit subject
    2. Finite clause with a past-tense verb
    3. Finite clause with a perfect-tense verb
    4. Non-finite clause with an omitted subject
    Show answer

    Answer: (A) Non-finite clause with an explicit subject

    Explanation: The last bus is explicit, but having departed has no finite tense-bearing verb. Perfect aspect alone does not make a clause finite.
