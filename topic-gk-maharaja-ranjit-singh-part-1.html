Maharaja Ranjit Singh - Part 1: all 20 questions

Read the question, pick an option, then open the answer.

  1. Where was Maharaja Ranjit Singh born?

    1. Lahore
    2. Amritsar
    3. Gujranwala
    4. Peshawar
    Show answer

    Answer: (C) Gujranwala

    Explanation: The notes identify Gujranwala as Maharaja Ranjit Singh's birthplace. Lahore, Amritsar and Peshawar are therefore incorrect as answers to this question. The notes give his date of birth as 13 November 1780.

  2. Consider the following statements:1.Ranjit Singh called his government 'Sarkar Khalsa'.2.He called his court 'Darbar Khalsa'.Which of the above is/are correct?

    1. Both 1 and 2
    2. 1 only
    3. 2 only
    4. Neither 1 nor 2
    Show answer

    Answer: (A) Both 1 and 2

    Explanation: Statement 1 is correct because the government was called Sarkar Khalsa. Statement 2 is correct because the court was called Darbar Khalsa. The options accepting only one statement or rejecting both contradict the notes. Lahore served as his capital.

  3. Who served as Head of Customs in Ranjit Singh's administration?

    1. Dhian Singh Dogra
    2. Fakir Aziz-ud-Din
    3. Lehna Singh Majithia
    4. Misr Ralia Ram
    Show answer

    Answer: (D) Misr Ralia Ram

    Explanation: Misr Ralia Ram is listed as Head of Customs. Dhian Singh Dogra was Prime Minister, while Fakir Aziz-ud-Din was Foreign Minister. Lehna Singh Majithia headed the Industrial or Ordnance Department. These assignments show the distinction between customs administration and the other ministerial responsibilities.

  4. Match List-I with List-II according to the rulers preceding Ranjit Singh's conquests:List-I (Territory)A.KasurB.JhangC.SialkotD.AttockList-II (Previous ruler)1.Ahmad Khan2.Jahandad Khan3.Qutb-ud-Din4.Jiwan SinghSelect the correct code in the order A, B, C, D.

    1. 1, 3, 2, 4
    2. 3, 1, 4, 2
    3. 3, 4, 1, 2
    4. 2, 1, 4, 3
    Show answer

    Answer: (B) 3, 1, 4, 2

    Explanation: Kasur was ruled by Qutb-ud-Din, so A matches 3. Jhang was ruled by Ahmad Khan, so B matches 1. Sialkot was ruled by Jiwan Singh, so C matches 4. Attock was ruled by Jahandad Khan, so D matches 2, and the other codes misassign these rulers. Kasur and Jhang were both conquered in 1807.

  5. Which wife of Ranjit Singh was the mother of Duleep Singh?

    1. Jind Kaur
    2. Mehtab Kaur
    3. Datar Kaur
    4. Moran Sarkar
    Show answer

    Answer: (A) Jind Kaur

    Explanation: The notes identify Jind Kaur as Duleep Singh's mother. Mehtab Kaur, Datar Kaur and Moran Sarkar are also listed as Ranjit Singh's wives, but they are not assigned this relationship. Duleep Singh is described as the last Maharaja.

  6. Assertion (A): Land revenue was the main source of income for Ranjit Singh's state.Reason (R): Land revenue was collected twice a year, at harvest time.Select the correct answer.

    1. Both A and R are true and R is the correct explanation of A
    2. Both A and R are true but R is not the correct explanation of A
    3. A is true but R is false
    4. A is false but R is true
    Show answer

    Answer: (B) Both A and R are true but R is not the correct explanation of A

    Explanation: Assertion A is true because land revenue contributed about 70% of state income. Reason R is also true because the notes specify collection twice a year at harvest time. The collection schedule does not explain why land revenue accounted for the largest share of income. The distinction to remember is between the importance of a revenue source and the timing of its collection.

  7. In which year was the Treaty of Amritsar between Ranjit Singh and the British concluded?

    1. 1801
    2. 1807
    3. 1809
    4. 1838
    Show answer

    Answer: (C) 1809

    Explanation: The notes date the Treaty of Amritsar to 1809. The other years, 1801, 1807 and 1838, do not give the date of this treaty. It is also called the Minto–Metcalfe Treaty.

  8. Consider the following statements about Ranjit Singh's name:1.His original name was Budh Singh.2.His name was changed to commemorate his father's victory over the Chattha chief Pir Muhammad.Which of the above is/are correct?

    1. 1 only
    2. 2 only
    3. Neither 1 nor 2
    4. Both 1 and 2
    Show answer

    Answer: (D) Both 1 and 2

    Explanation: Statement 1 is correct because the notes give Budh Singh as his original name. Statement 2 is correct because they link the change of name to his father's victory over Pir Muhammad. Options accepting only one statement or rejecting both are therefore incorrect. Pir Muhammad is identified specifically as a Chattha chief.

  9. Which currency–place pair is NOT correctly matched according to the notes?

    1. Hari Singhi — Kashmir
    2. Moran Shahi — Lahore
    3. Peshawari–Naunihal Singhi — Peshawar
    4. Nanakshahi introduction — Lahore
    Show answer

    Answer: (B) Moran Shahi — Lahore

    Explanation: Moran Shahi coins are associated with Amritsar, so their pairing with Lahore is incorrect. Hari Singhi coins belong to Kashmir, while Peshawari–Naunihal Singhi coins belong to Peshawar. The Nanakshahi currency was introduced at Lahore, making that pair correct. The notes date its introduction to 1801. Hari Singhi coins were named after Governor Hari Singh Nalwa.

  10. Arrange the following events in Ranjit Singh's early life from earliest to latest:1.His first battle at Sodhran2.Inheritance of his father's Misl3.Survival of Hashmat Khan's assassination attempt

    1. 2 → 1 → 3
    2. 3 → 1 → 2
    3. 1 → 3 → 2
    4. 1 → 2 → 3
    Show answer

    Answer: (D) 1 → 2 → 3

    Explanation: The notes place his first battle at Sodhran at the age of ten. He inherited the Misl after his father's death at the age of twelve. He survived Hashmat Khan's assassination attempt at the age of thirteen, giving the order 1 → 2 → 3 and ruling out the other sequences. These events show that both military experience and leadership responsibilities began during his childhood.

  11. For what purpose did Ranjit Singh's government provide Taccavi loans to farmers?

    1. To pay land revenue
    2. To dig irrigation wells
    3. To maintain village courts
    4. To equip garrison troops
    Show answer

    Answer: (B) To dig irrigation wells

    Explanation: The government provided Taccavi loans to help farmers dig wells. Payment of land revenue, maintenance of courts and equipment of garrison troops are not the purpose assigned to these loans in the notes. The loans are discussed under the state's revenue system.

  12. Match List-I with List-II:List-I (Office or department)A.Prime MinisterB.Foreign MinisterC.Finance MinisterD.Industrial (Ordnance) DepartmentList-II (Official)1.Dina Nath2.Dhian Singh Dogra3.Lehna Singh Majithia4.Fakir Aziz-ud-DinSelect the correct code in the order A, B, C, D.

    1. 2, 4, 1, 3
    2. 4, 2, 1, 3
    3. 2, 1, 4, 3
    4. 2, 4, 3, 1
    Show answer

    Answer: (A) 2, 4, 1, 3

    Explanation: Dhian Singh Dogra was Prime Minister, so A matches 2. Fakir Aziz-ud-Din was Foreign Minister, so B matches 4. Dina Nath was Finance Minister, so C matches 1. Lehna Singh Majithia headed the Industrial or Ordnance Department, so D matches 3, and the other codes interchange these responsibilities. The notes use Industrial and Ordnance together for the department in the fourth pair.

  13. Which force is specifically described as receiving strict French-style training?

    1. Fauj-i-Ain
    2. Fauj-i-Be-Qawaid
    3. Fauj-i-Qiladari
    4. Fauj-i-Khas
    Show answer

    Answer: (D) Fauj-i-Khas

    Explanation: The notes specifically associate strict French-style training with Fauj-i-Khas, the elite force. Fauj-i-Ain is described as the regular force, Fauj-i-Be-Qawaid as the irregular force, and Fauj-i-Qiladari as the fort garrison force. Fauj-i-Khas also had its own emblem and flag and the best weapons and equipment.

  14. Assertion (A): The Phulkian Misl retained its independence during Ranjit Singh's consolidation of the Misls.Reason (R): The Phulkian Misl was under British protection.Select the correct answer.

    1. Both A and R are true and R is the correct explanation of A
    2. Both A and R are true but R is not the correct explanation of A
    3. A is true but R is false
    4. A is false but R is true
    Show answer

    Answer: (A) Both A and R are true and R is the correct explanation of A

    Explanation: Assertion A is true because the notes state that the Phulkian Misl remained independent. Reason R is true because the notes place it under British protection. That protection explains the continued independence described in A, so the other combinations are incorrect. The chapter also identifies military conquest as a method used to consolidate other Misls.

  15. Consider the following statements about Ranjit Singh's coronation:1.He was crowned Maharaja of Punjab on 12 April 1801.2.Baba Sahib Singh Bedi, who conducted the ceremony, was a descendant of Guru Gobind Singh Ji.Which of the above is/are correct?

    1. 2 only
    2. 1 only
    3. Both 1 and 2
    4. Neither 1 nor 2
    Show answer

    Answer: (B) 1 only

    Explanation: Statement 1 is correct because the notes give 12 April 1801 as the coronation date. Statement 2 is incorrect because Baba Sahib Singh Bedi was a descendant of Guru Nanak Dev Ji, not Guru Gobind Singh Ji. Baba Sahib Singh Bedi conducted the formal ceremony in which Ranjit Singh was crowned Maharaja of Punjab. Thus, the options accepting statement 2 or rejecting statement 1 are incorrect.

  16. How many storeys did the tower of Gurdwara Baba Atal have, as described in the notes?

    1. 4
    2. 7
    3. 9
    4. 12
    Show answer

    Answer: (C) 9

    Explanation: The notes describe the tower of Gurdwara Baba Atal as nine-storeyed. The figures four, seven and twelve do not match the height given for this tower. The chapter also records Ranjit Singh's contribution of gold plating for the walls of Harmandir Sahib.

  17. Which place–territorial limit pair is NOT correctly matched for the Sikh Empire at its peak?

    1. Ladakh — Southern limit
    2. Shikarpur — Southern limit
    3. Jamrud — Northwestern limit
    4. Sutlej — Eastern limit
    Show answer

    Answer: (A) Ladakh — Southern limit

    Explanation: Ladakh marked the northern limit, so its pairing with the southern limit is incorrect. Shikarpur marked the southern limit and is correctly matched. Jamrud marked the northwestern limit, while the Sutlej marked the eastern limit, making both remaining pairs correct. The notes present these limits as the empire's extent at its peak.

  18. Consider the following statements about the judicial system:1.Panchayat courts delivered justice in villages.2.The Adalat-i-Ala at Lahore heard appeals from different regions.3.A Nazim's court was the final appellate authority above the Maharaja.Which of the above are correct?

    1. 1 only
    2. 2 and 3 only
    3. 1 and 2 only
    4. 1, 2 and 3
    Show answer

    Answer: (C) 1 and 2 only

    Explanation: Statement 1 is correct because Panchayat courts administered village justice. Statement 2 is correct because the Adalat-i-Ala at Lahore heard regional appeals. Statement 3 is incorrect because the Maharaja, rather than a Nazim, was the highest court of appeal. The notes also list the courts of Kardars and Adalatis within the judicial system.

  19. Arrange the following conquests from earliest to latest:1.Kashmir2.Lahore3.Multan4.Amritsar

    1. 2 → 3 → 4 → 1
    2. 4 → 2 → 3 → 1
    3. 2 → 4 → 1 → 3
    4. 2 → 4 → 3 → 1
    Show answer

    Answer: (D) 2 → 4 → 3 → 1

    Explanation: Lahore was conquered in 1799 and Amritsar in 1802. Multan followed in 1818 and Kashmir in 1819. Therefore, the correct sequence is 2 → 4 → 3 → 1; each other option reverses at least one of these relationships. The notes identify the Bhangi Sardars as Lahore's previous rulers.

  20. To which temple did Ranjit Singh wish to donate the Koh-i-Noor in 1839?

    1. Vishwanath Temple
    2. Jwalamukhi Temple
    3. Jagannath Temple at Puri
    4. Kangra Temple
    Show answer

    Answer: (C) Jagannath Temple at Puri

    Explanation: Ranjit Singh wished to donate the Koh-i-Noor to the Jagannath Temple at Puri in 1839. Vishwanath, Jwalamukhi and Kangra are temples to which the notes record gold donations, rather than this intended diamond donation. The wish concerning the Koh-i-Noor was never carried out. Puri is identified in the notes as being in Odisha.
