Question bank

03 Types Of Sentences Part 1: all 25 questions

Read the question, pick an option, then open the answer.

  1. Choose the correct exclamatory form of 'The garden is very beautiful.'

    1. What beautiful the garden is!
    2. How a beautiful garden it is!
    3. How beautiful the garden is!
    4. What the garden is beautiful!
    Show answer

    Answer: (C) How beautiful the garden is!

    Explanation: 'How' is followed by an adjective and then the subject and verb. 'What' requires a noun phrase, as in 'What a beautiful garden it is!'

  2. Identify the sentence that expresses a wish.

    1. May you succeed in the recruitment examination!
    2. Will you succeed in the recruitment examination?
    3. You will succeed in the recruitment examination.
    4. Prepare well for the recruitment examination.
    Show answer

    Answer: (A) May you succeed in the recruitment examination!

    Explanation: An optative sentence expresses a wish or prayer, often using 'may'. 'Will you succeed' asks a question rather than expressing a wish.

  3. Choose the correct tag: 'The witness hardly ever contradicts herself, _____'

    1. doesn't she?
    2. did she?
    3. does she?
    4. didn't she?
    Show answer

    Answer: (C) does she?

    Explanation: 'Hardly ever' gives the statement a negative sense, requiring a positive tag. The present simple verb 'contradicts' requires 'does', not 'did'.

  4. Choose the correct tag: 'The candidates have received their admit cards, _____'

    1. didn't they?
    2. haven't they?
    3. don't they?
    4. hadn't they?
    Show answer

    Answer: (B) haven't they?

    Explanation: A positive present perfect statement takes a negative tag with 'have'. 'Didn't they' would match a simple past statement.

  5. Choose the correct negative imperative corresponding to 'Touch the exposed wire.'

    1. Not touch the exposed wire.
    2. Do not touch the exposed wire.
    3. Does not touch the exposed wire.
    4. Do not touching the exposed wire.
    Show answer

    Answer: (B) Do not touch the exposed wire.

    Explanation: A negative imperative uses 'do not' followed by the base verb. 'Not touch' omits the auxiliary required in a normal complete command.

  6. Choose the correctly punctuated sentence containing an indirect question.

    1. Please explain why the results were delayed.
    2. Please explain why were the results delayed.
    3. Please explain why the results were delayed?
    4. Please explain, why were the results delayed?
    Show answer

    Answer: (A) Please explain why the results were delayed.

    Explanation: An indirect question uses statement word order, and this imperative request ends with a full stop. A question mark is not required merely because the sentence contains 'why'.

  7. Choose the assertive sentence that preserves the meaning of the wish 'If only I had checked the notification before applying!'

    1. I wish I had checked the notification before applying.
    2. I wish I would check the notification before applying.
    3. I wish I have checked the notification before applying.
    4. I wish I will check the notification before applying.
    Show answer

    Answer: (A) I wish I had checked the notification before applying.

    Explanation: A regret about an unrealised past action uses 'wish + past perfect'. 'Would check' shifts towards a desired future action or willingness and does not preserve this past regret.

  8. Choose the grammatically correct negative assertive sentence.

    1. The clerk does not issue passes on Sundays.
    2. The clerk do not issue passes on Sundays.
    3. The clerk does not issues passes on Sundays.
    4. The clerk not issues passes on Sundays.
    Show answer

    Answer: (A) The clerk does not issue passes on Sundays.

    Explanation: A singular third-person subject takes 'does not' followed by the base verb. 'Does not issues' incorrectly marks the verb for agreement twice.

  9. Choose the correctly punctuated sentence that reports a question without quoting the speaker's exact words.

    1. The officer asked, whether the gate was locked?
    2. The officer asked whether was the gate locked.
    3. The officer asked whether the gate was locked?
    4. The officer asked whether the gate was locked.
    Show answer

    Answer: (D) The officer asked whether the gate was locked.

    Explanation: A reported yes-no question uses 'whether' or 'if', statement word order, and a full stop when the reporting sentence is assertive. The original question's force does not require a question mark in the reported sentence.

  10. Choose the assertive sentence that preserves the meaning of 'Who can deny the importance of clean drinking water?'

    1. Everyone can deny the importance of clean drinking water.
    2. Someone can deny the importance of clean drinking water.
    3. Anyone can deny the importance of clean drinking water.
    4. No one can deny the importance of clean drinking water.
    Show answer

    Answer: (D) No one can deny the importance of clean drinking water.

    Explanation: The rhetorical question implies that nobody can deny the importance stated. 'Everyone can deny' reverses that meaning.

  11. Choose the assertive form of 'What a careless mistake Aman has made!'

    1. Aman has made a very careful mistake.
    2. Has Aman made a very careless mistake?
    3. Aman has made a very careless mistake.
    4. Aman has not made a careless mistake.
    Show answer

    Answer: (C) Aman has made a very careless mistake.

    Explanation: An exclamation beginning with 'What a' can become an assertive sentence using 'a very' before the adjective. The interrogative option changes the statement into a question.

  12. Choose the correctly punctuated imperative addressing a person named Inspector.

    1. Inspector check, the seal before opening the packet.
    2. Inspector check the seal, before opening the packet.
    3. Inspector, check the seal before opening the packet.
    4. Inspector check the seal before, opening the packet.
    Show answer

    Answer: (C) Inspector, check the seal before opening the packet.

    Explanation: A word used in direct address is separated from the command by a comma. A comma before 'before opening' does not mark the person being addressed.

  13. Choose the conventional tag for this inclusive suggestion: 'Let's review the evidence once more, _____'

    1. will you?
    2. won't you?
    3. do we?
    4. shall we?
    Show answer

    Answer: (D) shall we?

    Explanation: An inclusive suggestion beginning with 'Let's' conventionally takes 'shall we'. 'Will you' is commonly attached to a command addressed to someone else.

  14. Choose the correct tag: 'Nobody has challenged the selection procedure, _____'

    1. have they?
    2. hasn't he?
    3. haven't they?
    4. has he?
    Show answer

    Answer: (A) have they?

    Explanation: 'Nobody' makes the statement negative and is normally represented by singular 'they' in the tag, giving 'have they'. 'Haven't they' incorrectly makes the tag negative too.

  15. Choose the correctly formed yes-no question corresponding to 'Simran completed the survey yesterday.'

    1. Did Simran complete the survey yesterday?
    2. Did Simran completed the survey yesterday?
    3. Does Simran complete the survey yesterday?
    4. Had Simran complete the survey yesterday?
    Show answer

    Answer: (A) Did Simran complete the survey yesterday?

    Explanation: A simple past question uses 'did' followed by the base verb. 'Did Simran completed' incorrectly retains the past form after 'did'.

  16. Choose the correctly formed neutral question asking who performed the action in 'Someone misplaced the attendance register.'

    1. Who did misplaced the attendance register?
    2. Who misplaced the attendance register?
    3. Who did the attendance register misplace?
    4. Who was misplaced the attendance register?
    Show answer

    Answer: (B) Who misplaced the attendance register?

    Explanation: When 'who' is the subject, a neutral simple past question normally uses the past verb directly without 'did'. 'Who did misplaced' also incorrectly combines 'did' with a past-tense main verb.

  17. Choose the imperative sentence.

    1. You submitted the application yesterday.
    2. Did you submit the application yesterday?
    3. May your application be accepted!
    4. Submit the application before noon.
    Show answer

    Answer: (D) Submit the application before noon.

    Explanation: An imperative uses the base form of the verb with an understood subject 'you'. 'You submitted' is an assertive statement about a past action.

  18. Choose the negative assertive sentence that preserves the meaning of 'Only registered voters may enter this enclosure.'

    1. Registered voters may not enter this enclosure.
    2. Unregistered voters may also enter this enclosure.
    3. No registered voter may enter this enclosure.
    4. No one except registered voters may enter this enclosure.
    Show answer

    Answer: (D) No one except registered voters may enter this enclosure.

    Explanation: 'Only' can be replaced by 'no one except' to express the same restriction negatively. 'No registered voter' excludes precisely the group allowed by the original.

  19. Choose the standard question tag: 'I am next in the interview queue, _____'

    1. amn't I?
    2. isn't I?
    3. aren't I?
    4. don't I?
    Show answer

    Answer: (C) aren't I?

    Explanation: In standard English, the usual negative tag for affirmative 'I am' is 'aren't I'. 'Amn't I' is not the standard form expected in these examinations.

  20. Choose the correct tag: 'Few applicants understood the revised eligibility clause, _____'

    1. didn't they?
    2. did they?
    3. had they?
    4. hadn't they?
    Show answer

    Answer: (B) did they?

    Explanation: 'Few' has a negative sense, so the simple past statement takes the positive tag 'did they'. 'Didn't they' would suit 'a few applicants understood', which has a positive sense.

  21. Choose the assertive sentence equivalent to the rhetorical question 'Is this the way to treat an injured passenger?'

    1. This is not the way to treat an injured passenger.
    2. This is certainly the way to treat an injured passenger.
    3. This may be the way to treat an injured passenger.
    4. This must be the way to treat an injured passenger.
    Show answer

    Answer: (A) This is not the way to treat an injured passenger.

    Explanation: This rhetorical question expresses disapproval and therefore becomes a negative assertion. 'Certainly the way' expresses approval and reverses the intended meaning.

  22. Choose the correctly punctuated direct question.

    1. Where the Patiala bus stops?
    2. Where does the Patiala bus stop?
    3. Where does the Patiala bus stop.
    4. Where does the Patiala bus stop!
    Show answer

    Answer: (B) Where does the Patiala bus stop?

    Explanation: A direct wh-question uses interrogative word order and ends with a question mark. 'Where the Patiala bus stops?' lacks the auxiliary needed here.

  23. Choose the correct tag: 'The office was not open at nine, _____'

    1. wasn't it?
    2. was it?
    3. did it?
    4. didn't it?
    Show answer

    Answer: (B) was it?

    Explanation: A negative statement normally takes a positive tag using the same auxiliary. 'Wasn't it' incorrectly keeps the tag negative.

  24. Choose the correctly formed wh-question asking about the object in 'Harpreet purchased a dictionary.'

    1. What Harpreet purchased?
    2. What did Harpreet purchased?
    3. What did Harpreet purchase?
    4. What does Harpreet purchased?
    Show answer

    Answer: (C) What did Harpreet purchase?

    Explanation: An object question in the simple past uses 'what + did + subject + base verb'. 'What did Harpreet purchased' wrongly uses a past-tense verb after 'did'.

  25. Choose the correctly formed optative sentence expressing a wish for the team's victory.

    1. May the team wins the final!
    2. May the team winning the final!
    3. May the team won the final!
    4. May the team win the final!
    Show answer

    Answer: (D) May the team win the final!

    Explanation: The modal 'may' takes the base form of the verb even with a singular subject. 'May the team wins' incorrectly adds the third-person ending.
