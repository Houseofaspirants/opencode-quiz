## What this topic covers

### The fraction table: learn it by heart

| Fraction | Percent | Fraction | Percent |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 1/2 | 50% | 1/9 | 11.11% |
| 1/3 | 33.33% | 1/10 | 10% |
| 1/4 | 25% | 1/11 | 9.09% |
| 1/5 | 20% | 1/12 | 8.33% |
| 1/6 | 16.67% | 1/16 | 6.25% |
| 1/7 | 14.28% | 1/20 | 5% |
| 1/8 | 12.5% | 1/25 | 4% |

Why it matters: 12.5% of 640 is just 640 ÷ 8 = 80. No multiplication needed.

### Core percentage formulas

- **Successive change:** for changes of a% and b%, net change = **a + b + ab/100** (use minus for decrease).
- **"More than" and "less than":** if A is r% more than B, then B is **r/(100 + r) × 100** % less than A. If A is r% less than B, then B is **r/(100 − r) × 100** % more than A.
- **Price up, keep expenditure same:** if price rises by r%, consumption must fall by **r/(100 + r) × 100** %.
- **Population or depreciation:** after n years at r% a year, value = **P × (1 ± r/100)ⁿ**.

### Core profit and loss formulas

- **Profit % = Profit ÷ CP × 100.** Loss % = Loss ÷ CP × 100. Always on **cost price**.
- **SP = CP × (100 + Profit%) / 100**, or CP × (100 − Loss%) / 100.
- **Discount is on the marked price:** SP = MP × (100 − Discount%) / 100.
- **Successive discounts** d1% and d2% = d1 + d2 − d1·d2/100.
- **Same SP, x% profit on one and x% loss on the other:** always a **loss of x²/100 %**.
- **False weight:** gain % = **error ÷ (true value − error) × 100**.

## How it is asked in the exam

### Example 1: successive change

*A price rises by 20% and then falls by 10%. What is the net change?*

20 − 10 + (20 × −10)/100 = 10 − 2 = **8% increase**.

### Example 2: "more than / less than"

*A's salary is 20% more than B's. By what percent is B's salary less than A's?*

20/120 × 100 = **16.67%**. (1/6 from the table.)

### Example 3: expenditure

*The price of sugar rises by 25%. By how much must a family cut consumption to keep spending the same?*

25/125 × 100 = **20%**.

### Example 4: find CP, then new SP

*By selling an article for ₹540, a shopkeeper loses 10%. At what price should he sell it to gain 10%?*

- CP = 540 × 100/90 = ₹600.
- New SP = 600 × 110/100 = **₹660**.

### Example 5: CP of some = SP of others

*The cost price of 20 articles equals the selling price of 16 articles. Find the profit %.*

Profit % = (20 − 16)/16 × 100 = **25%**.

### Example 6: marked price and discount

*An article marked at ₹1,000 is sold at a 10% discount. The cost price is ₹750. Find the profit %.*

- SP = 1,000 × 90/100 = ₹900.
- Profit = 900 − 750 = ₹150.
- Profit % = 150/750 × 100 = **20%**.

### Example 7: false weight

*A shopkeeper sells at cost price but uses a 900 g weight instead of 1 kg. Find his gain %.*

100/900 × 100 = **11.11%**. (1/9 from the table.)

## Points to remember

- **Two successive discounts of 20% and 10% are not 30%.** They equal 20 + 10 − 2 = **28%**.
- **10% profit and 10% loss on the same SP is never "no profit no loss".** It is a 1% loss.
- When a question gives profit on SP (it sometimes does, to trap you), convert first: profit of 20% on SP is 25% on CP.
- Most answers here are "nice" fractions. If you get 16.66, look for 1/6. If you get 14.28, look for 1/7.

## Common mistakes

- Calculating discount on cost price. **Discount is on marked price.**
- Adding successive percentages directly.
- Using SP in the profit % denominator.
- Spending 3 minutes on one question. If the table doesn't give you a quick path within a minute, mark it and move on, then come back.
