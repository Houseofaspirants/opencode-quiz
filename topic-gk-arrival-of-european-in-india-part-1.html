Arrival of Europeans - Part 1: all 25 questions

Read the question, pick an option, then open the answer.

  1. Arrange the European powers in the order in which they arrived in India: Portuguese, Dutch, English, French.

    1. Portuguese–Dutch–English–French
    2. Dutch–Portuguese–French–English
    3. English–French–Portuguese–Dutch
    4. French–English–Dutch–Portuguese
    Show answer

    Answer: (A) Portuguese–Dutch–English–French

    Explanation: The correct option is supported because The arrival order given is Portuguese, Dutch, English and French. The other sequences reverse or rearrange this order. The Portuguese were both the first to arrive and the last to leave India.

  2. What nickname was given to Prince Henry of Portugal?

    1. The Conqueror
    2. The Lawgiver
    3. The Liberator
    4. The Navigator
    Show answer

    Answer: (D) The Navigator

    Explanation: The correct option is supported because Prince Henry was known as ‘the Navigator’ because finding a sea route to India became his obsession. The other titles are not given to Prince Henry in the source. European advances in shipbuilding and navigation supported the search for an ocean route to India.

  3. In which month and year did Vasco da Gama reach Calicut?

    1. August 1498
    2. December 1501
    3. May 1498
    4. May 1500
    Show answer

    Answer: (C) May 1498

    Explanation: The correct option is supported because Vasco da Gama reached Calicut in May 1498. 1500 belongs to Cabral’s arrival, while 1501 was Vasco da Gama’s second visit. He was the first European to reach India by sea according to the chapter.

  4. Who guided Vasco da Gama’s ships to Calicut?

    1. Francois Caron
    2. Abdul Majid
    3. Thomas Aldworth
    4. William Hedges
    Show answer

    Answer: (B) Abdul Majid

    Explanation: The correct option is supported because A Gujarati pilot named Abdul Majid guided the three ships to Calicut. Aldworth, Hedges and Caron are connected with English or French factories, not this voyage. The Zamorin, the local Hindu ruler of Calicut, welcomed Vasco da Gama.

  5. Who welcomed Vasco da Gama at Calicut?

    1. The Zamorin
    2. The Sultan of Bijapur
    3. Shah Shuja
    4. Farrukhsiyar
    Show answer

    Answer: (A) The Zamorin

    Explanation: The correct option is supported because The Zamorin, the local Hindu ruler of Calicut, welcomed Vasco da Gama. Bijapur’s Sultan is linked with Goa, Shah Shuja with Bengal, and Farrukhsiyar with the 1715 farmans. Calicut was the first Indian destination of Vasco da Gama’s sea voyage.

  6. Who was the second Portuguese to arrive in India and set up a factory at Calicut in 1500?

    1. Francisco de Almeida
    2. Alfonso de Albuquerque
    3. Nino da Cunha
    4. Pedro Alvarez Cabral
    Show answer

    Answer: (D) Pedro Alvarez Cabral

    Explanation: The correct option is supported because Pedro Alvarez Cabral came to Calicut in 1500, traded in spices and set up a factory there. Almeida was the first Portuguese governor, Albuquerque captured Goa, and Nino da Cunha shifted the headquarters. Vasco da Gama returned in 1501 and established factories at Cannanore, Calicut and Cochin.

  7. Which set correctly lists the Portuguese factories established by Vasco da Gama in 1501?

    1. Hooghly, Calcutta and Goa
    2. Daman, Diu and Nagapatnam
    3. Cannanore, Calicut and Cochin
    4. Surat, Masulipatnam and Pondicherry
    Show answer

    Answer: (C) Cannanore, Calicut and Cochin

    Explanation: The correct option is supported because Vasco da Gama established trading factories at Cannanore, Calicut and Cochin in 1501. The other sets combine places from English, Dutch or later Portuguese activity. These centres became important Portuguese trade centres.

  8. Consider the following statements about Francisco de Almeida: 1. He was the first Portuguese governor in India. 2. He is associated with the Blue Water Policy. Which of the statements is/are correct?

    1. Neither 1 nor 2
    2. 1 and 2
    3. 1 only
    4. 2 only
    Show answer

    Answer: (B) 1 and 2

    Explanation: The correct option is supported because Both statements are correct: Almeida was the first Portuguese governor and followed the Blue Water Policy, also called the cartaze system. The other options wrongly omit one or both correct statements. His task included strengthening Portuguese power and destroying Muslim trade.

  9. Who was regarded as the real founder of Portuguese power in India?

    1. Alfonso de Albuquerque
    2. Francisco de Almeida
    3. Pedro Alvarez Cabral
    4. Nino da Cunha
    Show answer

    Answer: (A) Alfonso de Albuquerque

    Explanation: The correct option is supported because Alfonso de Albuquerque was the real founder of Portuguese power in India. Almeida was the first governor; Cabral set up the Calicut factory; Nino da Cunha moved the headquarters. Albuquerque introduced the policy of Imperialism.

  10. In 1510, Albuquerque captured Goa from which power?

    1. The Sultan of Golconda
    2. The Mughal emperor
    3. The Zamorin of Calicut
    4. The Sultan of Bijapur
    Show answer

    Answer: (D) The Sultan of Bijapur

    Explanation: The correct option is supported because Albuquerque captured Goa from the Sultan of Bijapur in 1510. Golconda is associated with the Golden Farman, while the Zamorin ruled Calicut. Goa became the first piece of Indian territory under Europeans since the time of Alexander the Great, as stated in the chapter.

  11. Which crops were introduced by the Portuguese in Goa?

    1. Wheat and barley
    2. Cotton and indigo
    3. Tobacco and cashew nut
    4. Tea and coffee
    Show answer

    Answer: (C) Tobacco and cashew nut

    Explanation: The correct option is supported because The Portuguese introduced tobacco and cashew nut in Goa. Tea and coffee are not the crops stated here; cotton and indigo are listed among Dutch trade items. Albuquerque is linked with the policy of Imperialism.

  12. Who became governor of Portuguese interests in India in November 1529?

    1. Francois Martin
    2. Nino da Cunha
    3. Alfonso de Albuquerque
    4. Francisco de Almeida
    Show answer

    Answer: (B) Nino da Cunha

    Explanation: The correct option is supported because Nino da Cunha became governor of Portuguese interests in India in November 1529. Albuquerque and Almeida held earlier Portuguese roles, while Martin developed Pondicherry for the French. About a year later, Nino da Cunha shifted the headquarters from Cochin to Goa.

  13. Which statement is correct about the Portuguese settlements on the east coast?

    1. They established military posts at San Thome and Nagapatnam.
    2. They established their first factory at Masulipatnam in 1605.
    3. They founded Pondicherry in 1674.
    4. They fortified Fort William in 1700.
    Show answer

    Answer: (A) They established military posts at San Thome and Nagapatnam.

    Explanation: The correct option is supported because The Portuguese established military posts and settlements at San Thome in Chennai and Nagapatnam in Tamil Nadu. Masulipatnam was the Dutch first factory, Pondicherry was French, and Fort William was English. Portuguese power covered many coastal parts within fifty years of Vasco da Gama’s arrival.

  14. In which year did the Government of India take back Goa, Daman and Diu?

    1. 1947
    2. 1950
    3. 1971
    4. 1961
    Show answer

    Answer: (D) 1961

    Explanation: The correct option is supported because The Government of India took back Goa, Daman and Diu in 1961. 1947 marks Independence, 1950 marks the Constitution coming into force, and 1971 is not the stated year. The Portuguese were the first Europeans to arrive and the last to leave India.

  15. Captain William Hawkins reached Surat in 1608 on which ship?

    1. Mayflower
    2. Victoria
    3. Hector
    4. Red Dragon
    Show answer

    Answer: (C) Hector

    Explanation: The correct option is supported because William Hawkins reached Surat in 1608 on his ship Hector. The other ship names are not associated with Hawkins in the chapter. He carried a letter from James I to Jahangir seeking permission to trade.

  16. What mansab and salary did Jahangir grant William Hawkins?

    1. Mansab of 700 and Rs 40,000
    2. Mansab of 400 and Rs 30,000
    3. Mansab of 500 and Rs 20,000
    4. Mansab of 300 and Rs 10,000
    Show answer

    Answer: (B) Mansab of 400 and Rs 30,000

    Explanation: The correct option is supported because Pleased with Hawkins’s Turkish, Jahangir made him a mansabdar of 400 with a salary of Rs 30,000. The other mansab and salary combinations do not match the grant stated for Hawkins. Hawkins spoke to Jahangir without an interpreter.

  17. Which statement is NOT correct?

    1. During Shah Jahan’s reign, the Portuguese retained their advantages at the Mughal court forever.
    2. Hawkins reached Surat in 1608.
    3. Jahangir granted Hawkins a mansab of 400.
    4. The Portuguese were offended by the facilities given to the English.
    Show answer

    Answer: (A) During Shah Jahan’s reign, the Portuguese retained their advantages at the Mughal court forever.

    Explanation: The correct option is supported because The incorrect statement is that the Portuguese retained their advantages forever; during Shah Jahan’s reign they lost them permanently. The other three statements are directly supported by the chapter’s account of Hawkins and Portuguese decline. The English trading facilities had angered the Portuguese.

  18. Who was the first Dutchman to reach the East in 1596?

    1. John Surman
    2. Sir Thomas Roe
    3. Captain Middleton
    4. Cornelis de Houtman
    Show answer

    Answer: (D) Cornelis de Houtman

    Explanation: The correct option is supported because Cornelis de Houtman was the first Dutchman to reach the East in 1596, including Sumatra and Bantam. Surman, Roe and Middleton are associated with English missions or trade permissions. The Dutch established their East India Company in 1602.

  19. In 1602, who merged nearly 96 Dutch trading companies into an East India Company?

    1. The Mughal court
    2. The Sultan of Golconda
    3. The States-General of the Netherlands
    4. The English Parliament
    Show answer

    Answer: (C) The States-General of the Netherlands

    Explanation: The correct option is supported because The States-General of the Netherlands merged nearly 96 trading companies in 1602. The other bodies are not identified as the authority behind this Dutch merger. The Dutch company could wage war, sign treaties, take territory and build forts.

  20. Where did the Dutch establish their first factory in India in 1605?

    1. Pondicherry
    2. Masulipatnam
    3. Surat
    4. Calicut
    Show answer

    Answer: (B) Masulipatnam

    Explanation: The correct option is supported because The Dutch established their first factory at Masulipatnam in Andhra in 1605. Surat was the first English and French factory site in the chapter, while Pondicherry was French. Dutch main trade items included silk, cotton, indigo, rice and opium.

  21. Which set contains only the main Dutch trade items listed in the chapter?

    1. Silk, cotton, indigo, rice and opium
    2. Tobacco, cashew nut, tea, coffee and sugar
    3. Spices, wool, salt, silver and horses
    4. Gold, diamonds, pepper, tea and silk
    Show answer

    Answer: (A) Silk, cotton, indigo, rice and opium

    Explanation: The correct option is supported because The listed Dutch trade items are silk, cotton, indigo, rice and opium. The other sets add crops or commodities not listed as the Dutch group in the chapter. The Dutch were primarily interested in trade rather than building an empire in India.

  22. What was the result of the 1667 compromise between the Dutch and the English?

    1. The Dutch gave up Indonesia and the English withdrew from India.
    2. Both companies divided Bengal equally.
    3. The French received Pondicherry from the English.
    4. The English gave up claims on Indonesia and the Dutch withdrew from India.
    Show answer

    Answer: (D) The English gave up claims on Indonesia and the Dutch withdrew from India.

    Explanation: The correct option is supported because In 1667, the English gave up claims on Indonesia and the Dutch withdrew from India to focus on Indonesia. The other options reverse the arrangement or introduce events not connected with this compromise. Indonesia offered the Dutch more profitable trade than India.

  23. The Battle of Hooghly, also called Bedara, was fought in which year?

    1. 1763
    2. 1708
    3. 1759
    4. 1748
    Show answer

    Answer: (C) 1759

    Explanation: The correct option is supported because The English defeated the Dutch in the Battle of Hooghly or Bedara in November 1759. 1748 is linked with the end of the First Carnatic War, 1763 with the Third Carnatic War, and 1708 with the English company merger. The defeat was a crushing blow to Dutch ambitions in India.

  24. Assertion (A): The Dutch did not build a lasting empire in India. Reason (R): Their main concern in India was trade.

    1. A is false but R is true
    2. Both A and R are true and R is the correct explanation of A
    3. Both A and R are true but R is not the correct explanation of A
    4. A is true but R is false
    Show answer

    Answer: (B) Both A and R are true and R is the correct explanation of A

    Explanation: The correct option is supported because Both statements are true, and the Dutch focus on trade explains why they were not interested in building an empire in India. The other options deny either the truth of A, the truth of R, or the explanatory link between them. The English victory at Bedara further weakened Dutch ambitions in India.

  25. In 1599, what was the name of the group of English merchants that formed a company?

    1. Merchant Adventurers
    2. United Company of Merchants
    3. Compagnie des Indes Orientales
    4. States-General
    Show answer

    Answer: (A) Merchant Adventurers

    Explanation: The correct option is supported because A group of English merchants called the Merchant Adventurers formed a company in 1599. The United Company was the 1708 merged company, the Compagnie was French, and States-General was Dutch. The English company received its royal charter on 31 December 1600.
