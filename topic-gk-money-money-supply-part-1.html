Question bank

Money & Money Supply - Part 1: all 18 questions

Read the question, pick an option, then open the answer.

  1. Which statement best defines money?

    1. Anything commonly accepted as a medium of exchange
    2. Any asset that earns interest
    3. Any government security
    4. Any item with intrinsic value
    Show answer

    Answer: (A) Anything commonly accepted as a medium of exchange

    Explanation: Money is anything commonly accepted as a medium of exchange. An interest-bearing asset, a government security, or an intrinsically valuable item is not necessarily money. Money can be classified by its forms and by money-supply aggregates.

  2. A coin whose face value equals the value of the metal contained in it is called:

    1. Token money
    2. Full-bodied coin
    3. Legal tender money
    4. Fiduciary money
    Show answer

    Answer: (B) Full-bodied coin

    Explanation: A full-bodied coin has face value equal to the value of its metal content. Token money has a face value greater than its metal value. Old gold and silver coins are examples of full-bodied coins.

  3. Which statement about coins in India is correct?

    1. They are full-bodied coins
    2. They are commodity money
    3. They are token money
    4. They are fiduciary money
    Show answer

    Answer: (C) They are token money

    Explanation: All coins in India are token money. Their face value is greater than the value of the metal in them. Full-bodied coins instead have equal face and metal values.

  4. Which is an example of paper money?

    1. A ₹10 coin
    2. A cheque
    3. A government bond
    4. A ₹100 note
    Show answer

    Answer: (D) A ₹100 note

    Explanation: A ₹100 note is paper money issued by the central bank or government. A ₹10 coin is metallic token money, while a cheque is bank money. Government bonds are mentioned as near-money assets.

  5. Which instrument is an unconditional order by a customer to the bank to pay a stated sum?

    1. Cheque
    2. Draft
    3. Bill of exchange
    4. Government bond
    Show answer

    Answer: (A) Cheque

    Explanation: A cheque is the customer’s unconditional order to the bank to pay a stated sum to the customer or another party. A draft is drawn by a bank, and a bill of exchange is an order by the drawer to the drawee. These are listed under bank or credit money.

  6. A draft is best described as:

    1. A coin whose face value exceeds metal value
    2. A cheque drawn by a bank on its own branch or another bank
    3. A customer’s order to the bank
    4. An intrinsically valuable commodity used in exchange
    Show answer

    Answer: (B) A cheque drawn by a bank on its own branch or another bank

    Explanation: A draft is a cheque drawn by a bank on its own branch or another bank, payable on demand to the named person. A customer’s order is a cheque. The other alternatives describe token money and commodity money.

  7. Consider the following statements about legal tender money:1.It must be accepted for payment and discharge of debt.2.All notes and coins issued by the government and central bank are legal tender.Which of the above is/are correct?

    1. 1 only
    2. 2 only
    3. Both 1 and 2
    4. Neither 1 nor 2
    Show answer

    Answer: (C) Both 1 and 2

    Explanation: Both statements are correct. Legal tender must be accepted for payment and discharge of debt. Fiduciary money differs because it is not declared legal tender.

  8. Which pair is correctly matched?

    1. Plastic money — credit cards and smart cards
    2. Near money — all notes and coins
    3. Commodity money — deposits and government bonds
    4. Legal tender money — only cheques
    Show answer

    Answer: (A) Plastic money — credit cards and smart cards

    Explanation: Plastic money includes credit cards and smart cards. Deposits and government bonds are near money, while cheques are not legal tender. Commodity money is a commodity with intrinsic value used in exchange.

  9. The standard money of India is:

    1. Gold
    2. Rupee
    3. Government bond
    4. Demand deposit
    Show answer

    Answer: (B) Rupee

    Explanation: The Rupee is the standard money of India. Gold is an example of commodity money. Government bonds and demand deposits belong to other classifications.

  10. Which of the following is NOT a feature of fiduciary money?

    1. Its value depends on confidence in general acceptance
    2. It is not declared legal tender
    3. Its issuer promises exchange on request
    4. It has intrinsic value as a commodity
    Show answer

    Answer: (D) It has intrinsic value as a commodity

    Explanation: Fiduciary money does not require intrinsic commodity value. Its value rests on confidence, it is not legal tender, and its issuer promises exchange on request. Cheques, banknotes and drafts are examples of fiduciary money.

  11. Which item is an example of commodity money?

    1. Salt
    2. Credit card
    3. Cheque
    4. Time deposit
    Show answer

    Answer: (A) Salt

    Explanation: Salt is listed as commodity money because it has intrinsic value and is used as a medium of exchange. A credit card is plastic money, a cheque is bank money, and a time deposit is part of a money-supply aggregate. Gold and silver are other examples of commodity money.

  12. Money supply refers to:

    1. Only currency notes issued by RBI
    2. Total monetary assets circulating in an economy at a given time
    3. Only demand deposits with commercial banks
    4. Only government borrowing in an economy
    Show answer

    Answer: (B) Total monetary assets circulating in an economy at a given time

    Explanation: Money supply is the total quantity of monetary assets circulating in an economy at a given time. It includes money used by individuals, businesses and governments for transactions, investment and saving. It affects inflation, interest rates and overall economic activity.

  13. M0 is also known as:

    1. Narrow money
    2. Broad money
    3. The broadest measure
    4. Reserve money or high-powered money
    Show answer

    Answer: (D) Reserve money or high-powered money

    Explanation: M0 is reserve money, high-powered money, base money or the monetary base. It includes currency in circulation plus commercial-bank reserves with the central bank. M1 is narrow money, whereas M3 is called broad money.

  14. Which components together constitute M1?

    1. Currency in circulation + commercial-bank reserves with the central bank
    2. M1 + savings deposits + time deposits + money market mutual funds
    3. Currency in circulation + demand deposits
    4. M3 + all other deposits
    Show answer

    Answer: (C) Currency in circulation + demand deposits

    Explanation: M1 equals currency in circulation plus demand deposits. It is called narrow money and is the most liquid aggregate. The other alternatives describe M0, M2 and M4 respectively.

  15. Which aggregate includes M1, savings deposits, time deposits and money market mutual funds?

    1. M0
    2. M2
    3. M3
    4. M4
    Show answer

    Answer: (B) M2

    Explanation: M2 includes M1, savings deposits, time deposits and money market mutual funds. M0 is the monetary base, while M4 adds all other deposits to M3. M2 is classified as broad money.

  16. M3 consists of:

    1. M0 plus all other deposits
    2. M1 plus only demand deposits
    3. M2 plus large time deposits, institutional money market funds and other large liquid assets
    4. M3 plus all other deposits
    Show answer

    Answer: (C) M2 plus large time deposits, institutional money market funds and other large liquid assets

    Explanation: M3 includes M2 plus large time deposits, institutional money market funds and other large liquid assets. The final alternative defines M4. M3 is identified as broad money.

  17. Which money-supply measure is described as the broadest measure?

    1. M4
    2. M3
    3. M2
    4. M1
    Show answer

    Answer: (A) M4

    Explanation: M4 is the broadest measure of money supply. It comprises M3 plus all other deposits. M1 is the narrow and most liquid measure, while M3 is broad money.

  18. Which institution has the authority to issue currency notes and is the principal supplier of money?

    1. RBI
    2. Commercial banks
    3. Ministry of Finance
    4. Money market mutual funds
    Show answer

    Answer: (A) RBI

    Explanation: RBI has the authority to issue currency notes and is the principal supplier of money. It influences money supply using monetary-policy tools. The Ministry of Finance has the separate role of issuing the ₹1 note and all coins.
