About this subject

All about Quantitative Aptitude

What this page holds and how to work through it.

Quant is the section candidates postpone, which is exactly why it decides the cutoff. The topics themselves are small - simplification, percentage, profit and loss, time-speed-distance, averages, simple and compound interest, data interpretation - but each one rewards a method you can run in under a minute rather than a formula you can recite slowly.

Work these sets as drills, not as study. Attempt one short set untimed, read every explanation even when you were right, then repeat the same set against the clock two days later. The questions you get wrong twice are your syllabus; everything else is revision you do not need yet.

Quantitative Aptitude on House of Aspirants publishes as separate, self-contained sets rather than one endless paper. Each set runs 30 seconds per question with four options and a written explanation, so a wrong answer teaches the next attempt instead of only scoring it.

Work it in three moves: open the daily quiz (20 questions) to find the lane that is costing you marks, run a topic set when you want depth, and keep a full mock test for the weekend. A set is cleared at 40% accuracy, and your subject-wise accuracy is tracked on the progress page.

A wrong answer lands in your bookmarks together with its explanation, so revision becomes a second pass over the same set instead of a separate chapter of notes.

These are the papers Quantitative Aptitude shows up in most: Punjab Police Sub-Inspector (SI), Punjab Police Intelligence Assistant, Patwari, Naib Tehsildar, PSPCL jobs. Written guides on the portal cover the same ground: Punjab Police Exam Preparation: Constable, ASI & SI Strategy; Reasoning and Quant: Speed, Accuracy & MCQ Drills.
