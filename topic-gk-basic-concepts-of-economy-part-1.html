Basic Concepts of Economy - Part 1: all 18 questions

Read the question, pick an option, then open the answer.

  1. Which statement best defines an economic system?

    1. A mechanism for allocating available services, resources and commodities
    2. A method used only to collect taxes
    3. A system concerned only with foreign trade
    4. A process limited to manufacturing goods
    Show answer

    Answer: (A) A mechanism for allocating available services, resources and commodities

    Explanation: An economic system plans and allocates available services, resources and commodities across a country. Tax collection, foreign trade and manufacturing are activities within an economy, but none alone defines the whole system. This distinction is useful for matching concepts accurately in competitive examinations.

  2. In a capitalist economy, allocation is primarily controlled by:

    1. The government
    2. Market forces of demand and supply
    3. Public-sector enterprises
    4. International organisations
    Show answer

    Answer: (B) Market forces of demand and supply

    Explanation: A capitalist economy is controlled by market forces of demand and supply. Government control characterises socialism, while public enterprises and international organisations do not define capitalist allocation. This distinction is useful for matching concepts accurately in competitive examinations.

  3. Which economic system is also known as a controlled economy?

    1. Socialist economy
    2. Capitalist economy
    3. Mixed economy
    4. Monopolistic competition
    Show answer

    Answer: (A) Socialist economy

    Explanation: A socialist economy is government-controlled and is also called a controlled economy. Capitalist and mixed economies have different controlling arrangements, while monopolistic competition is a market form. This distinction is useful for matching concepts accurately in competitive examinations.

  4. India follows which economic system according to the notes?

    1. Capitalist economy
    2. Socialist economy
    3. Command economy
    4. Mixed economy
    Show answer

    Answer: (D) Mixed economy

    Explanation: India follows a mixed economy, where both market and government features operate. Capitalist and socialist economies are controlled primarily by one side, and command economy is not the term used for India in the notes. This distinction is useful for matching concepts accurately in competitive examinations.

  5. Which pair is correctly matched?

    1. Capitalist economy — Free economy
    2. Socialist economy — Dual economic system
    3. Mixed economy — Controlled economy
    4. Monopoly — Service sector
    Show answer

    Answer: (A) Capitalist economy — Free economy

    Explanation: The notes identify capitalist economy as a free economy. Socialist economy is controlled, mixed economy is dual, and monopoly is a market type rather than a sector. This distinction is useful for matching concepts accurately in competitive examinations.

  6. Which activity belongs to the primary sector?

    1. Mining
    2. Steel manufacturing
    3. Banking
    4. Restaurant service
    Show answer

    Answer: (A) Mining

    Explanation: Mining depends directly on natural resources and belongs to the primary sector. Steel manufacturing is secondary, while banking and restaurant service belong to the tertiary sector. This distinction is useful for matching concepts accurately in competitive examinations.

  7. A sector that converts raw material into a valuable product is the:

    1. Primary sector
    2. Tertiary sector
    3. Secondary sector
    4. External sector
    Show answer

    Answer: (C) Secondary sector

    Explanation: The secondary sector converts raw materials into valuable products. Primary sector uses natural resources, tertiary sector provides services, and external sector covers international dealings. This distinction is useful for matching concepts accurately in competitive examinations.

  8. Banking and insurance are examples of the:

    1. Agricultural sector
    2. Manufacturing sector
    3. External sector
    4. Tertiary sector
    Show answer

    Answer: (D) Tertiary sector

    Explanation: Banking and insurance involve production and exchange of services, so they are tertiary activities. Agricultural sector is primary, manufacturing is secondary, and external sector concerns dealings with the rest of the world. This distinction is useful for matching concepts accurately in competitive examinations.

  9. Consider the following statements about sectors by activity: 1. Primary sector depends directly on the environment or natural resources. 2. Secondary sector is also called the manufacturing sector. 3. Tertiary sector produces and exchanges services. Which of the statements given above are correct?

    1. 1 and 2 only
    2. 2 and 3 only
    3. 1, 2 and 3
    4. 1 and 3 only
    Show answer

    Answer: (C) 1, 2 and 3

    Explanation: All three statements accurately describe the primary, secondary and tertiary sectors. The primary sector is resource-based, the secondary sector is manufacturing, and the tertiary sector concerns services. This distinction is useful for matching concepts accurately in competitive examinations.

  10. Demand for which type of good rises when consumer income rises?

    1. Inferior good
    2. Normal good
    3. Giffen good
    4. Public good
    Show answer

    Answer: (B) Normal good

    Explanation: Demand for a normal good increases when income increases. Inferior-good demand falls as income rises, Giffen good is defined by a price-demand relation, and public good is not a category discussed here. This distinction is useful for matching concepts accurately in competitive examinations.

  11. A good whose demand rises by a larger percentage than income is called a:

    1. Normal good
    2. Inferior good
    3. Public good
    4. Luxury good
    Show answer

    Answer: (D) Luxury good

    Explanation: A luxury good has demand that rises by a bigger percentage as income rises. A normal good merely rises with income, an inferior good falls with income, and public good is not the classification used in the notes. This distinction is useful for matching concepts accurately in competitive examinations.

  12. When income rises, demand for a low-quality good falls. Such a good is called:

    1. Giffen good
    2. Luxury good
    3. Normal good
    4. Inferior good
    Show answer

    Answer: (D) Inferior good

    Explanation: An inferior good is a low-quality good for which demand falls as income rises. Giffen good concerns price movement, luxury-good demand rises more than income, and normal-good demand rises with income. This distinction is useful for matching concepts accurately in competitive examinations.

  13. Which good is a rare type of highly inferior good?

    1. Normal good
    2. Giffen good
    3. Luxury good
    4. Merit good
    Show answer

    Answer: (B) Giffen good

    Explanation: A Giffen good is described as a rare type of highly inferior good. Normal and luxury goods have different income-demand responses, while merit good is not used in these notes. This distinction is useful for matching concepts accurately in competitive examinations.

  14. Assertion (A): A Giffen good is an exception to the law of demand. Reason (R): For a Giffen good, price and demand move in the same direction.

    1. Both A and R are true and R is the correct explanation of A
    2. Both A and R are true but R is not the correct explanation of A
    3. A is true but R is false
    4. A is false but R is true
    Show answer

    Answer: (A) Both A and R are true and R is the correct explanation of A

    Explanation: Both statements are true, and the same-direction movement of price and demand explains the exception. The other alternatives incorrectly deny either the truth of the statements or their explanatory relationship. This distinction is useful for matching concepts accurately in competitive examinations.

  15. A market may be physical or virtual because it is a place where:

    1. Buyers and sellers meet to trade goods and services
    2. Only producers decide tax rates
    3. Only government agencies allocate grants
    4. Only households save money
    Show answer

    Answer: (A) Buyers and sellers meet to trade goods and services

    Explanation: A market is where buyers and sellers meet to trade goods and services, whether face to face or online. Tax decisions, grant allocation and household saving are economic activities but do not define a market. This distinction is useful for matching concepts accurately in competitive examinations.

  16. Which feature is associated with perfect competition?

    1. A single seller fixes the price
    2. Products are slightly differentiated
    3. Firms face entry barriers
    4. No single buyer or seller can affect the price
    Show answer

    Answer: (D) No single buyer or seller can affect the price

    Explanation: In perfect competition, no individual buyer or seller can influence price. A single price-setter describes monopoly, slight differentiation describes monopolistic competition, and entry barriers are associated with oligopoly in the notes. This distinction is useful for matching concepts accurately in competitive examinations.

  17. Which market form is regarded in the notes as the most realistic?

    1. Perfect competition
    2. Monopolistic competition
    3. Oligopoly
    4. Monopoly
    Show answer

    Answer: (B) Monopolistic competition

    Explanation: Monopolistic competition is described as the most realistic market type. Perfect competition is hypothetical, oligopoly is dominated by a few firms, and monopoly has one seller. This distinction is useful for matching concepts accurately in competitive examinations.

  18. The automobile industry and telecom sector are examples closest to:

    1. Perfect competition
    2. Monopolistic competition
    3. Oligopoly
    4. Monopoly
    Show answer

    Answer: (C) Oligopoly

    Explanation: The notes use automobile industry and telecom as examples of oligopoly. Perfect competition has very many sellers, monopolistic competition has differentiated products, and monopoly has only one seller. This distinction is useful for matching concepts accurately in competitive examinations.
