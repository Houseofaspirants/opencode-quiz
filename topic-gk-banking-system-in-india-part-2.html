Banking System in India - Part 2: all 15 questions

Read the question, pick an option, then open the answer.

  1. Arrange the following banking milestones in chronological order:1.Establishment of RRBs2.First major bank nationalisation3.Second major bank nationalisation4.Narasimham Committee for banking reforms

    1. 2-1-3-4
    2. 1-2-3-4
    3. 2-3-1-4
    4. 2-1-4-3
    Show answer

    Answer: (A) 2-1-3-4

    Explanation: The correct sequence is 1969, 1975, 1980 and 1991. Fourteen banks were nationalised in 1969, RRBs were set up in 1975, and six more banks were nationalised in 1980. The Narasimham Committee for banking reforms came in 1991.

  2. Which commission recommended the setting up of RBI?

    1. Gorwala Committee
    2. Hilton Young Commission
    3. Khan Committee
    4. Verma Committee
    Show answer

    Answer: (B) Hilton Young Commission

    Explanation: The Hilton Young Commission recommended the setting up of RBI. The Reserve Bank of India Act, 1934 gave effect to the RBI's legal framework. Gorwala Committee is linked with the formation of SBI, while Khan and Verma Committees relate to banking reforms.

  3. RBI started functioning on which date?

    1. 1 April 1934
    2. 1 April 1935
    3. 1 July 1935
    4. 1 January 1949
    Show answer

    Answer: (B) 1 April 1935

    Explanation: RBI started functioning on 1 April 1935. The Reserve Bank of India Act was passed in 1934. The remaining dates do not mark the commencement of RBI's functioning.

  4. Consider the following statements about the Reserve Bank of India Act, 1934:1.It gave RBI power over monetary policy.2.It gave RBI power over currency issue.3.It gave RBI power over bank supervision.Which of the statements given above are correct?

    1. 1 and 2 only
    2. 1 and 3 only
    3. 2 and 3 only
    4. 1, 2 and 3
    Show answer

    Answer: (D) 1, 2 and 3

    Explanation: All three statements are correct. The RBI Act gave RBI powers over monetary policy, currency issue and bank supervision. RBI began functioning on 1 April 1935 under this statutory framework.

  5. Which law replaced FERA, 1973?

    1. Banking Regulation Act, 1949
    2. State Bank of India Act, 1955
    3. Foreign Exchange Management Act, 1999
    4. Banking Regulation (Amendment) Act, 2020
    Show answer

    Answer: (C) Foreign Exchange Management Act, 1999

    Explanation: The Foreign Exchange Management Act, 1999 replaced FERA, 1973. FEMA provides the legal framework for foreign-exchange transactions. The other options relate to bank regulation, SBI, or cooperative-bank supervision.

  6. Assertion (A): FEMA provides a legal framework for foreign-exchange transactions.Reason (R): FEMA was passed in 1999.

    1. Both A and R are true and R is the correct explanation of A
    2. Both A and R are true but R is not the correct explanation of A
    3. A is true but R is false
    4. A is false but R is true
    Show answer

    Answer: (B) Both A and R are true but R is not the correct explanation of A

    Explanation: Both the assertion and reason are true, but the year of passage does not explain FEMA's purpose. FEMA was passed in 1999 and regulates foreign-exchange transactions. It replaced FERA, 1973.

  7. Which Act brought cooperative banks under RBI supervision by giving RBI more power over them?

    1. Banking Regulation Act, 1949
    2. State Bank of India Act, 1955
    3. Foreign Exchange Management Act, 1999
    4. Banking Regulation (Amendment) Act, 2020
    Show answer

    Answer: (D) Banking Regulation (Amendment) Act, 2020

    Explanation: The Banking Regulation (Amendment) Act, 2020 gave RBI more power over cooperative banks and brought them under RBI supervision. The 1949 Act regulates banking generally. The SBI Act concerns SBI, while FEMA deals with foreign exchange.

  8. The First Narasimham Committee on banking-sector reforms was set up in which year?

    1. 1988
    2. 1991
    3. 1997
    4. 1998
    Show answer

    Answer: (B) 1991

    Explanation: The First Narasimham Committee on banking-sector reforms was set up in 1991. Narasimham Committee is linked with banking-sector reforms in both 1991 and 1998. The later year relates to the Second Narasimham Committee.

  9. Arrange the following banking reform committees in chronological order:1.Verma Committee2.Khan Committee3.First Narasimham Committee4.Second Narasimham Committee

    1. 3-2-4-1
    2. 2-3-4-1
    3. 3-4-2-1
    4. 3-2-1-4
    Show answer

    Answer: (A) 3-2-4-1

    Explanation: The correct order is First Narasimham Committee in 1991, Khan Committee in 1997, Second Narasimham Committee in 1998, and Verma Committee in 1999. These committees are listed as banking-sector reform committees. Their sequence follows their respective years.

  10. Consider the following pairs:1.Khan Committee - 19972.Second Narasimham Committee - 19983.Verma Committee - 19994.First Narasimham Committee - 1991Which of the pairs given above are correctly matched?

    1. 1 and 2 only
    2. 1, 2 and 3 only
    3. 2, 3 and 4 only
    4. 1, 2, 3 and 4
    Show answer

    Answer: (D) 1, 2, 3 and 4

    Explanation: All four pairs are correctly matched. The listed committee years are 1997 for Khan, 1998 for the Second Narasimham, 1999 for Verma and 1991 for the First Narasimham Committee. Narasimham Committee is specifically associated with banking-sector reforms.

  11. Consider the following statements about banking-sector reforms in 1991:1.The Narasimham Committee was set up for banking reforms.2.RBI gave licences to 10 private-sector banks.3.Foreign banks could form joint ventures with Indian banks.Which of the statements given above are correct?

    1. 1 and 2 only
    2. 1 and 3 only
    3. 2 and 3 only
    4. 1, 2 and 3
    Show answer

    Answer: (D) 1, 2 and 3

    Explanation: All three statements are correct. In 1991, the Narasimham Committee was set up for banking reforms. RBI also gave licences to 10 private-sector banks, and foreign banks could form joint ventures with Indian banks.

  12. In which year was SEBI given statutory powers to regulate the securities market?

    1. 1988
    2. 1991
    3. 1992
    4. 1999
    Show answer

    Answer: (C) 1992

    Explanation: SEBI was given statutory powers to regulate the securities market in 1992. It had been set up in 1988. The years 1991 and 1999 are linked with banking reforms and FEMA respectively.

  13. Assertion (A): SEBI was set up before it received statutory powers.Reason (R): SEBI was set up in 1988 and received statutory powers in 1992.

    1. Both A and R are true and R is the correct explanation of A
    2. Both A and R are true but R is not the correct explanation of A
    3. A is true but R is false
    4. A is false but R is true
    Show answer

    Answer: (A) Both A and R are true and R is the correct explanation of A

    Explanation: Both the assertion and reason are true, and the reason correctly explains the assertion. SEBI was set up in 1988 and received statutory powers in 1992. Those powers enabled it to regulate the securities market.

  14. Which Act established SBI and provided the legal framework for it and its subsidiaries?

    1. Reserve Bank of India Act, 1934
    2. Banking Regulation Act, 1949
    3. State Bank of India Act, 1955
    4. Foreign Exchange Management Act, 1999
    Show answer

    Answer: (C) State Bank of India Act, 1955

    Explanation: The State Bank of India Act, 1955 established SBI and provided the legal framework for SBI and its subsidiaries. The RBI Act concerns RBI, and the Banking Regulation Act regulates banks generally. FEMA governs foreign-exchange transactions.

  15. Which bank was the first commercial bank fully owned by Indians?

    1. Allahabad Bank
    2. Bank of Hindustan
    3. Oudh Commercial Bank
    4. Punjab National Bank
    Show answer

    Answer: (C) Oudh Commercial Bank

    Explanation: Oudh Commercial Bank was the first commercial bank fully owned by Indians. It existed from 1881 to 1958. Allahabad Bank was the first commercial bank of India, while Bank of Hindustan was the first bank of India.
