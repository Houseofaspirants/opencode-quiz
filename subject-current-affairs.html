About this subject

All about Current Affairs

What this page holds and how to work through it.

Current affairs is the only section with an expiry date. National and international events, sports results, awards and Punjab's own decisions stop mattering the moment the paper moves on, which is why reading once is not enough: the material has to come back as questions before it becomes recall.

Treat it as a habit rather than a chapter. Attempt the daily quiz, keep a one-line note per event, then revise the notes as MCQs at the end of the week. The Expected MCQs habit in particular trains you to guess what an examiner will ask rather than what a magazine will print.

Current Affairs on House of Aspirants publishes as separate, self-contained sets rather than one endless paper. Each set runs 30 seconds per question with four options and a written explanation, so a wrong answer teaches the next attempt instead of only scoring it.

Work it in three moves: open the daily quiz (20 questions) to find the lane that is costing you marks, run a topic set when you want depth, and keep a full mock test for the weekend. A set is cleared at 40% accuracy, and your subject-wise accuracy is tracked on the progress page.

A wrong answer lands in your bookmarks together with its explanation, so revision becomes a second pass over the same set instead of a separate chapter of notes.

These are the papers Current Affairs shows up in most: Punjab Police Constable, Excise Inspector. Written guides on the portal cover the same ground: Punjab Police Exam Preparation: Constable, ASI & SI Strategy; Punjab Current Affairs: Daily Preparation Strategy.
