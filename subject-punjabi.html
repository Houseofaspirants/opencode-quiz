About this subject

All about Punjabi

What this page holds and how to work through it.

Punjabi is the section most candidates treat as a formality and then lose marks in anyway. The paper is rarely hard - grammar rules, muhavare, akhan, vocabulary and a short run through literature - it is simply unprepared, which shows up as a minute spent per question on something that should take ten seconds.

Practise it the way you would practise reasoning: small, frequent and self-correcting. Attempt a set, read the explanation behind every wrong answer, and keep the idiom and muhavara notes to one line each. Within a fortnight the same questions stop being reading and start being recognition.

Punjabi on House of Aspirants publishes as separate, self-contained sets across 21 category lanes - Punjabi Bhasha (Language), Gurmukhi Lipi (Script), Shabad Bodh (Word Knowledge), Punjabi Noun (Naanv), Punjabi Gender (Ling), Punjabi Number (Vachan), Punjabi Case (Karak), Punjabi Pronoun (Parnaanv), Punjabi Adjective (Visheshan), Punjabi Adverb (Kiriya Visheshan), Punjabi Postposition (Sambandhak), Punjabi Conjunction (Yojak), Punjabi Interjection (Vismik), Punjabi Compound Words (Samas), Punjabi Sentence (Vaak Bodh), Punjabi Correct Spelling (Shudh Ashudh), Punjabi Idioms (Muhavare), Punjabi Synonyms & Antonyms, Punjabi Proverbs (Akhaan), English to Punjabi Translation, Punjabi One Word Substitution (Shabad Badal) - rather than one endless paper. Each set is untimed, every question comes with four options and a written explanation, so a wrong answer teaches the next attempt instead of only scoring it.

Work it in three moves: open the daily quiz (20 questions) to find the lane that is costing you marks, run a topic set when you want depth, and keep a full mock test for the weekend. A set is cleared at 40% accuracy, and your subject-wise accuracy is tracked on the progress page.

A wrong answer lands in your bookmarks together with its explanation, so revision becomes a second pass over the same set instead of a separate chapter of notes.

These are the papers Punjabi shows up in most: Punjab Police Constable, Punjab Police ASI, Punjab Police Sub-Inspector (SI), Punjab Police Jail Warder, Patwari. Written guides on the portal cover the same ground: Punjab Police Exam Preparation: Constable, ASI & SI Strategy.

Exam relevance

Exams that lean on Punjabi

Preparation papers where this subject carries real weight.

Punjab Police Constable

The highest-volume recruitment on this list. Build your base with Punjab GK and current affairs, then drill reasoning until speed is automatic.

Punjab Police ASI

Assistant Sub-Inspector papers separate candidates on English and Punjabi accuracy as much as on general awareness. Practise language daily.

Punjab Police Sub-Inspector (SI)

The most competitive paper here. Polity, history and quant reasoning decide the rank - so take full timed mocks before the exam.

Punjab Police Jail Warder

A tighter syllabus: general awareness, Punjabi and basic reasoning. Perfect for a daily quiz habit - short sessions, high consistency.

Patwari

Revenue work starts with Punjab geography, history and current affairs. Add quant for the sections that reward calculation speed.

Excise Inspector

General awareness and English carry this paper. Track Punjab policy news daily and revise it as MCQs, not as notes.

Senior Assistant

Office procedure, computer applications and both languages. Computer MCQs are the highest-yield block - practise them until they are free marks.

Clerk

Grammar, comprehension, typing and basic computer knowledge. Accuracy beats speed here - review every wrong answer.

PSSSB

PSSSB recruitment. Whichever post you are writing, general awareness, Punjabi and reasoning are the shared subjects you can practise here as daily MCQs.

Punjab Police Head Constable

The communication and clerical tier of Punjab Police. Punjabi, English and general knowledge carry the paper, and computer awareness is worth real marks.
