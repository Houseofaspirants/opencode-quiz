Question bank

Money & Money Supply - Part 2: all 17 questions

Read the question, pick an option, then open the answer.

  1. Which is NOT listed as a monetary-policy tool through which RBI influences money supply?

    1. Interest rates
    2. Open market operations
    3. Reserve requirements
    4. Issuing the ₹1 note
    Show answer

    Answer: (D) Issuing the ₹1 note

    Explanation: RBI influences money supply through interest rates, open market operations and reserve requirements. Issuing the ₹1 note is the job of the Ministry of Finance. RBI is nevertheless the principal supplier of money and issues currency notes.

  2. Consider the following statements:1.The Ministry of Finance issues only the ₹1 note.2.The Ministry of Finance issues all coins.Which of the above is/are correct?

    1. 1 only
    2. Both 1 and 2
    3. 2 only
    4. Neither 1 nor 2
    Show answer

    Answer: (B) Both 1 and 2

    Explanation: Both statements are correct. The Ministry of Finance issues only the ₹1 note and all coins. RBI issues all other notes.

  3. How do commercial banks create money?

    1. By minting coins
    2. By lending and creating a new deposit in the borrower’s account
    3. By issuing the ₹1 note
    4. By declaring instruments legal tender
    Show answer

    Answer: (B) By lending and creating a new deposit in the borrower’s account

    Explanation: Commercial banks create money through lending because a loan creates a new deposit in the borrower’s account. That increases money supply. Minting, issuing the ₹1 note and declaring legal tender are not their stated roles.

  4. Money created by commercial banks in the form of demand deposits is called:

    1. Reserve money
    2. Plastic money
    3. Bank money
    4. Commodity money
    Show answer

    Answer: (C) Bank money

    Explanation: Money created by commercial banks as demand deposits is called bank money. Reserve money is M0, plastic money consists of cards, and commodity money has intrinsic value. Commercial-bank lending is the mechanism that creates bank money.

  5. Assertion (A): A reserve requirement can limit banks’ lending.Reason (R): It is the share of deposits that banks must keep.Select the correct answer.

    1. Both A and R are true and R is the correct explanation of A
    2. Both A and R are true but R is not the correct explanation of A
    3. A is true but R is false
    4. A is false but R is true
    Show answer

    Answer: (A) Both A and R are true and R is the correct explanation of A

    Explanation: Both A and R are true, and R correctly explains A. Reserve requirements specify the share of deposits banks must keep, thereby limiting lending. They are listed as a central-bank-policy determinant of money supply.

  6. If commercial banks lend more to businesses and individuals, what is the likely effect?

    1. Money supply decreases because deposits are destroyed
    2. Money supply increases because lending creates deposits
    3. Only M0 increases because coins are issued
    4. Money supply remains unchanged because lending is not a determinant
    Show answer

    Answer: (B) Money supply increases because lending creates deposits

    Explanation: Greater lending creates deposits and increases money supply. Commercial-bank lending practices are specifically listed as a determinant. M0 concerns currency and bank reserves, not coin issuance by commercial banks.

  7. Which public preference is identified as a determinant of money supply?

    1. Preference for holding cash versus interest-bearing deposits
    2. Preference for gold versus silver coins only
    3. Preference for legal tender versus fiduciary money only
    4. Preference for credit cards versus smart cards only
    Show answer

    Answer: (A) Preference for holding cash versus interest-bearing deposits

    Explanation: The public’s preference for holding cash versus interest-bearing deposits is a determinant of money supply. The other preferences are not listed as determinants. This preference affects how money is held within the economy.

  8. Velocity of money means:

    1. The speed at which money circulates
    2. The rate at which coins are minted
    3. The interest rate on time deposits
    4. The value of metal in a coin
    Show answer

    Answer: (A) The speed at which money circulates

    Explanation: Velocity of money is the speed at which money circulates. It appears under economic activity as a determinant of money supply. Coin minting, deposit interest and metal value are different concepts.

  9. Consider the following statements about government influence on money supply:1.Government spending and fiscal policy can affect it.2.This is especially so when spending is financed by borrowing.Which of the above is/are correct?

    1. 1 only
    2. 2 only
    3. Both 1 and 2
    4. Neither 1 nor 2
    Show answer

    Answer: (C) Both 1 and 2

    Explanation: Both statements are correct. Government spending and fiscal policy influence money supply, especially when financed by borrowing. This is listed separately from central-bank policy and commercial-bank actions.

  10. Which is an example of financial innovation as a determinant of money supply?

    1. New financial products and instruments
    2. Face value equal to metal value
    3. Currency plus demand deposits
    4. All notes and coins issued by government and the central bank
    Show answer

    Answer: (A) New financial products and instruments

    Explanation: Financial innovation refers to new financial products and instruments. Equal face and metal values describe full-bodied coins, while currency plus demand deposits defines M1. The final option describes legal tender money.

  11. Assertion (A): Open market operations can influence money supply.Reason (R): They involve buying or selling government securities.Select the correct answer.

    1. Both A and R are true and R is the correct explanation of A
    2. Both A and R are true but R is not the correct explanation of A
    3. A is true but R is false
    4. A is false but R is true
    Show answer

    Answer: (A) Both A and R are true and R is the correct explanation of A

    Explanation: Both A and R are true, and R correctly explains A. Open market operations means buying or selling government securities. They are a central-bank-policy factor influencing money supply.

  12. A bill of exchange is an order by the:

    1. Bank to its customer
    2. Central bank to a commercial bank
    3. Drawee to the drawer
    4. Drawer to the drawee
    Show answer

    Answer: (D) Drawer to the drawee

    Explanation: A bill of exchange is an order by the drawer to the drawee to pay a sum to the drawer or another party. A cheque is instead a customer’s order to a bank. Both are included under bank or credit money.

  13. Which pair is correctly matched?

    1. Near money — deposits and government bonds
    2. Paper money — credit cards and smart cards
    3. Standard money — large time deposits
    4. Commodity money — demand deposits
    Show answer

    Answer: (A) Near money — deposits and government bonds

    Explanation: Near money consists of assets easily converted into money, such as deposits and government bonds. Credit cards are plastic money, and the Rupee is standard money. Demand deposits are part of M1 and are not commodity money.

  14. Money made of gold or silver and existing as coins is called:

    1. Metallic money
    2. Paper money
    3. Plastic money
    4. Near money
    Show answer

    Answer: (A) Metallic money

    Explanation: Money made of metal such as gold or silver and existing as coins is metallic money. Paper money consists of notes, plastic money consists of cards, and near money comprises easily convertible assets. Metallic money includes full-bodied and token coins.

  15. Where do commercial banks hold their reserves?

    1. With the Ministry of Finance
    2. With individual borrowers
    3. With money market mutual funds
    4. With the central bank
    Show answer

    Answer: (D) With the central bank

    Explanation: Commercial banks hold reserves with the central bank. Such reserves are also a component of M0 together with currency in circulation. The Ministry of Finance, borrowers and mutual funds do not hold these commercial-bank reserves in the stated arrangement.

  16. Why is money supply described as a crucial economic indicator?

    1. It determines only the metal value of coins
    2. It classifies all forms of legal tender
    3. It affects inflation, interest rates and overall economic activity
    4. It is issued only by commercial banks
    Show answer

    Answer: (C) It affects inflation, interest rates and overall economic activity

    Explanation: Money supply is a crucial indicator because it affects inflation, interest rates and overall economic activity. It does not determine a coin’s metal value or merely classify legal tender. RBI is the principal supplier of money, while commercial banks create money through lending.

  17. Which of the following is given as an example of fiduciary money?

    1. Gold coin
    2. Government bond
    3. Banknote
    4. Smart card
    Show answer

    Answer: (C) Banknote

    Explanation: A banknote is listed as an example of fiduciary money, along with cheques and drafts. Gold coin is commodity or metallic money, government bonds are near money, and smart cards are plastic money. Fiduciary money rests on confidence and is not declared legal tender.
